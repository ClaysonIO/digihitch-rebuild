# Alberta (CA-AB)

**Status:** Legal. Neither the Traffic Safety Act nor the Use of Highway and Rules of the Road Regulation contains a rule against soliciting a ride. The ordinary pedestrian rules still apply: stay off the roadway where there's a sidewalk, and otherwise walk on the left edge or the shoulder facing traffic.

## Summary

I searched the current consolidations of the Traffic Safety Act and its Rules of the Road regulation for "solicit", "hitch" and "ride" and found no provincial hitchhiking rule. Hitchhiking is governed only by the general pedestrian rules in Part 3 of the regulation.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Where there's a sidewalk or path beside the road**, you must use it and must not "proceed along or remain on the roadway" (s. 90(1)).
- **Where there isn't one**, walk on the left side of the roadway or the shoulder, facing traffic (s. 90(2)).
- **Crossing:** don't stop or loiter while crossing, and don't step into the path of a close vehicle (s. 91).
- **Freeways:** I found no provincial statute or regulation that bans pedestrians from freeways. Posted traffic control devices and local bylaws may do so, so obey any "no pedestrians" signs.
- **Municipal and park rules can be stricter.** Check local bylaws in cities and towns, including the townsite of Jasper.

A violation of the regulation carries the fine set under the Provincial Offences Procedure Act (TSA s. 158).

## Laws

### Use of Highway and Rules of the Road Regulation s. 90: Pedestrians on roadway

**Source:** [Alberta King's Printer, Alta. Reg. 304/2002](https://kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Regs/2002_304.pdf) (office consolidation with amendments up to Alta. Reg. 167/2025, current as of August 8, 2025)

> 90(1) When a sidewalk or path is located beside a roadway, a pedestrian
> (a) shall at all times when it is reasonable and practicable to do so use the sidewalk or path, and
> (b) shall not proceed along or remain on the roadway.
>
> (2) If there is no sidewalk or path, a pedestrian who is proceeding along or on a highway shall at all times when reasonable and practicable to do so, proceed only on the left side of the roadway or the shoulder of the highway facing traffic approaching from the opposite direction.

**What it means:** In town, where there's a sidewalk, don't stand in the street to thumb. Use the sidewalk or a pull-out. On rural highways without a sidewalk, the shoulder is fine. No rule says which side of the road you must stand on while waiting for a ride, but walk facing traffic.

### Use of Highway and Rules of the Road Regulation s. 91: Pedestrians crossing roadway

**Source:** [Alberta King's Printer, Alta. Reg. 304/2002](https://kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Regs/2002_304.pdf) (consolidation current as of August 8, 2025)

> 91(1) A pedestrian who is crossing a roadway
> (a) shall cross as quickly as is reasonable, and
> (b) shall not stop or loiter while crossing the highway or otherwise impede the free movement of vehicles on the highway.
>
> (2) A pedestrian shall not proceed onto a roadway or proceed along a roadway into the path of any vehicle that is so close that it is impracticable for the driver of the vehicle to yield the right of way.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the lane or step out toward a car to flag it down.

### Traffic Safety Act s. 1(1): Definitions of "highway" and "roadway"

**Source:** [Alberta King's Printer, Traffic Safety Act, R.S.A. 2000, c. T-6](https://kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Acts/T06.pdf) (office consolidation current as of July 2, 2026)

> (p) "highway" means any thoroughfare, street, road, trail, avenue, parkway, driveway, viaduct, lane, alley, square, bridge, causeway, trestleway or other place or any part of any of them, whether publicly or privately owned, that the public is ordinarily entitled or permitted to use for the passage or parking of vehicles and includes
> (i) a sidewalk, including a boulevard adjacent to the sidewalk,
> (ii) if a ditch lies adjacent to and parallel with the roadway, the ditch, and
> (iii) if a highway right of way is contained between fences or between a fence and one side of the roadway, all the land between the fences, or all the land between the fence and the edge of the roadway, as the case may be,
> but does not include a place declared by regulation not to be a highway;
>
> [...]
>
> (nn) "roadway" means that part of a highway intended for use by vehicular traffic;

**What it means:** The "roadway" is the part used by vehicles. The "highway" takes in the sidewalk, the ditch and everything up to the fence line. Since Alberta has no ban on soliciting rides, these definitions matter mainly for s. 90: keep off the roadway where a sidewalk or path exists.

### Traffic Safety Act s. 111: Observance of Rules of the Road

**Source:** [Alberta King's Printer, Traffic Safety Act, R.S.A. 2000, c. T-6](https://kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Acts/T06.pdf) (consolidation current as of July 2, 2026)

> 111 A driver of a vehicle and any other person using a highway shall, insofar as applicable, obey the Rules of the Road, except when otherwise
> (a) instructed by any applicable traffic control device, or
> (b) directed by a peace officer.

**What it means:** The pedestrian rules bind you as "any other person using a highway". If a peace officer tells you to move, do it.

### Traffic Safety Act s. 158: Punishment

**Source:** [Alberta King's Printer, Traffic Safety Act, R.S.A. 2000, c. T-6](https://kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Acts/T06.pdf) (consolidation current as of July 2, 2026)

> 158(1) Except as otherwise provided in this Act, a person who is guilty of an offence under this Act for which a penalty is not otherwise provided is liable to a fine or other punishment as provided for under the Provincial Offences Procedure Act.

**What it means:** A pedestrian-rule ticket is a provincial offence fine, not a criminal matter.
