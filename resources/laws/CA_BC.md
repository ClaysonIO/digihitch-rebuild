# British Columbia (CA-BC)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not be on the **roadway** to solicit a ride (Motor Vehicle Act s. 182(3)). The roadway excludes the shoulder, so thumbing from the shoulder is lawful under this section. Pedestrians are banned outright on the "Schedule 1" freeways, such as parts of Highway 1, the Coquihalla and Highway 99.

## Summary

BC follows the common "not in the roadway" model. The Motor Vehicle Act defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicles, "but does not include the shoulder". So hitchhiking from the shoulder or curb is not an offence under s. 182, and soliciting a ride in an emergency is expressly exempt.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay off the roadway** while thumbing. Stand on the shoulder, or on the sidewalk where there is one.
- **Schedule 1 highways are off-limits to pedestrians** except at intersection crossings or when attending a disabled vehicle (B.C. Reg. 26/58 s. 19.07). The listed sections include Highway 1 from Horseshoe Bay to the Second Narrows Bridge and from Rupert Street to Hope, Coquihalla Highway 5, Highway 99 from 1st Avenue in Surrey to the Oak Street Bridge, Highway 97C, and the Inland Island Highway. On those, wait before the on-ramp.
- **Where there's no sidewalk**, walk on the extreme left side of the roadway or the shoulder, facing traffic.
- **Municipalities have their own bylaws.** Check local rules in cities.

The Act sets no specific penalty for s. 182. The general penalty in the Offence Act is a fine of up to $2,000 or up to 6 months in jail, or both. I found no fixed ticket fine for s. 182 in the fines schedule (see open questions).

## Laws

### Motor Vehicle Act s. 182: Pedestrians using highways

**Source:** [BC Laws, Motor Vehicle Act, R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 318, Part 3](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96318_05) (official consolidation, current to October 27, 2026)

> (1) If there is a sidewalk that is reasonably passable on either or both sides of a highway, a pedestrian must not use a roadway.
>
> (2) If there is no sidewalk, a pedestrian using a highway must use only the extreme left side of the roadway or the shoulder of the highway, facing traffic approaching from the opposite direction.
>
> (3) A person must not be on a roadway to solicit a ride, employment or business from an occupant of a vehicle.
>
> (4) Except for a person who solicits a ride in an emergency situation, a person who contravenes this section commits an offence.

**What it means:** It is an offence to be in the travel lanes while asking for a ride. Standing on the shoulder or the sidewalk is not covered by s. 182(3). Subsection (4) means a genuine emergency, such as a breakdown or an injury, is a defence to soliciting a ride.

### Motor Vehicle Act s. 119 and s. 1: Definitions of "roadway" and "highway"

**Source:** [BC Laws, Motor Vehicle Act, s. 119 (Part 3 definitions)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96318_05) and [s. 1 (general definitions)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96318_01) (official consolidation)

> **s. 119:** "roadway" means the portion of the highway that is improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, but does not include the shoulder, and if a highway includes 2 or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" refers to any one roadway separately and not to all of them collectively;
>
> **s. 1:** "highway" includes (a) every highway within the meaning of the Transportation Act, (b) every road, street, lane or right of way designed or intended for or used by the general public for the passage of vehicles, and (c) every private place or passageway to which the public, for the purpose of the parking or servicing of vehicles, has access or is invited, but does not include an industrial road;

**What it means:** The shoulder is expressly excluded from the "roadway", so the s. 182(3) ban stops at the white line. The "highway" is broader, but s. 182(3) doesn't use that word.

### Motor Vehicle Act Regulations s. 19.07: Schedule 1 highways restrictions

**Source:** [BC Laws, Motor Vehicle Act Regulations, B.C. Reg. 26/58, Division 19](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/26_58_06) (official consolidation)

> (1) Except as authorized by a permit issued by the Minister of Transportation and Infrastructure, and except for crossing a highway at an intersection, use of any highway named in Schedule 1 by the following is prohibited at all times:
> [...]
> (d) pedestrians, unless attending a disabled vehicle;
> [...]
>
> (2) Subsection (1) does not apply to pedestrians and to operators of pedal cycles and limited speed motorcycles using footpaths constructed adjacent to the travel portion of the highway or the shoulder on the travel portion of the highway where the minister causes signs to be erected designating the footpath or shoulder for such permitted use.

> **Schedule 1 — Highways**
> (1) Trans-Canada Highway #1 — from the ferry terminal at Horseshoe Bay to the north approach to the Ironworkers Memorial Second Narrows Bridge; from its intersection with Rupert Street to its junction with Route #3 in Hope; from its junction with the Coquihalla Highway (Afton Interchange) on the west approach to Kamloops to its junction with the Yellowhead Highway on the east approach to Kamloops.
> (2) Hope-Princeton Highway #3 — from its junction with the Trans-Canada Highway in Hope to its junction with the Coquihalla Highway, 7.7 km east.
> (3) Coquihalla Highway #5 — from its junction with the Hope-Princeton Highway, 7.7 km east of Hope to its junction with the Trans-Canada Highway (Afton Interchange) on the west approach to Kamloops.
> (4) Annacis Highway #91 — [...]
> (5) Annacis Highway #91A — from the Richmond Connector to the south approach to the Queensborough Bridge.
> (6) Vancouver-Blaine Highway #99 — from 1st Avenue in Surrey to the south approach of the Oak Street Bridge.
> (7) Okanagan Connector Highway #97C — from its junction with Highway 5A to its junction with Okanagan Highway 97, a total distance of approximately 84 kilometres.
> (8) The Inland Island Highway 19 from Craig's Crossing south of Parksville to its intersection with Highway 19A at Willow/Tamarac in Campbell River.
> (9) Highway 19 from its North Cedar Road intersection with Trans-Canada Highway 1, south of Nanaimo, to its intersection with Highway 19A north of Nanaimo.

**What it means:** On these highway sections you may not be on foot at all, not even on the shoulder, unless you are with a broken-down vehicle or crossing at an intersection. Hitchhike from the on-ramp approach on the local road, or from a rest area or gas station before the restricted section. Highways not listed in Schedule 1 are not covered by this ban. There, s. 182 and the shoulder rule apply, along with any posted signs.

### Offence Act s. 4: General penalty

**Source:** [BC Laws, Offence Act, R.S.B.C. 1996, c. 338](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96338_01) (official consolidation)

> Unless otherwise specifically provided in an enactment, a person who is convicted of an offence is liable to a fine of not more than $2 000 or to imprisonment for not more than 6 months, or to both.

**What it means:** This is the maximum for an offence with no specific penalty, which appears to include s. 182. It is a ceiling, not a typical fine.
