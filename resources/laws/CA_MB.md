# Manitoba (CA-MB)

**Status:** Legal. The current Highway Traffic Act contains no provision on soliciting rides or hitchhiking. The ordinary pedestrian rules apply: use the sidewalk where one is reasonably passable, otherwise keep to the left edge of the roadway or shoulder.

## Summary

A search of the full current consolidation of The Highway Traffic Act (current as of October 28, 2026) found no section on soliciting rides, hitchhiking, or soliciting from vehicle occupants. The Act has no statewide ban on pedestrians on limited-access highways either. Section 194 only governs where *drivers* enter and leave them. The province's transportation department says on its website that walking on provincial highways is legal, though not recommended.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Use the sidewalk** if there is a reasonably passable one (s. 143(1)).
- **No sidewalk:** walk as close as practicable to the left-hand edge of the roadway or shoulder, no more than two abreast (s. 143(2)).
- **Obey signs.** Every person must obey traffic control devices (s. 85). Traffic authorities (the minister for provincial highways, municipalities for local roads) can make rules on pedestrian traffic (s. 90(1)(e)). A posted "no pedestrians" sign is binding.
- **Use caution** entering or walking along any highway (s. 75).

Penalty: there is no specific hitchhiking offence. A breach of the pedestrian rules is an offence under the general penalty in s. 239(1), with a fine of up to $2,000.

## Laws

### The Highway Traffic Act s. 143: Use of sidewalks required; keeping left

**Source:** [Manitoba Laws, The Highway Traffic Act, C.C.S.M. c. H60](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/laws/statutes/ccsm/h060.php) (unofficial version, current as of October 28, 2026, in effect since June 1, 2026)

> 143(1) Where there is a sidewalk that is reasonably passable on either or both sides of a highway, a pedestrian shall not walk on a roadway.
>
> 143(2) Any pedestrian proceeding along a highway where no sidewalk is provided or where the sidewalk is not passable, shall walk as closely as is practicable to
> (a) the left-hand edge of the roadway or of the shoulder, as the case may be; or
> (b) any person who may be walking on the pedestrian's left side;
> but persons walking on a roadway shall not walk more than two abreast.

**What it means:** When you are walking, stay on the left facing traffic. The Act does not forbid standing still on a shoulder to thumb a ride.

### The Highway Traffic Act s. 1(1): Definitions of "highway" and "roadway"

**Source:** [Manitoba Laws, The Highway Traffic Act, s. 1(1)](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/laws/statutes/ccsm/h060.php)

> "highway" means any place or way, including any structure forming part thereof, which or any part of which the public is ordinarily entitled or permitted to use for the passage of vehicles, with or without fee or charge therefor, and includes all the space between the boundary lines thereof; but does not include any area designed or intended, and primarily used, for the parking of vehicles and the necessary passageways thereon;
>
> "roadway" means the portion of a highway that is improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, and includes that portion thereof that, but for the presence of a safety zone, would be ordinarily so used, but does not include the shoulder; [...]

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway. Standing on the shoulder keeps you out of vehicle traffic.

### The Highway Traffic Act ss. 75 and 85: Due care; obey traffic control devices

**Source:** [Manitoba Laws, The Highway Traffic Act](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/laws/statutes/ccsm/h060.php)

> 75 A pedestrian or the operator of a bicycle or power-assisted bicycle who is entering, crossing or proceeding along a highway shall, at all times, do so with due caution, care and attention, taking into account the traffic on the highway at the time.
>
> 85 Except when otherwise directed by a peace officer, every person shall obey the instructions or directions on, or conveyed by means of, an applicable traffic control device.

**What it means:** Use common sense near traffic. If a sign bans pedestrians on a stretch of road, it applies to you.

### The Highway Traffic Act s. 90(1): Traffic authority rules

**Source:** [Manitoba Laws, The Highway Traffic Act](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/laws/statutes/ccsm/h060.php)

> 90(1) A traffic authority may classify vehicles [...] and may make rules or by-laws supplementary to, or in addition to, but not contrary to, any provision of this Act, The Drivers and Vehicles Act or the regulations under either Act and applicable on highways over which the traffic authority has jurisdiction or within any area over which the traffic authority has jurisdiction, with respect to
> [...]
> (d) prescribing routes of travel, including prohibiting certain traffic from travelling on certain routes of travel or requiring certain traffic to travel only on certain routes of travel;
> (e) pedestrian traffic including pedestrian corridors;

**What it means:** The province (on provincial highways) and municipalities (on city streets) can add local pedestrian rules. "Traffic" includes pedestrians under s. 1(1). No such rule banning pedestrians or hitchhikers was found, but check signs and city by-laws, for example in Winnipeg.

### The Highway Traffic Act s. 239(1): General offences and penalties

**Source:** [Manitoba Laws, The Highway Traffic Act](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/laws/statutes/ccsm/h060.php)

> 239(1) A person who contravenes or fails to comply with or obey
> (a) a provision of this Act or the regulations;
> (b) a municipal by-law passed under the authority of this Act or the regulations; or
> (c) an order, direction or requirement of a peace officer, a traffic authority or another authority or person
> (i) given under the authority of this Act or the regulations, or
> (ii) indicated or conveyed by a traffic control device;
> is guilty of an offence and, except where another penalty is provided in this Act, is liable on summary conviction to a fine of not more than $2,000.

**What it means:** Breaking a pedestrian rule (such as walking on the roadway beside a usable sidewalk) is a provincial offence with a maximum $2,000 fine. Ticket amounts are usually far lower.
