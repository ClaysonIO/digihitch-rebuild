# Newfoundland and Labrador (CA-NL)

**Status:** Restricted. "A person shall not be on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride" (s. 128(3)). The Act's "roadway" excludes the shoulder *unless the shoulder is paved*. An unpaved shoulder or the verge is the lawful place to stand. No provincial statute bans pedestrians from controlled-access highways.

## Summary

Newfoundland and Labrador uses the Uniform Vehicle Code-style rule: you may not be on a roadway to solicit a ride, employment or business from a vehicle's occupant. As in New Brunswick and PEI, the definition of "roadway" excludes the shoulder only if the shoulder is unpaved. On stretches of the Trans-Canada with paved shoulders, standing on the shoulder to thumb a ride falls within the rule.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand off the roadway.** Use a gravel shoulder, the verge, a sidewalk or a pull-off. Do not stand on a paved shoulder or in a lane.
- **Use the sidewalk** if there is a reasonably passable one (s. 128(1)).
- **No sidewalk:** walk on the left side of the roadway or shoulder, facing traffic, no more than 2 abreast (s. 128(2)).
- **Obey traffic-control devices**, including any "no pedestrians" sign (s. 106(1)).

Penalty: a fine of $25 to $180 for soliciting rides on the roadway. In default of payment, the Schedule lists imprisonment of 1 to 6 days.

## Laws

### Highway Traffic Act s. 128: Pedestrians to use sidewalks; soliciting rides

**Source:** [House of Assembly of Newfoundland and Labrador, Highway Traffic Act, R.S.N.L. 1990, c. H-3](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/Legislation/sr/statutes/h03.htm) (consolidated statute; amendment list ends "2024 c17; 2024 cN-5.1 s59")

> 128. (1) Where there is a sidewalk that is reasonably passable on either or both sides of a highway, a pedestrian shall not walk on a roadway.
>
> (2) Where there is no sidewalk, a pedestrian walking along or upon a roadway or the shoulder of a highway shall, where practicable walk only on the left side of the roadway or the shoulder of the highway facing traffic approaching from the opposite direction and no more than 2 persons shall walk abreast on the roadway.
>
> (3) A person shall not be on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment or business from the occupant of a vehicle.

**What it means:** Thumbing from the roadway, including a paved shoulder, is an offence. Thumbing from beyond the pavement, or from an unpaved shoulder, is not covered.

### Highway Traffic Act s. 2: Definitions

**Source:** [House of Assembly of Newfoundland and Labrador, Highway Traffic Act, s. 2](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/Legislation/sr/statutes/h03.htm)

> (k) "controlled-access highway" means a highway
> (i) on to which persons have a right to enter from abutting land, and
> (ii) from which persons have a right to enter on to abutting land, only at fixed locations;
>
> (aa) "highway" means a place or way, including a structure forming part of the place or way, designed and intended for, or used by, the public for the passage of traffic or the parking of vehicles and includes all the space between the boundary lines of the place or way;
>
> (jjj) "roadway" means the portion of a highway that is improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, but does not include the shoulder unless the shoulder is paved, and where a highway includes 2 or more separate roadways the term "roadway" refers to any 1 roadway separately and not to all of the roadways collectively;

**What it means:** A paved shoulder counts as roadway. A gravel shoulder does not.

### Highway Traffic Act ss. 105 and 106: Controlled-access highways; traffic control devices

**Source:** [House of Assembly of Newfoundland and Labrador, Highway Traffic Act, ss. 105 to 106](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/Legislation/sr/statutes/h03.htm)

> 105. (1) Where on a controlled-access highway there is a sign indicating a location at which vehicles are permitted to enter, a driver shall not drive a vehicle onto the highway except at that location.
> (2) Where on a controlled-access highway there is a sign indicating a location at which vehicles are permitted to leave, a driver shall not drive a vehicle from the highway except at that location.
>
> 106. (1) Except when a traffic officer directs otherwise, drivers and pedestrians shall obey the instructions of a traffic-control signal or traffic-control device prescribed by the Minister of Transportation and Infrastructure in accordance with this Part.

**What it means:** The controlled-access rule in s. 105 is about where drivers enter and exit. No section of the Act bans pedestrians from those highways. If a sign prohibits pedestrians somewhere, s. 106(1) makes it binding on you.

### Highway Traffic Act s. 208 and Schedule: Penalties

**Source:** [House of Assembly of Newfoundland and Labrador, Highway Traffic Act, s. 208 and Schedule](https://www.assembly.nl.ca/Legislation/sr/statutes/h03.htm)

> 208. (1) Notwithstanding the Provincial Offences Act, unless otherwise provided by this Act or the regulations or regulations made under section 189, a person who has been convicted of an offence under this Act or the regulations or regulations made under section 189 is liable to a fine of not more than the maximum and not less than the minimum amount prescribed for the offence in the Schedule [...] and in default of payment of the fine or, where no fine is prescribed, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding the maximum and not less than the minimum period prescribed for that offence in that Schedule [...]
>
> Schedule, Penalties [fine Max. / Min.; default imprisonment Max. / Min.]
> 128(1) Walking on roadway when sidewalks provided | 180 | 25 | 6 days | 1 day
> 128(2) Walking on wrong side of highway | 180 | 25 | 6 days | 1 day
> 128(3) Standing on roadway to solicit rides, etc. | 180 | 25 | 6 days | 1 day

**What it means:** Soliciting a ride from the roadway carries a fine of $25 to $180. Jail time applies only if you don't pay.
