# Nova Scotia (CA-NS)

**Status:** Restricted. Under the new Traffic Safety Act (in effect October 19, 2026), "No person shall stand on a main travelled way for the purpose of soliciting a ride." The "main travelled way" excludes paved shoulders, parking areas and bike lanes, so standing on any shoulder to thumb a ride is not covered. A traffic authority can ban pedestrians from a highway by posting a sign.

## Summary

Nova Scotia replaced its old Motor Vehicle Act with the Traffic Safety Act (TSA), Chapter 20 of the Acts of 2025, effective October 19, 2026. The hitchhiking rule is now s. 208(3). It applies only to the "main travelled way", a term the Act defines to exclude "a paved shoulder, paved area provided for parking or bicycle lane". This is more hitchhiker-friendly than New Brunswick, PEI or Newfoundland, where a paved shoulder counts as roadway.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand on the shoulder, never in the travel lanes.** The shoulder (paved or unpaved) is outside the "main travelled way".
- **Don't approach cars on the roadway** to solicit people in them (s. 208(1)). Note the wider word "roadway" there, which *includes* paved shoulders. Stay put and let drivers come to you rather than walking up to stopped cars.
- **Obey "no pedestrians" signs.** Any traffic authority can ban pedestrians from a highway by posting a sign (s. 26). Ignoring the sign is an offence under s. 160. Check the ramps of 100-series highways for such signs.
- **When walking,** use the sidewalk, or if none, the shoulder on the left facing traffic (Using the Road Regulations s. 20(5)).

Penalty: soliciting a ride on the main travelled way (s. 208(3)) is a category A offence with a maximum fine of $100. Being on a highway against a sign or the regulations (s. 160) is category B, maximum $200.

## Laws

### Traffic Safety Act s. 208: Selling or soliciting on highway

**Source:** [Nova Scotia Legislature, Traffic Safety Act, Chapter 20 of the Acts of 2025](https://nslegislature.ca/sites/default/files/legc/statutes%20HTML/traffic%20safety.htm) (consolidated electronic version, "amended 2026, c. 3, Sch., ss. 36-39", page created October 19, 2026)

> 208 (1) No person shall stop, attempt to stop or approach a motor vehicle on a roadway for the purpose of offering, selling or providing any good or service to, or soliciting a person in, the motor vehicle.
>
> (2) Subsection (1) does not apply to
> (a) the offer, sale or provision of towing, vehicle removal or repair services or another commodity or service in an emergency situation; or
> (b) a fund-raising activity that is
> (i) permitted by a bylaw of the municipality in which the activity is being conducted, and
> (ii) approved by the traffic authority.
>
> (3) No person shall stand on a main travelled way for the purpose of soliciting a ride.

**What it means:** You may not stand in the lanes that cars drive in to ask for a ride. Standing on the shoulder is outside (3). Subsection (1) is broader: it bans stopping or approaching a car "on a roadway" to solicit the people in it. Don't walk up to cars stopped at lights or in traffic.

### Traffic Safety Act s. 2: Definitions

**Source:** [Nova Scotia Legislature, Traffic Safety Act, s. 2](https://nslegislature.ca/sites/default/files/legc/statutes%20HTML/traffic%20safety.htm)

> "main travelled way" means the part of a roadway that is improved and ordinarily used for vehicular travel and, for greater certainty, does not include a paved shoulder, paved area provided for parking or bicycle lane;
>
> "roadway" means
> (a) the part of a highway between the curbs or, in the absence of curbs, the part of a highway that is improved and ordinarily used by vehicles or bicycles, including any paved shoulder or paved area provided for parking and any bicycle lane; and
> (b) with respect to a highway that is a divided highway and includes two or more separate roadways, any one roadway separately and not all of the roadways collectively;
>
> "shoulder" means the paved or unpaved portion of a highway that is adjacent to, but does not form part of, the main travelled way;

**What it means:** There are two different terms. The "roadway" includes a paved shoulder, but the "main travelled way" does not. The hitchhiking rule in s. 208(3) uses the narrower "main travelled way", so a paved shoulder is fine for thumbing.

### Traffic Safety Act ss. 26, 155 and 160: Prohibiting pedestrians on a highway

**Source:** [Nova Scotia Legislature, Traffic Safety Act](https://nslegislature.ca/sites/default/files/legc/statutes%20HTML/traffic%20safety.htm)

> 26 A traffic authority with jurisdiction over a highway may prohibit the use of the highway by a type of traffic specified by the traffic authority, including pedestrians, individuals driving bicycles and individuals driving slow-moving vehicles or other conveyances, by placing an official traffic sign on the highway that indicates the type of traffic that is prohibited.
>
> 155 Every driver of a vehicle or other conveyance, and every pedestrian, travelling on or otherwise using a highway shall comply with Sections 156 to 214 and the regulations, except when directed to do otherwise by a traffic control device, traffic control person or peace officer.
>
> 160 No pedestrian shall walk on or use a highway or a portion of the highway contrary to the regulations or the direction of a traffic control device, a traffic control person or a peace officer.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians from every controlled-access highway. Instead, a highway is closed to pedestrians where an official sign says so. If you pass a "no pedestrians" sign at a ramp, you are committing an offence under s. 160.

### Using the Road Regulations s. 20(5): Where pedestrians travel

**Source:** [Nova Scotia Royal Gazette Part II, Using the Road Regulations, N.S. Reg. 230/2026 (Schedule "D" of O.I.C. 2026-346), PDF](https://novascotia.ca/just/regulations/regs/2026-230.pdf) (effective on and after October 19, 2026)

> 20 [...]
> (5) Unless it is impracticable, a pedestrian travelling on a highway must travel
> (a) if a sidewalk is present, on the sidewalk;
> (b) if no sidewalk is present, on the shoulder on the left side of the highway facing oncoming traffic; or
> (c) if no sidewalk is present and there is no shoulder on the left side of the highway, on the outside edge of the left side of the roadway facing oncoming traffic.

**What it means:** When walking between spots, use the sidewalk, or else the left shoulder facing traffic. The rule speaks of pedestrians "travelling". It is not clear that it stops you from standing still on the right-hand shoulder to face traffic heading your way.

### Offences, Penalties and Mandatory Suspension Periods Regulations, Schedule B

**Source:** [Nova Scotia Royal Gazette Part II, N.S. Reg. 223/2026 (Schedule "C" of O.I.C. 2026-343), PDF](https://novascotia.ca/just/regulations/regs/2026-223.pdf) (effective on and after October 19, 2026)

> Offence | Section | Offence Category | Maximum Fine
> Pedestrian walking on or using highway contrary to regulations | 160 | B | $200.00
> [...]
> Stopping, attempting to stop or approaching motor vehicle to offer, sell or provide commodity or service to or solicit person in motor vehicle | 208(1) | B | $200.00
> Soliciting ride while on main travelled way | 208(3) | A | $100.00
> [...]
> Pedestrian failing to walk on sidewalk | 20(5)(a) | A | $100.00
> Pedestrian failing to walk on shoulder on [...] | 20(5)(b) | A | $100.00

**What it means:** Thumbing from the travel lane is the lowest class of offence (category A), with a maximum fine of $100. Ignoring a "no pedestrians" sign is category B, with a maximum of $200. The "maximum fine" in these tables is the third-offence amount under the Summary Proceedings Act (Schedule B, s. 1(1)), so a first ticket is usually lower.

### Traffic Safety Act s. 317: General offence

**Source:** [Nova Scotia Legislature, Traffic Safety Act, s. 317](https://nslegislature.ca/sites/default/files/legc/statutes%20HTML/traffic%20safety.htm)

> 317 A person who contravenes a provision of this Act, the regulations or a bylaw made under this Act is guilty of an offence and liable on summary conviction to the penalties prescribed for that offence by the regulations.

## History

### Motor Vehicle Act replaced on October 19, 2026

**Source:** [Province of Nova Scotia, "Traffic Safety Act: legislative changes"](https://novascotia.ca/tsa); [Nova Scotia Legislature, Consolidated Public Statutes](https://nslegislature.ca/legislative-business/bills-statutes/consolidated-public-statutes)

The province states: "The Traffic Safety Act (TSA) passed in the Nova Scotia Legislature in October 2025 and will come into effect on 19 October 2026. [...] The Act will replace the Motor Vehicle Act (MVA), in place since the early 1900s." The Legislature's statute list is "Consolidated to October 19, 2026" and no longer lists the Motor Vehicle Act. The TSA regulations quoted above are each made "effective on and after October 19, 2026". An earlier Traffic Safety Act (S.N.S. 2018, c. 29) was passed but is listed as "not proclaimed in force". The TSA defines "former Act" as "Chapter 293 of the Revised Statutes, 1989, the Motor Vehicle Act".

**What it means:** Guides written before late October 2026 quote the old Motor Vehicle Act. Section numbers and wording have changed.
