# Nunavut (CA-NU)

**Status:** Legal. Nunavut's Traffic Safety Act, formerly the Motor Vehicles Act, bans pedestrians from being on the roadway to solicit **employment or business** (s. 257), but it says nothing about rides. Ordinary pedestrian rules apply.

## Summary

Nunavut inherited the Northwest Territories Motor Vehicles Act in 1999 and later renamed it the Traffic Safety Act. The pedestrian sections are the same as in the NWT: no hitchhiking rule, and a ban on soliciting "employment or business" from the roadway. The Act has no freeway or controlled-access highway provisions.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Walk on the sidewalk** where one is usable. Otherwise walk on the left-hand side of the roadway or the shoulder (s. 255).
- **Stay off the roadway** while soliciting anything. The shoulder is not part of the roadway.
- **Municipal by-laws** may regulate pedestrians (s. 347), so check local rules in Iqaluit and other communities.

Offences carry a maximum fine of $5,000 or up to six months in jail (s. 338).

## Laws

### Traffic Safety Act ss. 255 to 257: Pedestrians

**Source:** [Nunavut Legislation, Consolidation of Traffic Safety Act (PDF)](https://nunavutlegislation.ca/en/file-download/download/public/8180) (consolidation current to December 6, 2019. The site notes later amendments by S.Nu. 2020, c. 15; 2021, c. 10; 2021, c. 20; 2025, c. 15; and 2026, c. 7. I checked the texts of 2021, c. 10; 2025, c. 15, s. 50; and 2026, c. 7, s. 23, and none of them changes ss. 255 to 257 or s. 338.)

> 255. (1) A pedestrian shall not walk or run on a roadway if there is a sidewalk that is usable on either side of the roadway.
>
> (2) Where there is no sidewalk that is usable on either side of a roadway, a pedestrian shall, unless it is impracticable, walk or run on the left-hand side of the roadway or the shoulder of the highway.
>
> 256. (1) No person who is outside of a vehicle shall
> (a) hold onto a vehicle that is in motion, or
> (b) hold onto a vehicle that is stationary,
> for the purpose of being towed.
>
> (2) No driver shall knowingly tow a person that is holding onto the vehicle he or she is driving.
>
> 257. No pedestrian shall be on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting employment or business with an occupant of a vehicle using the roadway.

**What it means:** Asking for a free ride is not mentioned anywhere. Section 257 targets selling or job-seeking from the roadway. Stand on the shoulder or the roadside and you are clear of it.

### Traffic Safety Act s. 1: Definitions of "highway" and "roadway"

**Source:** [Nunavut Legislation, Consolidation of Traffic Safety Act (PDF)](https://nunavutlegislation.ca/en/file-download/download/public/8180) (current to December 6, 2019)

> "highway" means a road, place, bridge or structure, whether publicly or privately owned, that the public is ordinarily entitled or permitted to use for the passage of vehicles and includes
> (a) a privately or publicly owned area that is designed and primarily used for the parking of vehicles, other than the driveway of a private dwelling,
> (b) where a plan of survey or other instrument establishes a highway, the area between the boundary lines of the highway as shown on the plan of survey or instrument,
> (c) a sidewalk, pathway, ditch or shoulder adjacent to and on either side of the travelled portion of the road or place and the area between the sidewalk, pathway, ditch or shoulder and the travelled portion of the road or place, and
> (d) a road on a frozen body of water or water course or a road that can be used for only a portion of a year; (route)
>
> [...]
>
> "roadway" means the part of a highway that is improved, designed or ordinarily used for the passage of vehicles, but does not include the shoulder of a highway; (chaussée)

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway. Ice roads and seasonal trails open to vehicles count as highways.

### Traffic Safety Act ss. 336 and 338: Offence and general punishment

**Source:** [Nunavut Legislation, Consolidation of Traffic Safety Act (PDF)](https://nunavutlegislation.ca/en/file-download/download/public/8180) (current to December 6, 2019)

> 336. Every person who contravenes this Act or the regulations is guilty of an offence and liable on summary conviction to the punishment set out in this Act.
>
> [...]
>
> 338. Subject to section 337, every person who is found guilty of an offence under this Act or the regulations is liable to a fine not exceeding $5,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding six months or to both.

**What it means:** A breach of the pedestrian rules is a summary offence. The $5,000 figure is the maximum, not a typical fine.

## History

### Renamed from Motor Vehicles Act to Traffic Safety Act

**Source:** [Nunavut Legislation, current consolidated laws, "T"](https://nunavutlegislation.ca/en/consolidated-law/current?title=T)

The listing for the Traffic Safety Act consolidation states "Formerly: Motor Vehicles Act." An older consolidation (PDF file dated 2005), then titled the Motor Vehicles Act (Nunavut), already contained the same s. 257 wording on soliciting employment or business, with no mention of rides.

**What it means:** Older references to the Nunavut "Motor Vehicles Act" refer to this same law.
