# Ontario (CA-ON)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not solicit a ride while standing on the roadway. Ontario's definition of "roadway" excludes the shoulder, so thumbing from the shoulder or curb is not covered by s. 177(1). Pedestrians are banned by regulation from the 400-series highways, the QEW, Highway 407 and the other controlled-access highways listed in Reg. 627.

## Summary

Ontario has a specific hitchhiking rule. Highway Traffic Act (HTA) s. 177(1) says no person "while on the roadway" shall solicit a ride from a driver, except from a public passenger vehicle such as a bus or taxi. The HTA defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicles, and the definition expressly says it "does not include the shoulder".

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay off the roadway.** Stand on the shoulder, the curb or the sidewalk, never in a travel lane.
- **Stay off the controlled-access highways listed in R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 627.** These include all of Highways 400 (except one short stretch in Barrie), 401, 402, 403, 404, 405, 406, 407, 407 East, 409, 410, 412, 416, 417, 418 and 427, the QEW, and listed sections of other King's Highways. Hitchhike from the on-ramp approach on the local road, before the controlled-access section begins.
- **Walk facing traffic** on the left side when there is no sidewalk (s. 179).
- **Municipalities can ban pedestrians** on highways under their jurisdiction by by-law (s. 185(2)).

Penalty: s. 177(1) has no penalty of its own, so the general penalty in s. 214(1) applies: a fine of $60 to $1,000. Using a controlled-access highway contrary to Reg. 627 falls under the same general penalty.

## Laws

### Highway Traffic Act s. 177: Soliciting rides or business from drivers

**Source:** [Ontario e-Laws, Highway Traffic Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. H.8, s. 177](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h08) (consolidation period from July 1, 2026; last amendment 2026, c. 7, Sched. 5)

> 177 (1) No person, while on the roadway, shall solicit a ride from the driver of a motor vehicle other than a public passenger conveyance.
>
> (2) No person, while on the roadway, shall stop, attempt to stop or approach a motor vehicle for the purpose of offering, selling or providing any commodity or service to the driver or any other person in the motor vehicle.
>
> [...]
>
> (4) Every person who contravenes subsection (2) is guilty of an offence and is liable,
> (a) on a first conviction, to a fine of not more than $500; and
> (b) on each subsequent conviction, to a fine of not more than $1,000 or to imprisonment for a term of not more than six months, or to both.

**What it means:** Asking for a ride while you stand in the roadway is an offence. Asking from the shoulder or the sidewalk is not covered by this subsection. The tougher penalty in (4) applies only to selling or offering services to drivers under (2), not to soliciting a ride under (1).

### Highway Traffic Act s. 1(1): Definitions of "highway" and "roadway"

**Source:** [Ontario e-Laws, Highway Traffic Act, s. 1(1)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h08)

> "highway" includes a common and public highway, street, avenue, parkway, driveway, square, place, bridge, viaduct or trestle, any part of which is intended for or used by the general public for the passage of vehicles and includes the area between the lateral property lines thereof;
>
> "roadway" means the part of the highway that is improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, but does not include the shoulder, and, where a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" refers to any one roadway separately and not to all of the roadways collectively;

**What it means:** The shoulder is part of the "highway" but not part of the "roadway". Section 177(1) only applies on the roadway, so the shoulder is the legal place to stand, as long as the highway itself is not closed to pedestrians.

### Highway Traffic Act s. 179: Duties of pedestrian when walking along highway

**Source:** [Ontario e-Laws, Highway Traffic Act, s. 179](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h08)

> 179 (1) Where sidewalks are not provided on a highway, a pedestrian walking along the highway shall walk on the left side thereof facing oncoming traffic and, when walking along the roadway, shall walk as close to the left edge thereof as possible.

**What it means:** When you walk between spots and there is no sidewalk, walk on the left, facing oncoming cars.

### Highway Traffic Act s. 185: Regulating or prohibiting use of highway by pedestrians

**Source:** [Ontario e-Laws, Highway Traffic Act, s. 185](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h08)

> 185 (1) The Minister may make regulations prohibiting or regulating the use of any highway or part thereof by pedestrians or animals or any class or classes of vehicles.
>
> (2) The council of a municipality may by by-law prohibit pedestrians or the use of motor assisted bicycles, bicycles, wheelchairs or animals on any highway or portion of a highway under its jurisdiction.
>
> (3) Where a pedestrian is on a highway in contravention of a regulation made or by-law passed under this section, a police officer may require the pedestrian to accompany him or her to the nearest intersecting highway on which pedestrians are not prohibited and the pedestrian shall comply with the request.

**What it means:** The province bans pedestrians from specific highways by regulation (Reg. 627, below), and cities can ban them from their own roads by by-law. If you are on one of those roads, police can walk you to the nearest road where pedestrians are allowed.

### R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 627: Use of Controlled-Access Highways by Pedestrians

**Source:** [Ontario e-Laws, R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 627](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900627) (consolidation period from December 15, 2021; last amendment O. Reg. 827/21)

> 1. (1) Subject to subsection (2), pedestrians are prohibited from using those parts of the controlled-access highways described in Schedule 1.
>
> (2) Subsection (1) does not apply to pedestrians,
> (a) engaged in police, highway maintenance, highway construction or vehicle inspection duties;
> (b) within commuter parking lots established and maintained by the Ministry or proceeding directly between such lots and adjacent intersecting highways;
> (c) within truck inspection stations or pulp load check areas established and maintained by the Ministry;
> (d) making use of a controlled-access highway where the use is necessary because of an emergency; or
> (e) crossing at a traffic control signal or a crosswalk.
>
> [...]
>
> SCHEDULE 1
> 1. All of the King's Highways known as Nos. 401, 402, 403, 404, 405, 406, 409, 410, 412, 416, 417, 418 and 427 and the Queen Elizabeth Way.
> 1.1 All of the King's Highway known as Highway 407 East.
> 1.2 All of the private toll highway known as Highway 407.
> 2. All of the King's Highway known as No. 400, except for that part of King's Highway No. 400 known as Rose Street in the City of Barrie [...]
> [...]

**What it means:** No walking or standing on the 400-series highways, the QEW, the 407 or the other listed sections, including their shoulders. Schedule 1 also lists sections of Highways 3, 6, 7, 8, 11, 17, 26, 35, 58, 61, 69, 85, 115, 137 and others, so check the full schedule for your route. There is a useful exception in (2)(b): you may be inside a Ministry commuter parking lot (carpool lot) and walk directly between it and the crossroad. Carpool lots near interchanges are lawful places to look for rides.

### Highway Traffic Act s. 214: General penalty

**Source:** [Ontario e-Laws, Highway Traffic Act, s. 214](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h08)

> 214 (1) Every person who contravenes this Act or any regulation is guilty of an offence and on conviction, where a penalty for the contravention is not otherwise provided for herein, is liable to a fine of not less than $60 and not more than $1,000.

**What it means:** Soliciting a ride from the roadway, or walking on a highway listed in Reg. 627, can bring a fine of $60 to $1,000. It is a provincial offence, not a criminal one.

## Notable local rules

### Safe Streets Act, 1999, s. 3(2): Solicitation of captive audience

**Source:** [Ontario e-Laws, Safe Streets Act, 1999, S.O. 1999, c. 8](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/99s08) (consolidation period from December 15, 2005)

> "solicit" means to request, in person, the immediate provision of money or another thing of value, regardless of whether consideration is offered or provided in return, using the spoken, written or printed word, a gesture or other means.
>
> 3 (2) No person shall,
> [...]
> (e) solicit a person who is in the process of getting in, out of, on or off a vehicle or who is in a parking lot; or
> (f) while on a roadway, solicit a person who is in or on a stopped, standing or parked vehicle.

**What it means:** This province-wide Act is aimed at panhandling. It is not a local by-law, but it matters most in cities. It bans asking people for money "or another thing of value" in parking lots or at stopped cars. Whether a free ride counts as a "thing of value" has not been confirmed here (see below). Avoid going car to car in parking lots or at red lights. Fines are up to $500 for a first conviction (s. 5(1)).

## History

**Source:** amendment notes in the [current HTA s. 177](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h08)

The current wording of s. 177(1) and (2) was enacted by S.O. 1999, c. 8, s. 7(1), the [Safe Streets Act, 1999](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/99s08), in force January 31, 2000. The e-Laws consolidation lists later amendments to s. 177 in 2005 (charity fund-raising exception) and 2021 (towing cross-reference, in force January 1, 2024). None of these changed the rule on soliciting rides.

## Unverified / open questions

- The text of s. 177 as it read before the 1999 amendment was not retrieved.
