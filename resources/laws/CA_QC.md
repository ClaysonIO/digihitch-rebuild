# Quebec (CA-QC)

**Status:** Restricted. The SAAQ, Quebec's road-safety agency, states that under the Highway Safety Code pedestrians must not "hitchhike on the roadway or in an area where passing is prohibited". It also says they must not "walk on highways or access ramps" (autoroutes and their ramps). The statute text itself could not be retrieved for this page, so the exact articles and wording are unverified.

## Summary

Quebec's rules of the road are in the *Code de la sécurité routière* (official English title: *Highway Safety Code*, CQLR c. C-24.2). Both the official LégisQuébec site and CanLII refused connections from this research environment (HTTP 403), so no article of the Code is quoted here. The rules below come from the Société de l'assurance automobile du Québec (SAAQ). The SAAQ is the provincial government agency that administers the Code, and its summary is official guidance, not the law itself.

What applies to hitchhikers, per the SAAQ:

- **Don't hitchhike on the roadway.** Standing off the roadway (on the shoulder or sidewalk) is not covered by the rule the SAAQ describes.
- **Don't hitchhike where passing is prohibited**, for example where there is a solid centre line or a no-passing sign. This is a Quebec-specific rule. It applies even if you are off the roadway, so pick a spot where passing is allowed.
- **Don't walk on autoroutes or their access ramps.** Hitchhike from the local road before the ramp begins, or from a service area or park-and-ride lot.
- **Use the sidewalk.** Where there isn't one, walk along the side of the roadway or on the shoulder, against traffic, unless walking with traffic is safer.

Penalty (per the SAAQ): "Pedestrians who don't obey the rules laid out in the Highway Safety Code face a fine of $15 to $30."

## Laws

### SAAQ summary of the Highway Safety Code for pedestrians (official guidance, not statute text)

**Source:** [Société de l'assurance automobile du Québec, "As a pedestrian: What the law says"](https://saaq.gouv.qc.ca/en/road-safety/modes-transportation/as-pedestrian/what-the-law-says) (retrieved 2026-11-02)

> According to the Highway Safety Code, pedestrians must: use the sidewalk bordering the roadway. If there is no sidewalk, pedestrians can walk along the side of the roadway or on the shoulder, against traffic. However, they may walk with the flow of traffic if that option is safer (for example, if the road is better lit or the shoulder is wider) [...]
>
> According to the Highway Safety Code, pedestrians must not: [...] hitchhike on the roadway or in an area where passing is prohibited [...] walk on highways or access ramps
>
> [...]
>
> Pedestrians who don't obey the rules laid out in the Highway Safety Code face a fine of $15 to $30.

**What it means:** Hitchhiking is legal in Quebec if you stand off the roadway, at a place where passing is allowed, and not on an autoroute or its ramps. A shoulder on an ordinary two-lane highway, at a stretch with a broken centre line, fits the SAAQ's description of a lawful spot. The fine for breaking these pedestrian rules is small: $15 to $30, according to the SAAQ.
