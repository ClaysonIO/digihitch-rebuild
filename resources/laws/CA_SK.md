# Saskatchewan (CA-SK)

**Status:** Legal. The Traffic Safety Act contains no rule on soliciting rides or hitchhiking, and no provincial ban on pedestrians on controlled-access highways. When walking along the travelled part of a highway, keep close to the left edge.

## Summary

A full-text search of the consolidated Traffic Safety Act (amended through S.S. 2024, c. 4) found no section on soliciting rides, hitchhiking, or soliciting from vehicle occupants. The only controlled-access rule (s. 226) tells *drivers* where they may enter and leave. Pedestrian rules in the Act cover right of way at crosswalks and stepping into traffic (s. 223), plus the duty to walk near the left edge of the travelled portion (s. 223(5)).

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Walk near the left edge** of the part of the highway used by vehicles, facing traffic (s. 223(5)).
- **Don't step into the path of a vehicle** that is too close to yield (s. 223(3)).
- **Pick a spot where cars can pull fully off the road.** The Act does not forbid standing on a shoulder or at an on-ramp to ask for a ride.
- **Check city by-laws.** Regina, Saskatoon and other municipalities have their own traffic by-laws, which were not reviewed here.

Penalty: there is no hitchhiking offence. A breach of the pedestrian rules falls under the general penalty in s. 275, with a fine of up to $1,000 for an individual.

## Laws

### The Traffic Safety Act s. 223: Rules re pedestrians

**Source:** [Publications Saskatchewan, The Traffic Safety Act, S.S. 2004, c. T-18.1 (consolidated PDF)](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/12208/formats/18166/download) (unofficial consolidation, amended through S.S. 2024, c. 4)

> 223(1) A driver of a vehicle on a highway within a hamlet or any municipality other than a rural municipality or the prescribed part of a municipal district shall stop the vehicle and yield the right of way to the pedestrian if:
> (a) the driver of the vehicle approaches an intersection or clearly marked pedestrian crosswalk where a peace officer is not on duty and traffic lights are not in operation; and
> (b) the pedestrian is crossing the highway.
>
> [...]
>
> (3) No pedestrian shall leave a curb or other place of safety and proceed into the path of a vehicle on a highway that is so close that it is impracticable for the driver to yield the right of way.
>
> (4) Nothing in this section relieves the driver of a vehicle from the duty to exercise due care for the safety of pedestrians.
>
> (5) No person, other than a pedestrian in charge of an animal, shall walk along that portion of a highway used for vehicular traffic, except close to the edge on his or her left.

**What it means:** If you are walking on the road surface itself, stay close to the left edge, facing oncoming cars. Never step out in front of a car that can't stop. The section does not regulate standing on the shoulder.

### The Traffic Safety Act s. 2: Definitions of "highway" and "pedestrian"

**Source:** [Publications Saskatchewan, The Traffic Safety Act, s. 2](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/12208/formats/18166/download)

> (k) "highway" means a road, parkway, driveway, square or place designed and intended for or used by the general public for the passage of vehicles, but does not include any area, whether privately or publicly owned, that is primarily intended to be used for the parking of vehicles and the necessary passageways on that area;
>
> (aa) "pedestrian" means:
> (i) a person on foot; or
> (ii) a person in or on a mobility aid;

**What it means:** The Act does not define "roadway" or "shoulder". Section 223(5) instead speaks of "that portion of a highway used for vehicular traffic". A gravel or paved shoulder that is not used for traffic is arguably outside that phrase.

### The Traffic Safety Act s. 226: Rules re controlled access highways

**Source:** [Publications Saskatchewan, The Traffic Safety Act, s. 226](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/12208/formats/18166/download)

> 226 No driver of a vehicle shall enter or leave a controlled access highway except at a place where signs indicate that vehicles may do so.

**What it means:** This applies to drivers only. No provincial statute found here bans pedestrians from controlled-access highways. Stopping on a high-speed highway is still dangerous, so stand where a driver can pull over safely, such as an on-ramp approach, a service road or a gas station.

### The Traffic Safety Act s. 275: General offence and penalty

**Source:** [Publications Saskatchewan, The Traffic Safety Act, s. 275](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/12208/formats/18166/download)

> 275 Any person who contravenes any provision of this Act or the regulations for which no other penalty is specifically provided, or who disobeys an order of the board, is guilty of an offence and liable on summary conviction:
> (a) in the case of an individual, to a fine of not more than $1,000; and
> (b) in the case of a corporation, to a fine of not more than $2,000.

**What it means:** Walking the wrong way along the road, or stepping into traffic, can bring a fine of up to $1,000.

## Unverified / open questions

- The PDF consolidation lists amendments through S.S. 2024, c. 4. Any 2025 or 2026 amendments were not checked.
- Regulations under the Act and The Highways and Transportation Act, 1997 were not searched for pedestrian bans on particular highways (for example, sections of Highways 1, 11 or 16, or the Regina Bypass).
- Municipal traffic by-laws (Regina, Saskatoon, Prince Albert, Moose Jaw) were not checked. They may restrict pedestrians on expressways such as Circle Drive or the Ring Road.
