# Alaska (US-AK)

**Status:** Restricted. Alaska does not ban hitchhiking outright, but you may not solicit a ride "in a manner which distracts a driver's attention" (13 AAC 02.180). Walking on controlled-access highways is prohibited except in an emergency.

## Summary

Alaska's rules of the road are mostly in the Alaska Administrative Code (13 AAC 02), not the statutes. The hitchhiking rule is 13 AAC 02.180. Unlike the common "no standing in the roadway" model, it doesn't say where you may stand. It bans soliciting a ride in a way that distracts a driver.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Don't distract drivers.** Thumbing from a safe spot is not banned. Running at cars, waving in the lane, or anything that pulls a driver's attention off the road is.
- **No walking on controlled-access highways** except in an emergency (13 AAC 02.175(b)). Wait at the on-ramp entrance or a pull-out on an ordinary highway.
- **Use the shoulder.** Where there's no sidewalk, walk on the shoulder, away from the roadway. "Roadway" excludes the shoulder.
- **Don't loiter on the highway.** 13 AAC 02.175(e) bans sleeping or loitering "upon a highway", and "highway" covers the entire width between the boundary lines, shoulders included.
- **Don't interfere with traffic** or put yourself at unreasonable risk on a roadway (13 AAC 02.175(d)).

Breaking a traffic regulation is an infraction with a fine of up to $300, unless another law sets a different penalty (AS 28.90.010(c)).

## Laws

### 13 AAC 02.180: Pedestrians soliciting rides or business

**Source:** [Alaska Legislature, Alaska Administrative Code, 13 AAC 02.180](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/aac.asp#13.02.180) (official AAC database, retrieved 2026-11-02)

> No person may solicit a ride in a manner which distracts a driver's attention, nor may a pedestrian upon a highway solicit employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of a vehicle.

**What it means:** Hitchhiking itself is not banned. The offence is soliciting a ride in a way that distracts a driver. Stand well off the travel lane, somewhere drivers can see you early and pull over safely, and don't step toward moving cars. The second half of the rule bans asking drivers for work, business or donations from a highway. It does not mention rides.

### 13 AAC 02.175: Pedestrians on highways

**Source:** [Alaska Legislature, Alaska Administrative Code, 13 AAC 02.175](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/aac.asp#13.02.175) (official AAC database, retrieved 2026-11-02)

> (a) Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, a pedestrian may not walk upon an adjacent roadway except when crossing the roadway.
>
> (b) Where a sidewalk is not available, a pedestrian walking upon a highway shall walk on a shoulder as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway. Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, a pedestrian walking on a highway shall walk as near as practicable to the outside edge of the highway and, if walking along a two-way roadway, shall walk only on the left side of the roadway. No pedestrian may walk on a controlled-access highway except in an emergency.
>
> [...]
>
> (d) No pedestrian may be upon or along a roadway in such a manner as to create an unreasonable risk of danger to himself or interfere with the normal flow of traffic.
>
> (e) No pedestrian may sleep or loiter upon a highway or, without lawful permit, obstruct free passage upon a highway.

**What it means:** Walk on the sidewalk if there is one, otherwise on the shoulder, as far from traffic as you can. Never walk on a controlled-access highway (a road you can only enter at ramps or designated entrances) except in an emergency. Subsection (e) is the one to watch. An officer could treat standing around on a highway shoulder as "loitering", so look like you are actively getting a ride, and use pull-outs, gas stations or wide shoulders where cars can stop.

### 13 AAC 40.010: Definition of "controlled-access highway"

**Source:** [Alaska Legislature, Alaska Administrative Code, 13 AAC 40.010](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/aac.asp#13.40.010) (official AAC database, retrieved 2026-11-02)

> (9) "controlled-access highway" means every highway, street, or roadway where access to or from the highway is determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over the highway, street or roadway;

**What it means:** This covers freeways and expressways where you can only get on and off at ramps. On those, the walking ban in 13 AAC 02.175(b) applies.

### AS 28.90.990: Definitions of "highway" and "roadway"

**Source:** [Alaska Legislature, Alaska Statutes, AS 28.90.990](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#28.90.990) (official statutes database, retrieved 2026-11-02)

> (17) "highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every way that is publicly maintained when a part of it is open to the public for purposes of vehicular travel, including but not limited to every street and the Alaska state marine highway system but not vehicular ways or areas;
>
> [...]
>
> (27) "roadway" means that portion of a highway designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder, even though the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder is used by persons riding bicycles or other human powered vehicles; and in the event that a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term refers to each roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively;

**What it means:** The **roadway** is the travel lanes only. The shoulder is not part of it, so rules that refer to the "roadway" do not reach you on the shoulder. The **highway** is the whole public right-of-way, shoulders included. Rules that say "upon a highway" (loitering, the walking ban on controlled-access highways) apply on the shoulder too.

### AS 28.90.010: Penalties

**Source:** [Alaska Legislature, Alaska Statutes, AS 28.90.010](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#28.90.010) (official statutes database, retrieved 2026-11-02)

> (c) Unless otherwise specified by law a person convicted of a violation of a regulation adopted under this title, or a municipal ordinance regulating vehicles or traffic when the municipal ordinance does not correspond to a provision of this title, is guilty of an infraction and is punishable by a fine not to exceed $300.
>
> (d) An infraction, as provided for in (c) of this section, is not considered a criminal offense and may not result in imprisonment, [...]

**What it means:** Breaking 13 AAC 02.175 or 02.180 is an infraction, not a crime. The fine is capped at $300 and there is no jail.
