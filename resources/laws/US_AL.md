# Alabama (US-AL)

**Status:** Illegal along state-maintained roads; restricted elsewhere. The traffic code only bans standing *in the roadway* to solicit a ride. Since 2023, however, Alabama's loitering law has made it a crime to "loiter or remain" on any state-maintained road *or its right-of-way*. Since 2024 that also covers anywhere within 30 feet of a freeway interchange. In practice, that covers the shoulders and on-ramp areas where hitchhikers usually stand.

## Summary

Alabama has two overlapping rules.

1. **Traffic code, Ala. Code § 32-5A-216(a):** "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride." "Roadway" excludes the berm and shoulder. Taken alone, this would make shoulder hitchhiking lawful.
2. **Loitering, Ala. Code § 13A-11-9(a)(8):** A person commits loitering if they loiter or remain on a state-maintained public roadway, on the right-of-way of a state-maintained public roadway, or within 30 feet of any interchange involving a controlled-access or limited-access highway. The right-of-way includes shoulders and grass margins. Unlike the ride rule, this provision does not depend on your purpose.

The loitering provision was added by Act 2023-245 (2023 H.B. 24) and expanded to interchanges by Act 2024-326 (2024 S.B. 89). Its stated target is roadside panhandling and camping. Its text, though, covers anyone who "remains" in a state highway right-of-way. A hitchhiker standing on the shoulder of a U.S. or state route, or at an interstate on-ramp, is plainly within its words.

What this means for hitchhikers:

- **Interstate on-ramps are risky.** Anywhere within 30 feet of an interchange is covered by the loitering law.
- **State and U.S. highways:** Standing still on the shoulder or right-of-way is covered.
- **City and county roads** that are not state-maintained are covered only by § 32-5A-216(a). Stay off the travel lanes there.
- Before arresting someone under (a)(8), an officer *may* instruct the person to leave the right-of-way. Comply if asked.

Penalties: A first loitering offense is a "violation," punishable by a fine of up to $200 and up to 30 days in jail. A second or later offense in the same jurisdiction is a Class C misdemeanor, punishable by up to $500 and up to 3 months in jail. A first violation of § 32-5A-216 is a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $100 or up to 10 days in jail.

## Laws

### Ala. Code § 32-5A-216: Pedestrian soliciting rides or business or fishing

**Source:** [Alabama Legislature, ALISON, Code of Alabama § 32-5A-216](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=32-5A-216) (official; history: Acts 1980, No. 80-434; Acts 1981, No. 81-803)

> (a) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
>
> (b) No person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle, nor for the purpose of distributing any article, unless otherwise authorized by official permit of the governing body of the city or county having jurisdiction over the highway.
>
> (c) No person shall stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes to hitchhike. Taken alone, this section leaves the shoulder open. On state-maintained roads, though, the loitering law below applies on top of it.

### Ala. Code § 13A-11-9: Loitering

**Source:** [Alabama Legislature, ALISON, Code of Alabama § 13A-11-9](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=13A-11-9) (official; history: Acts 1977, No. 607; Act 2019-465; Act 2023-245; Act 2024-326)

> (a) A person commits the crime of loitering if he or she does any of the following:
> [...]
> (8) Loiters or remains on a public roadway maintained by the state, the right-of-way of a public roadway maintained by the state, or any area within 30 feet of any interchange involving a controlled-access or limited-access highway. An "interchange" is defined as a system of interconnecting roadways providing for traffic movement between two or more roadways that do not intersect at grade.
> [...]
> (e) Loitering is a violation. A second or subsequent violation of this section in the same jurisdiction is a Class C misdemeanor.
>
> (f)(1) Prior to making an arrest for a violation of subdivision (a)(8), a law enforcement officer may instruct any person in violation of subdivision (a)(8) to immediately and peaceably exit the public roadway maintained by the state or the right-of-way of the public roadway maintained by the state.

**What it means:** Standing on the shoulder or grass beside a state or U.S. highway, or near an interstate interchange, can be charged as loitering. You don't have to be in the travel lanes. The law does not mention hitchhiking and was aimed at panhandling, but its words are broad enough to reach hitchhikers. Safer spots are private property with the owner's permission, such as a gas station or truck stop, or city streets that are not state routes.

### Ala. Code § 32-1-1.1: Definitions ("roadway," "highway," "controlled-access highway")

**Source:** [Alabama Legislature, ALISON, Code of Alabama § 32-1-1.1](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=32-1-1.1) (official)

> (8) CONTROLLED-ACCESS HIGHWAY. Every highway, street, or roadway to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over the highway, street, or roadway.
>
> [...]
>
> (26) HIGHWAY. The entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel.
>
> [...]
>
> (59) ROADWAY. That portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term refers to each roadway separately but not to all roadways collectively.

**What it means:** Under the traffic code, the shoulder is not part of the "roadway."

### Ala. Code § 32-5A-92: Restrictions on use of controlled-access roadway

**Source:** [Alabama Legislature, ALISON, Code of Alabama § 32-5A-92](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=32-5A-92) (official)

> (a) The Department of Transportation by resolution or order entered in its minutes, and local authorities by ordinance, may regulate or prohibit the use of any controlled-access roadway (or highway) within their respective jurisdictions by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic.
>
> (b) The Department of Transportation or the local authority adopting any such prohibition shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the controlled-access highway on which such prohibitions are applicable and when in place no person shall disobey the restrictions stated on such devices.

**What it means:** There is no blanket statutory ban on pedestrians on Alabama freeways. ALDOT or a local authority can ban them by order, and the ban is enforceable where signs are posted. Obey "No Pedestrians" signs at ramps.

### Ala. Code § 32-5A-215: Pedestrians on roadways

**Source:** [Alabama Legislature, ALISON, Code of Alabama § 32-5A-215](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=32-5A-215) (official)

> (a) Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (b) Where a sidewalk is not available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk only on a shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (c) Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway, and if on a two-way roadway, shall walk only on the left side of the roadway.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one. If not, use the shoulder. If there's no shoulder either, walk on the left, facing traffic.

### Penalties: Ala. Code §§ 32-5A-8, 13A-5-7, 13A-5-12

**Source:** [Ala. Code § 32-5A-8](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=32-5A-8), [§ 13A-5-7](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=13A-5-7), [§ 13A-5-12](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=13A-5-12) (official)

> § 32-5A-8(b) Every person convicted of a misdemeanor for a violation of any of the provisions of this chapter for which another penalty is not provided, shall for a first conviction thereof be punished by a fine of not more than $100.00 or by imprisonment for not more than 10 days; [...]
>
> § 13A-5-7(b) Sentences for violations shall be for a definite term of imprisonment in the county jail, not to exceed 30 days.
>
> § 13A-5-12(a)(3) For a Class C misdemeanor, not more than $500; [...]
> (b) A sentence to pay a fine for a violation shall be for a definite amount, fixed by the court, not to exceed $200, [...]

**What it means:** Standing in the roadway to hitch carries up to $100 or 10 days for a first offense. A first loitering offense is a violation, with up to $200 or 30 days. Repeat loitering in the same jurisdiction is a Class C misdemeanor.

## History

### 2023 and 2024: loitering on state roads added

**Source:** [2023 H.B. 24, House substitute (J0BEFF-1)](https://www.legislature.state.al.us/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2023RS/J0BEFF-1.pdf); [2024 S.B. 89, House committee substitute (LLQEYYW-1)](https://www.legislature.state.al.us/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2024RS/LLQEYYW-1.pdf); codified history in [§ 13A-11-9](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=13A-11-9)

Act 2023-245 (2023 H.B. 24) created the "state-maintained roadway or right-of-way" loitering offense. One draft of H.B. 24 would also have struck § 32-5A-216(b), the employment and contribution rule. The current official text of § 32-5A-216 still contains (b), and its history lists no 2023 amendment, so that part was not enacted. Act 2024-326 (2024 S.B. 89, effective June 1, 2024) added the "within 30 feet of any interchange" language and moved the offense to subdivision (a)(8). A 2026 bill (H.B. 168, introduced) would amend other parts of § 13A-11-9 (mask exemptions) and leaves (a)(8) unchanged.
