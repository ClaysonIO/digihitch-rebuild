# Arkansas (US-AR)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in a roadway to solicit a ride from the driver of a private vehicle. Since 2023, pedestrians may not be anywhere within the property boundaries of a controlled-access highway.

## Summary

Ark. Code Ann. § 27-51-1205 reads: "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle." It follows the Uniform Vehicle Code pattern. Whether the shoulder counts as part of the "roadway" under Arkansas's own definition could not be confirmed in this research (see open questions). Standing on the grass or a pull-off beyond the shoulder is the safest choice.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **No freeways, including ramps and right-of-way.** Act 779 of 2023 created § 27-51-1206: "A pedestrian shall not be within the property boundaries of a controlled-access highway." The only exceptions are being directed by a police officer, construction personnel in a work zone, and people who leave a vehicle after a breakdown, crash or other emergency. The fine is up to $100. Hitchhike from the surface road before the interchange, or from a gas station or truck stop.

## Laws

### Ark. Code Ann. § 27-51-1205: Soliciting rides prohibited

**Source:** [FindLaw, Arkansas Code § 27-51-1205](https://codes.findlaw.com/ar/title-27-transportation/ar-code-sect-27-51-1205.html) (unofficial mirror, marked "Current as of March 28, 2024"). Arkansas's official code is published by LexisNexis and was not retrieved.

> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes to hitch. The rule only covers soliciting rides from *private* vehicles.

### Ark. Code Ann. § 27-51-1206: Pedestrians prohibited on controlled-access highway

**Source:** [Arkansas General Assembly, Act 779 of 2023 (SB 504)](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Acts/FTPDocument?path=%2FACTS%2F2023R%2FPublic%2F&file=779.pdf&ddBienniumSession=2023%2F2023R) (official act text, approved April 12, 2023). The codified text matches [FindLaw, § 27-51-1206](https://codes.findlaw.com/ar/title-27-transportation/ar-code-sect-27-51-1206.html) ("Current as of March 28, 2024"), except that FindLaw reads "defense to prosecution under this section" where the act reads "of this section."

> (a) As used in this section:
> (1) "Construction personnel" means an employee of the Arkansas Department of Transportation or a contractor of the State Highway Commission;
> (2) "Controlled-access highway" means a state highway or street that:
> (A) Is designed for an unhindered flow of traffic without intersections, traffic signals, or property access; and
> (B) Regulates the ingress and egress of a motor vehicle;
> [...]
> (4)(A) "Pedestrian" means a person afoot.
> (B) "Pedestrian" does not include a person who is the driver or passenger of a motor vehicle traveling on a controlled-access highway.
>
> (b) A pedestrian shall not be within the property boundaries of a controlled-access highway unless he or she is:
> (1) Directed by a police officer; or
> (2) Construction personnel in a highway work zone.
>
> (c) A person who violates this section, upon conviction shall pay a fine not to exceed one hundred dollars ($100).
>
> (d) It is a defense to prosecution of this section that the pedestrian was first within a motor vehicle upon the controlled-access highway and exited the motor vehicle due to a mechanical breakdown, a motor vehicle accident, or other emergency.

**What it means:** The ban covers the whole property of an interstate or other freeway, not just the lanes: shoulders, ramps, medians and the grass inside the fence line. If your ride drops you on the interstate, the defense in (d) only covers leaving a vehicle because of a breakdown, crash or other emergency. Ask to be let out at an exit, off the ramp.

## History

### 2023: Act 779 bans pedestrians from controlled-access highways

**Source:** [Act 779 of 2023 (SB 504)](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Acts/FTPDocument?path=%2FACTS%2F2023R%2FPublic%2F&file=779.pdf&ddBienniumSession=2023%2F2023R)

Act 779, "An Act to Prohibit Pedestrians on a Controlled Access Highway," added § 27-51-1206. Before 2023, no Arkansas statute was found that banned pedestrians on freeways. Older guides that say Arkansas has no interstate pedestrian ban describe the pre-2023 law.

## Unverified / open questions

- **Definition of "roadway":** Arkansas's statutory definition of "roadway" (and whether it excludes the shoulder) could not be retrieved; the section number was not confirmed. Do not assume the shoulder is outside the roadway until this is checked.
- **Penalty for § 27-51-1205:** No penalty provision specific to § 27-51-1205 was found. Ark. Code § 27-50-301 makes "any moving traffic law violation not enumerated in § 27-50-302" a "violation" punishable under § 5-4-201. Whether standing in a roadway counts as a "moving traffic law violation" was not confirmed, and the fine amount was not retrieved.
- **Text source:** § 27-51-1205 was read from FindLaw (current as of March 28, 2024) through a fetch tool, not from the official code. Amendments in 2025 and 2026 were not ruled out.
- Arkansas's loitering statute (Ark. Code § 5-71-213) was not checked for roadside provisions.
- Local ordinances (Little Rock, Fayetteville) were not checked.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** Don't stand in the roadway to ask a private driver for a ride. Whether the shoulder counts as roadway is unconfirmed, so stand beyond it. Never enter freeway property, ramps included.

> **Ark. Code Ann. § 27-51-1205**
> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle.

> **Ark. Code Ann. § 27-51-1206(b)**
> (b) A pedestrian shall not be within the property boundaries of a controlled-access highway unless he or she is:
> (1) Directed by a police officer; or
> (2) Construction personnel in a highway work zone.
