# Arizona (US-AZ)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride from a driver. The roadway excludes the berm and shoulder, so the statute does not reach hitchhiking from the shoulder. Pedestrians can be banned from controlled-access highways where signs are posted.

## Summary

Arizona has the classic Uniform Vehicle Code hitchhiking rule in A.R.S. § 28-796(C): "A person shall not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of a vehicle." Arizona's definition of "roadway" is "exclusive of the berm or shoulder". Standing on the shoulder, curb or sidewalk to hitchhike is not covered by this rule.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** A violation is a civil traffic violation (a fine, not a crime). The maximum civil penalty is generally $250 plus surcharges.
- **Freeways:** ADOT's director or a city or county may ban pedestrians from a controlled-access highway. Once the signs are up, you must obey them. Stand at the ramp entrance before any "pedestrians prohibited" sign.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** where there is one. Otherwise walk on the left side of the road or its shoulder, facing traffic.
- **Proposed median and ramp bans were not enacted.** Bills in 2024 and 2025 would have banned pedestrians from congregating or soliciting on medians and freeway ramps (see History).

## Laws

### A.R.S. § 28-796: Pedestrian on roadways

**Source:** [Arizona Legislature, A.R.S. § 28-796](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/00796.htm) (official site, current text)

> A. If sidewalks are provided, a pedestrian shall not walk along and on an adjacent roadway.
>
> B. If sidewalks are not provided, a pedestrian walking along and on a highway shall walk when practicable only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic that may approach from the opposite direction.
>
> C. A person shall not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of a vehicle.

**What it means:** Subsection C is the hitchhiking rule. It applies only to the "roadway", meaning the travel lanes. Standing on the shoulder or behind the curb is not banned by this section. Pick a spot with room for a car to pull fully off the road.

### A.R.S. § 28-601: Definitions (chapter 3, traffic and vehicle regulation)

**Source:** [Arizona Legislature, A.R.S. § 28-601](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/00601.htm)

> 2. "Controlled access highway" means a highway, street or roadway to or from which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access except at such points only and in the manner determined by the public authority that has jurisdiction over the highway, street or roadway.
> [...]
> 22. "Roadway" means that portion of a highway that is improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. If a highway includes two or more separate roadways, roadway refers to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway. The § 28-796(C) ban therefore stops at the white edge line.

### A.R.S. § 28-733: Restrictions on use of controlled access highway

**Source:** [Arizona Legislature, A.R.S. § 28-733](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/00733.htm)

> A. The director may, and local authorities by ordinance may, prohibit the use of any part of a controlled access highway under their respective jurisdictions by pedestrians, bicycles or other nonmotorized traffic or by any person operating a motor driven cycle.
>
> B. The director or the local authority adopting the prohibition prescribed in subsection A shall erect and maintain official signs on the applicable controlled access highway. When the signs are erected, a person shall not disobey the restrictions stated on the signs.

**What it means:** Pedestrian bans on Arizona freeways depend on signs. Where a sign prohibits pedestrians, walking or standing past it is a violation. Hitchhike from the surface street before the ramp, or from a gas station or truck stop.

### A.R.S. §§ 28-121 and 28-1598: Penalty

**Source:** [Arizona Legislature, A.R.S. § 28-121](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/00121.htm) and [A.R.S. § 28-1598](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/01598.htm)

> 28-121. B. A violation of or failure or refusal to do or perform an act or thing required by chapter 3, 5, 7 or 8 or chapter 9, article 4 of this title is a civil traffic violation unless the statute defining the violation provides for a different classification. [...]
>
> 28-1598. Except as otherwise provided, a civil penalty imposed pursuant to this article shall not exceed two hundred fifty dollars. In addition, the court shall levy surcharges pursuant to sections 12-116.01 and 12-116.02.

**What it means:** Sections 28-796 and 28-733 are in chapter 3 of Title 28, so a violation is a civil traffic violation. That means a fine of up to $250 plus state surcharges, not a criminal charge.

### A.R.S. § 13-2906: Obstructing a highway or other public thoroughfare

**Source:** [Arizona Legislature, A.R.S. § 13-2906](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/02906.htm)

> A. A person commits obstructing a highway or other public thoroughfare if the person, alone or with other persons, does any of the following:
> 1. Having no legal privilege to do so, recklessly interferes with the passage of any highway or public thoroughfare by creating an unreasonable inconvenience or hazard.
> 2. Intentionally activates a pedestrian signal on a highway or public thoroughfare if the person's reason for activating the signal is not to cross the highway or public thoroughfare but to do both of the following:
> (a) Stop the passage of traffic on the highway or public thoroughfare.
> (b) Solicit a driver for a donation or business.
> [...]
> B. Obstructing a highway or other public thoroughfare under: [...]
> 2. Subsection A, paragraph 1 of this section is a class 2 misdemeanor, except that a second or subsequent violation within a period of twenty-four months is a class 1 misdemeanor.
> 3. Subsection A, paragraph 2 of this section is a class 3 misdemeanor.

**What it means:** This is a criminal law, separate from the traffic code. Hitchhiking in a way that creates an unreasonable hazard or blocks traffic can be charged as a class 2 misdemeanor. Paragraph 2 names donations and business, not rides. Don't push the walk button to stop cars so you can ask them for a lift.

## History

### Vetoed and pending median and ramp bills (2024 to 2025)

**Source:** [2025 H.B. 2684 (introduced), "pedestrians; congregating; medians; unsafe locations"](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1r/bills/hb2684p.htm)

H.B. 2684 would have added a new § 28-798: "A pedestrian may not congregate or engage in solicitation if the pedestrian is any of the following: 1. on a painted or raised traffic island or median. 2. on an exit or entrance ramp or roadway of a controlled access highway. 3. in an unsafe location where there is not a sidewalk or a safe corridor for pedestrians." Its penalties were a warning for a first offense, a civil traffic violation for a second, and a class 1 misdemeanor after that. As of 2026-11-02, A.R.S. § 28-798 does not exist on the Legislature's site (the URL returns "page not found"), so the bill is not codified law. A similar 2024 bill, H.B. 2658, passed both chambers and was vetoed, according to the Legislature's House bill summary marked "Vetoed".

**What it means:** Arizona has not banned standing on freeway on-ramps as such. Check for new law before relying on this, because the idea keeps coming back.
