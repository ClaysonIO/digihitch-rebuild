# California (US-CA)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in the roadway to solicit a ride. The shoulder and sidewalk are not part of the "roadway", so hitchhiking from the curb or shoulder is not banned by state law. Pedestrians are kept off freeways and expressways wherever Caltrans or a local authority has posted signs prohibiting them.

## Summary

California's hitchhiking rule is one sentence long and has not changed since the Vehicle Code was enacted in 1959: "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle." The Vehicle Code defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicle travel, so the rule targets standing in the travel lanes.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Stand on the shoulder, curb or sidewalk and let drivers pull over to you.
- **Freeways and expressways:** Caltrans and local authorities can prohibit pedestrians by order, and the ban takes effect once signs are posted. Check the ramp entrance for a prohibition sign and stand before it.
- **Walking along a road outside towns:** walk close to the left edge, facing traffic.
- **Penalty:** a pedestrian infraction, with a fine of up to $50.

## Laws

### Cal. Veh. Code § 21957: Hitchhiking

**Source:** [California Legislative Information, Vehicle Code § 21957](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=21957) (text retrieved from the Legislative Counsel's official bulk database, pubinfo_2025, file dated 2026-11-01; enacted by Stats. 1959, Ch. 3)

> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle.

**What it means:** Thumbing a ride is not banned in California. Standing in the travel lanes to do it is. Stand on the shoulder or curb, where a driver can pull out of traffic to pick you up.

### Cal. Veh. Code § 530: "Roadway" defined

**Source:** [California Legislative Information, Vehicle Code § 530](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=530) (text retrieved from the Legislative Counsel's official bulk database, pubinfo_2025; enacted by Stats. 1959, Ch. 3)

> A "roadway" is that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel.

**What it means:** The roadway is the part cars drive on. California's definition doesn't mention the shoulder, unlike states that explicitly exclude the shoulder. A paved shoulder isn't "ordinarily used for vehicular travel", though, so the usual reading is that the shoulder isn't roadway. Stay well to the outside edge.

### Cal. Veh. Code § 21960: Freeways and expressways

**Source:** [California Legislative Information, Vehicle Code § 21960](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=21960) (text retrieved from the Legislative Counsel's official bulk database, pubinfo_2025; amended by Stats. 2015, Ch. 777, Sec. 4, AB 604)

> (a) The Department of Transportation and local authorities, by order, ordinance, or resolution, with respect to freeways, expressways, or designated portions thereof under their respective jurisdictions, to which vehicle access is completely or partially controlled, may prohibit or restrict the use of the freeways, expressways, or any portion thereof by pedestrians, bicycles or other nonmotorized traffic [...]. Notwithstanding any order, ordinance, or resolution to the contrary, the driver or passengers of a disabled vehicle stopped on a freeway or expressway may walk to the nearest exit, in either direction, on that side of the freeway or expressway upon which the vehicle is disabled, from which telephone or motor vehicle repair services are available.
>
> (b) The prohibitory regulation authorized by subdivision (a) shall be effective when appropriate signs giving notice thereof are erected upon any freeway or expressway and the approaches thereto. [...]

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians on every freeway. Caltrans or the local authority does it road by road, and a ban takes effect once its signs are posted on the freeway and its approaches. If a ramp is signed "pedestrians prohibited", wait before the sign. If your car breaks down, you may walk to the nearest exit.

### Cal. Veh. Code § 21956: Walking along a roadway

**Source:** [California Legislative Information, Vehicle Code § 21956](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=21956) (text retrieved from the Legislative Counsel's official bulk database, pubinfo_2025; amended by Stats. 2022, Ch. 957, Sec. 12, AB 2147)

> (a) A pedestrian shall not walk upon a roadway outside of a business or residence district otherwise than close to the pedestrian's left-hand edge of the roadway.
>
> (b) A pedestrian may walk close to their right-hand edge of the roadway if a crosswalk or other means of safely crossing the roadway is not available or if existing traffic or other conditions would compromise the safety of a pedestrian attempting to cross the road.
>
> (c) (1) A peace officer [...] shall not stop a pedestrian for a violation of this section unless a reasonably careful person would realize there is an immediate danger of a collision with a moving vehicle or other device moving exclusively by human power.

**What it means:** Out of town, if you have to walk on the road itself, keep to the left edge, facing oncoming traffic. Since 2023, police may stop you for this only if there is an immediate danger of a collision.

### Cal. Veh. Code §§ 40000.1 and 42001: Penalty

**Source:** [California Legislative Information, Vehicle Code § 40000.1](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=40000.1) and [§ 42001](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=42001) (text retrieved from the Legislative Counsel's official bulk database, pubinfo_2025; § 42001 amended by Stats. 2006, Ch. 900, Sec. 3)

§ 40000.1:

> Except as otherwise provided in this article, it is unlawful and constitutes an infraction for any person to violate, or fail to comply with any provision of this code, or any local ordinance adopted pursuant to this code.

§ 42001:

> (b) A pedestrian convicted of an infraction for a violation of this code or any local ordinance adopted pursuant to this code shall be punished by a fine not exceeding fifty dollars ($50).

**What it means:** Standing in the roadway to hitch, or walking on a posted freeway, is an infraction. It is not a crime. The fine set by § 42001(b) for a pedestrian is up to $50. Other court fees and assessments may apply; they are not covered here.

## History

### 2022 AB 2147 ("Freedom to Walk Act")

**Source:** History notes on [Vehicle Code § 21956](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=21956) and § 21955, § 21961 and § 21966 in the Legislative Counsel's database ("Amended by Stats. 2022, Ch. 957 ... (AB 2147) Effective January 1, 2023")

AB 2147 did not touch the hitchhiking section (§ 21957, unchanged since 1959). It did limit when officers may stop pedestrians for walking-in-the-road and jaywalking offenses, which is why § 21956(c) now requires an immediate danger of collision before a stop.
