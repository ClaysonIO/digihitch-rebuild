# Colorado (US-CO)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in the roadway (the part used by moving traffic) to solicit a ride. On interstates you may solicit a ride only at an entrance or exit, or at places the Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) designates. The statute itself refers to "an individual lawfully soliciting a ride", and it tells drivers to pick up pedestrians only where there is room to pull off safely.

## Summary

Colorado's C.R.S. § 42-4-805 accommodates hitchhikers more than most states' laws do:

- **(1)(a)** lets "an individual lawfully soliciting a ride" stand on either side of a two-way road where there's a view of traffic from both directions. The usual left-side rule doesn't apply to them.
- **(2)** bans standing *in the roadway* to solicit a ride from a private vehicle. For this subsection, roadway means "that portion of the road normally used by moving motor vehicle traffic", so the shoulder is outside it.
- **(6)** bans soliciting a ride *on an interstate*, **except at an entrance to or exit from** the interstate or at CDOT-designated places. A driver or passenger of a disabled vehicle may solicit a ride anywhere.
- **(7)** says pedestrians shall be picked up only where vehicles have room to pull off without endangering or impeding traffic.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand on the shoulder, not in the lane.** Pick a spot where a car can pull fully off the road.
- **Interstates:** hitch at the on-ramp (an "entrance"), not along the interstate itself.
- **Penalty:** a class B traffic infraction. This is a civil matter, not a crime. The scheduled penalty for § 42-4-805 is $15 plus a $6 surcharge.

## Laws

### C.R.S. § 42-4-805: Pedestrians walking or traveling in a wheelchair on highways

**Source:** [Colorado Office of Legislative Legal Services, 2026 C.R.S. Title 42](https://olls.info/crs/crs2026-title-42.htm) (current through the 2026 Regular Session; subsection (1) last amended by SB 25-149, effective August 6, 2025)

> (1) Pedestrians walking or traveling in a wheelchair along and upon highways where sidewalks are not provided shall walk or travel only on a road shoulder as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway. Where neither a sidewalk nor road shoulder is available, any pedestrian walking or traveling in a wheelchair along and upon a highway shall walk or travel as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway and, in the case of a two-way roadway, shall walk or travel only on the left side of the roadway facing traffic that may approach from the opposite direction; except that:
> (a) An individual lawfully soliciting a ride may stand on either side of a two-way roadway where there is a view of traffic approaching from both directions; [...]

**What it means:** Walk on the shoulder. When you stop to hitch, you may stand on whichever side of the road your traffic is on, as long as drivers coming both ways can see you.

> (2) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle. For the purposes of this subsection (2), "roadway" means that portion of the road normally used by moving motor vehicle traffic.

**What it means:** Stay out of the travel lanes. The section defines its own "roadway" as the part used by moving traffic, so the shoulder doesn't count.

> (6) No person shall solicit a ride on any highway included in the interstate system, as defined in section 43-2-101 (2), C.R.S., except at an entrance to or exit from such highway or at places specifically designated by the department of transportation; or, in an emergency affecting a vehicle or its operation, a driver or passenger of a disabled vehicle may solicit a ride on any highway.
>
> (7) Pedestrians shall only be picked up where there is adequate road space for vehicles to pull off and not endanger and impede the flow of traffic.

**What it means:** On interstates, hitch from the on-ramp or off-ramp, not from the interstate shoulder. Subsection (7) is aimed at drivers. A driver who stops in a lane to pick you up is breaking it, so choose spots with room to pull over.

> (5) Any city or town may, by ordinance, regulate the use by pedestrians of streets and highways under its jurisdiction to the extent authorized under subsection (6) of this section and sections 42-4-110 and 42-4-111, but no ordinance regulating such use of streets and highways in a manner differing from this section shall be effective until official signs or devices giving notice thereof have been placed as required by section 42-4-111 (2).
>
> (9) Any person who violates any provision of this section commits a class B traffic infraction.

**What it means:** Cities and towns can add pedestrian rules, but a rule that differs from the state law takes effect only once it is signed.

### C.R.S. § 42-1-102(85): "Roadway" defined

**Source:** [Colorado Office of Legislative Legal Services, 2026 C.R.S. Title 42](https://olls.info/crs/crs2026-title-42.htm)

> (85) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder even though such sidewalk, berm, or shoulder is used by persons riding bicycles or other human-powered vehicles [...].

**What it means:** The general definition agrees with § 42-4-805(2): the shoulder isn't roadway.

### C.R.S. § 42-4-1010(3): Restrictions on controlled-access highways

**Source:** [Colorado Office of Legislative Legal Services, 2026 C.R.S. Title 42](https://olls.info/crs/crs2026-title-42.htm)

> (3) The department of transportation may by resolution or order entered in its minutes and local authorities may by ordinance consistent with the provisions of section 43-2-135 (1)(g), C.R.S., with respect to any controlled-access highway under their respective jurisdictions, prohibit the use of any such highway by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic. The department of transportation or the local authority adopting such prohibitory regulations shall install official traffic control devices [...] at entrance points or along the highway on which such regulations are applicable. When such devices are so in place, giving notice thereof, no person shall disobey the restrictions made known by such devices. This subsection (3) shall not be construed to give the department authority to regulate pedestrian use of highways in a manner contrary to the provisions of section 42-4-805.

**What it means:** CDOT can post signs closing a controlled-access highway to a class of traffic, and you must obey them. The statute adds that CDOT may not regulate pedestrians in a way that contradicts § 42-4-805, which expressly allows soliciting rides at interstate entrances and exits.

### C.R.S. § 42-4-1701: Penalty

**Source:** [Colorado Office of Legislative Legal Services, 2026 C.R.S. Title 42](https://olls.info/crs/crs2026-title-42.htm)

> (1) It is a traffic infraction for any person to violate any of the provisions of articles 1 to 3 of this title 42 and parts 1 to 3 and 5 to 19 of this article 4 unless such violation is [...] declared to be a felony, misdemeanor, petty offense, civil infraction, or misdemeanor traffic offense. Such a traffic infraction constitutes a civil matter.

The penalty and surcharge schedule in § 42-4-1701(4)(a)(I), under "Pedestrian violations", lists:

> 42-4-805 | $15.00 | $6.00

**What it means:** A ticket under § 42-4-805 is a civil traffic infraction with a $15 penalty and a $6 surcharge. No points are assessed for class B infractions (§ 42-4-1701(3)(c)).

## History

### 2025 SB 25-149: subsection (1) restructured

**Source:** [2024 C.R.S. Title 42](https://olls.info/crs/crs2024-title-42.htm) compared with [2026 C.R.S. Title 42](https://olls.info/crs/crs2026-title-42.htm) (official OLLS publications)

The 2024 text of § 42-4-805(1) ended: "except that any person lawfully soliciting a ride may stand on either side of such two-way roadway where there is a view of traffic approaching from both directions." SB 25-149 (effective August 6, 2025) moved this into a lettered paragraph (1)(a), "An individual lawfully soliciting a ride may stand on either side of a two-way roadway [...]", and added (1)(b) for horse riders. The hitchhiking rules in subsections (2), (6) and (7) did not change.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may solicit a ride from the shoulder, but not in the lanes of moving traffic. On interstates, solicit only at an entrance or exit (or CDOT-designated places), where cars can pull off safely.

> (a) An individual lawfully soliciting a ride may stand on either side of a two-way roadway where there is a view of traffic approaching from both directions; [...]
