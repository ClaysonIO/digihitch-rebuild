# Connecticut (US-CT)

**Status:** Restricted. Soliciting a ride from the traveled portion of any public highway is an infraction. The statute expressly allows it from the shoulder of any public highway except a limited-access highway. On limited-access highways and parkways, state regulations ban hitchhiking and pedestrians across the entire right-of-way, and they also ban picking up hitchhikers.

## Summary

Connecticut's statute, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 53-181, is unusually clear for hitchhikers:

- Soliciting a ride "while on or within the limits of the traveled portion of any public highway" is an infraction with a $35 fine.
- But "any person may, while on or within the shoulder portion of any public highway, except a limited access highway, solicit such a ride."

So on ordinary roads, hitchhiking from the shoulder is expressly lawful.

Limited-access highways (the interstates, the Merritt and Wilbur Cross Parkways and other designated routes) are governed by Office of the State Traffic Administration regulations, which apply to the whole "highway right-of-way":

- Pedestrians are prohibited except in emergencies or on facilities provided for them (§ 14-298-238).
- "Hitchhiking, soliciting of rides and loitering are prohibited within the highway right of way", and "the picking up of hitchhikers within the highway right of way is prohibited" (§ 14-298-243).

Section 14-300e of the General Statutes confirms that the pedestrian-rights statutes do not override § 53-181 or any regulation barring pedestrians from a highway.

In practice: hitchhike from the shoulder of the local road or the approach to an interstate on-ramp. Do not stand on the ramp itself or anywhere inside the limited-access right-of-way.

## Laws

### Conn. Gen. Stat. § 53-181: Soliciting rides in motor vehicles

**Source:** [LawServer mirror of Conn. Gen. Stat. § 53-181](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/connecticut/ct-laws/connecticut_statutes_53-181) (unofficial mirror, labelled "Current as of: 2024"). The official site, [cga.ct.gov, Chapter 943](https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_943.htm), could not be retrieved in this session because of TLS errors and HTTP 503.

> Any person who, while on or within the limits of the traveled portion of any public highway, solicits a ride in a motor vehicle, other than a public service motor bus or taxicab, except in case of accident or emergency, shall be deemed to have committed an infraction and be fined thirty-five dollars, except that any person may, while on or within the shoulder portion of any public highway, except a limited access highway, solicit such a ride.

**What it means:** Shoulder: allowed. Travel lanes: a $35 infraction. Limited-access highways: the shoulder exception does not apply, and the regulations below ban hitchhiking there entirely.

### Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-1: Definitions ("Limited access highway", "Shoulder")

**Source:** [LawServer mirror of Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-1](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/connecticut/ct-laws/connecticut_statutes_14-1) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (50) "Limited access highway" means a state highway so designated under the provisions of section 13b-27 [...]
>
> (90) "Shoulder" means that portion of a highway immediately adjacent and contiguous to the travel lanes or main traveled portion of the roadway;

**What it means:** The shoulder is the strip right next to the travel lanes, and that is where § 53-181 lets you stand. Limited-access highways are designated by the DOT Commissioner. They include the interstates and the parkways.

### Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-243: Hitchhiking

**Source:** [Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-243, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/connecticut/Regs-Conn-State-Agencies-SS-14-298-243) (unofficial mirror; effective June 3, 1976). The rule is part of "Use of Limited Access State Highways", §§ 14-298-235 to 14-298-260k.

> (a) Hitchhiking, soliciting of rides and loitering are prohibited within the highway right of way.
> (b) The picking up of hitchhikers within the highway right of way is prohibited.

**What it means:** On interstates, parkways and other limited-access state highways, hitchhiking is banned everywhere inside the right-of-way, including ramps and shoulders. Drivers may not pick you up there either.

### Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-238: Restricted use of limited access state highways

**Source:** [Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-238, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/connecticut/Regs-Conn-State-Agencies-SS-14-298-238) (unofficial mirror; effective June 3, 1976)

> The following are prohibited from entry upon and use of the highway right of way of the parkways and other limited access state highways; (a) Pedestrians, except during emergencies or except on facilities specifically provided therefore; [...]

**What it means:** Walking on an interstate or parkway, or on its ramps, is prohibited unless you are in an emergency.

### Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-236(5): "Highway right-of-way"

**Source:** [Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-236, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/connecticut/Regs-Conn-State-Agencies-SS-14-298-236) (unofficial mirror; amended December 27, 2017)

> (5) "Highway right-of-way" means the entire area, including highways, bridges, buildings, structures and lands encompassed by the lateral property lines purchased and owned by the state for the construction and maintenance of highways, bridges and access to service facilities. Where "highway" is used herein, it shall be interpreted as meaning or including the highway right-of-way;

**What it means:** The ban reaches everything the state owns for the highway, including state-owned service areas. Stay on local roads or private property.

### Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-242: Picking up or discharging passengers

**Source:** [Regs. Conn. State Agencies § 14-298-242, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/connecticut/Regs-Conn-State-Agencies-SS-14-298-242) (unofficial mirror; effective June 3, 1976)

> Picking up or discharging passengers within the highway right-of-way is prohibited except in areas designed for such purposes.

**What it means:** Drivers may not stop on a limited-access highway or its ramps to let you in or out, except in designated areas.

### Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-300e: Application of pedestrian rights to solicitation of rides and walking on limited access highways

**Source:** [LawServer mirror of Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-300e](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/connecticut/ct-laws/connecticut_statutes_14-300e) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> Nothing contained in sections 14-299, 14-300 or 14-300b to 14-300d, inclusive, shall be construed to limit the provisions of section 53-181 or to permit any pedestrian to walk upon or along any highway where pedestrians are prohibited by any provision of the general statutes or any regulations issued thereunder.

**What it means:** General pedestrian rights do not override the hitchhiking statute or the limited-access pedestrian ban.

### Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-300c(a): Pedestrian use of roads and sidewalks

**Source:** [LawServer mirror of Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-300c](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/connecticut/ct-laws/connecticut_statutes_14-300c) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (a) No pedestrian shall walk along and upon a roadway where a sidewalk adjacent to such roadway is provided and the use thereof is practicable. Where a sidewalk is not provided adjacent to a roadway each pedestrian walking along and upon such roadway shall walk only on the shoulder thereof and as far as practicable from the edge of such roadway. [...]

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one. Otherwise walk on the shoulder, away from the lane.
