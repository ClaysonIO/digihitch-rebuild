# District of Columbia (US-DC)

**Status:** Restricted. D.C. traffic regulations ban standing *in a roadway* to solicit a ride from a driver. The civil fine is $10. Hitchhiking is also banned on National Park Service land in the city unless the park has designated an area for it.

## Summary

The District's rule is in the D.C. Municipal Regulations (DCMR), Title 18, not in the D.C. Code: "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle" (18 DCMR § 2305.4). "Roadway" is the part of the highway "improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel." Unlike many states, the D.C. definition does not expressly mention shoulders. A sidewalk or curb is still clearly outside the roadway, so asking for a ride from the sidewalk is not covered by § 2305.4.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Don't step into the street to flag cars.** Stand on the sidewalk or curb. The fine for "soliciting rides while standing in roadway" is $10 (§ 2603.1).
- **Federal park land is different.** Large parts of D.C. are National Park Service property (for example the National Mall and Rock Creek Park). There, federal rule 36 C.F.R. § 4.31 prohibits "hitchhiking or soliciting transportation" except in designated areas, wherever you stand.
- **Freeways:** D.C. regulations let the DDOT Director restrict "any class or kind of traffic" on controlled-access roadways by posting signs. When signs are posted, disobeying them is a violation. I did not find a regulation that bans pedestrians from all D.C. freeways outright (see open questions), but expect signed bans at ramps. Stay off the interstates and other freeways on foot.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** if there is one; otherwise on the left side facing traffic (§ 2305.2, § 2305.3).

## Laws

### 18 DCMR § 2305: Walking and standing on sidewalks and roadways

**Source:** [D.C. Office of Documents and Administrative Issuances, DCRegs, 18 DCMR § 2305](https://dcregs.dc.gov/Common/DCMR/SectionList.aspx?SectionNumber=18-2305) (current text, as amended by Final Rulemaking at 68 DCR 7212, July 23, 2021)

> 2305.2 Where a paved sidewalk is provided, a pedestrian shall not walk upon a roadway adjacent to the sidewalk, except as appropriate to avoid a risk to the pedestrian's, or another individual's, safety or health.
>
> 2305.3 Where sidewalks are not provided, any pedestrian walking along and upon a street or highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic, which may approach from the opposite direction.
>
> 2305.4 No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle.

**What it means:** You may not stand in the street itself to ask for a ride. From the sidewalk or curb is not covered by this rule. If you walk where there is no sidewalk, walk on the left facing traffic.

### 18 DCMR § 9901: Definitions ("Highway", "Roadway", "Controlled Access Highway")

**Source:** [DCRegs, 18 DCMR § 9901](https://dcregs.dc.gov/Common/DCMR/SectionList.aspx?SectionNumber=18-9901)

> Controlled Access Highway - a highway, street, or roadway with respect to which owners or occupants of abutting property or lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same, except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the Council.
>
> [...]
>
> Highway - the entire width between the boundary lines of every publicly maintained way, when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular or pedestrian travel. (Reg. No. 71-26)
>
> [...]
>
> Roadway - that portion of a highway which is improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term roadway as used herein shall refer to any such roadway separately, but not to all such roadways collectively. (Reg. No. 71-26)

**What it means:** The roadway is the part of the street cars drive on. The sidewalk is not part of it. D.C.'s definition, unlike Pennsylvania's or Maryland's, does not expressly carve out the shoulder, so on the few D.C. roads with paved shoulders it is less certain that the shoulder is safe ground.

### 18 DCMR § 2201.13 and § 2201.14: Restrictions on controlled-access roadways

**Source:** [DCRegs, 18 DCMR § 2201](https://dcregs.dc.gov/Common/DCMR/SectionList.aspx?SectionNumber=18-2201) (Final Rulemaking at 42 DCR 3064, June 16, 1995)

> 2201.13 The Director may regulate or prohibit the use of any controlled-access roadway or highway by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic.
>
> 2201.14 The Director shall erect and maintain official traffic control devices on the controlled access highway on which such prohibitions are applicable and, when in place, no person shall disobey restrictions on such devices.

**What it means:** DDOT closes freeways to classes of traffic (such as pedestrians) by posting signs. If a sign at the ramp bars pedestrians, walking past it is a violation.

### 18 DCMR § 2603: Pedestrian infractions (fines)

**Source:** [DCRegs, 18 DCMR § 2603](https://dcregs.dc.gov/Common/DCMR/SectionList.aspx?SectionNumber=18-2603) (as amended by Final Rulemaking at 66 DCR 56, January 4, 2019)

> 2603.1 The following civil infractions and their respective fines set forth in this section refer to pedestrians:
>
> [...]
>
> Roadway [...] Walking in (sidewalk provided) [§ 2305.2] $ 10.00
>
> Walking on wrong side (no sidewalk) [§ 2305.3] $ 10.00
>
> Soliciting rides while standing in roadway [§ 2305.4] $ 10.00

**What it means:** Hitchhiking from the street is a civil infraction, like a parking ticket, with a $10 fine. It is not a crime.

## Notable local rules

### 36 C.F.R. § 4.31: Hitchhiking in National Park Service areas (federal)

**Source:** [eCFR, 36 C.F.R. § 4.31](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/chapter-I/part-4/section-4.31) (last amended 2016-12-21)

> § 4.31 Hitchhiking.
>
> Hitchhiking or soliciting transportation is prohibited except in designated areas and under conditions established by the superintendent.

**What it means:** On roads and land run by the National Park Service, hitchhiking is banned outright unless the park has set aside an area for it. In D.C. this covers a lot of ground, including the National Mall and Rock Creek Park and their parkways. This is federal law enforced by the U.S. Park Police, and it applies whether or not you are standing in the roadway.
