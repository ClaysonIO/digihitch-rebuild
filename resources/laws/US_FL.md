# Florida (US-FL)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in the paved travel part of a roadway to solicit a ride. The shoulder and curb are outside the "roadway" as Florida defines it. Pedestrians are banned from limited access highways and from their ramps.

## Summary

Florida's hitchhiking rule is narrower than most. Fla. Stat. § 316.130(5) only bans standing "in the portion of a roadway paved for vehicular traffic" to solicit a ride. Florida defines "roadway" to exclude the berm or shoulder. Standing on the shoulder, curb or sidewalk with your thumb out is therefore not covered by this rule.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay off limited access highways and their ramps.** Florida bans pedestrians from walking on a limited access facility (interstates, turnpikes, expressways) or on a ramp that connects one to another road. Hitchhike from the surface street before the ramp starts.
- **Don't block traffic.** A separate statute (§ 316.2045) makes it a pedestrian violation to willfully obstruct a street by standing in it or impeding traffic.
- **Walk facing traffic** on the left shoulder where there is no sidewalk.

A violation of § 316.130 is a noncriminal traffic infraction. The pedestrian penalty in § 318.18(1) is $15, plus any court costs.

## Laws

### Fla. Stat. § 316.130: Pedestrians; traffic regulations

**Source:** [Florida Legislature, Online Sunshine, Fla. Stat. § 316.130](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.130.html) (2026 Florida Statutes; history ends with s. 2, ch. 2008-33)

> (3) Where sidewalks are provided, no pedestrian shall, unless required by other circumstances, walk along and upon the portion of a roadway paved for vehicular traffic.
>
> (4) Where sidewalks are not provided, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the shoulder on the left side of the roadway in relation to the pedestrian's direction of travel, facing traffic which may approach from the opposite direction.
>
> (5) No person shall stand in the portion of a roadway paved for vehicular traffic for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, or business from the occupant of any vehicle.

**What it means:** The ban covers only the paved travel lanes. Standing on the shoulder, the curb or the sidewalk to solicit a ride is not prohibited by this subsection. Pick a spot where a driver can pull completely off the road. Where there is no sidewalk, walk on the left shoulder facing traffic.

> (18) No pedestrian shall walk upon a limited access facility or a ramp connecting a limited access facility to any other street or highway; however, this subsection does not apply to maintenance personnel of any governmental subdivision.

**What it means:** Interstates, Florida's Turnpike and other limited access expressways are off limits on foot, and so are their on-ramps and off-ramps. Stand on the local road before the ramp begins.

> (19) A violation of this section is a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable pursuant to chapter 318 as either a pedestrian violation or, if the infraction resulted from the operation of a vehicle, as a moving violation.

**What it means:** A violation is a ticket, not a crime.

### Fla. Stat. § 316.003: Definitions ("roadway" and "limited access facility")

**Source:** [Florida Legislature, Online Sunshine, Fla. Stat. § 316.003](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.003.html) (2026 Florida Statutes)

> (36) LIMITED ACCESS FACILITY.—A street or highway especially designed for through traffic and over, from, or to which owners or occupants of abutting land or other persons have no right or easement, or only a limited right or easement, of access, light, air, or view by reason of the fact that their property abuts upon such limited access facility or for any other reason. Such highways or streets may be parkways from which trucks, buses, and other commercial vehicles are excluded or may be freeways open to use by all customary forms of street and highway traffic.
>
> [...]
>
> (74) ROADWAY.—That portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. If a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" refers to any such roadway separately, but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the "roadway." Section 316.130(5) goes further and limits the ban to the *paved travel* part of the roadway.

### Fla. Stat. § 316.2045: Obstruction of public streets, highways, and roads

**Source:** [Florida Legislature, Online Sunshine, Fla. Stat. § 316.2045](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2045.html) (2026 Florida Statutes; last amended s. 2, ch. 2021-6)

> (1)(a) A person may not willfully obstruct the free, convenient, and normal use of a public street, highway, or road by:
> 1. Impeding, hindering, stifling, retarding, or restraining traffic or passage thereon;
> 2. Standing on or remaining in the street, highway, or road; or
> 3. Endangering the safe movement of vehicles or pedestrians traveling thereon.
>
> (b) A person who violates paragraph (a) shall be cited for a pedestrian violation, punishable as provided in chapter 318.

**What it means:** This is a general anti-obstruction rule, not a hitchhiking rule. It does mean that standing in the street so that cars must stop or swerve can get you a ticket. Stay clear of traffic.

### Fla. Stat. § 318.18: Amount of penalties

**Source:** [Florida Legislature, Online Sunshine, Fla. Stat. § 318.18](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0318/Sections/0318.18.html) (2026 Florida Statutes)

> The penalties required for a noncriminal disposition pursuant to s. 318.14 or a criminal offense listed in s. 318.17 are as follows:
> (1) Fifteen dollars for:
> (a) All infractions of pedestrian regulations.

**What it means:** The base penalty for a pedestrian ticket is $15. Court costs and fees may be added.

## History

### 2021: § 316.2045 rewritten

**Source:** [Fla. Stat. § 316.2045 history note](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2045.html)

The section's history line shows it was last amended by chapter 2021-6, Laws of Florida. The current text makes willful obstruction a pedestrian violation. The ride-solicitation rule in § 316.130(5) itself has not changed since at least 2008.
