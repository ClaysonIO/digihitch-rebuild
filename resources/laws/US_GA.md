# Georgia (US-GA)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in the roadway to solicit a ride. Georgia defines "roadway" to exclude the berm and shoulder, so hitchhiking from the shoulder or curb is not covered by this rule.

## Summary

Georgia follows the Uniform Vehicle Code pattern. O.C.G.A. § 40-6-97(a) says "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride." Georgia's definition of "roadway" excludes the berm or shoulder. The rule therefore targets people standing in the travel lanes.

Note the contrast with subsection (b). That subsection bans soliciting employment, business or contributions while standing anywhere "on a highway," which includes the shoulder. That broader wording does not apply to soliciting rides.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand on the shoulder or sidewalk, not in the lanes.**
- **Use the sidewalk where there is one.** Georgia makes it unlawful to stand or walk on the roadway next to a sidewalk unless no vehicle is within 1,000 feet.
- **Where there's no sidewalk, stay on the shoulder.**
- **Interstates:** No Georgia statute banning pedestrians from controlled-access highways was found in this research (see open questions). Many interstate ramps are signed against pedestrians. Hitchhiking from the surface road before the ramp is the safer choice.

Violations of Chapter 6 of Title 40 are misdemeanors unless the chapter says otherwise (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-1).

## Laws

### O.C.G.A. § 40-6-97: Soliciting rides, business, or watching of vehicles

**Source:** [FindLaw, Georgia Code § 40-6-97](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-40-motor-vehicles-and-traffic/ga-code-sect-40-6-97.html) (unofficial mirror, marked "Current as of March 28, 2024"). Georgia's official code is published by LexisNexis and could not be retrieved in this session. Subsection (a) also matches the Georgia Governor's Office of Highway Safety [pedestrian safety page](https://www.gahighwaysafety.org/pedestrian-safety/).

> (a) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
>
> (b) Except as provided in Code Section 40-6-97.1, no person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (c) No person shall stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes with your thumb out. The ride rule says "roadway," which excludes the shoulder. The business and contribution rule in (b) says "highway," which covers the whole right-of-way. Hitchhikers fall under (a), not (b).

### O.C.G.A. § 40-1-1: Definitions ("roadway," "highway," "controlled-access highway")

**Source:** [FindLaw, Georgia Code § 40-1-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-40-motor-vehicles-and-traffic/ga-code-sect-40-1-1.html) (unofficial mirror, marked "Current as of March 28, 2024")

> (9) [...] every highway, street, or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same except only at such points and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over such highway, street, or roadway.
>
> [...]
>
> (19) [...] the entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel.
>
> [...]
>
> (53) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" shall refer to any such roadway separately, but not to all such roadways collectively.

(Paragraph (9) is the definition of "controlled-access highway" and paragraph (19) is the definition of "highway." The opening words of each were not captured verbatim.)

**What it means:** The "roadway" is the travel lanes only. The shoulder is outside it. A "highway" is everything between the boundary lines, shoulders included.

### O.C.G.A. § 40-6-96: Pedestrians on or along roadway

**Source:** [FindLaw, Georgia Code § 40-6-96](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-40-motor-vehicles-and-traffic/ga-code-sect-40-6-96.html) (unofficial mirror, marked "Current as of March 28, 2024")

> (a) As used in this Code section, the term "pedestrian" means any person afoot and shall include, without limitation, persons standing, walking, jogging, running, or otherwise on foot.
>
> (b) Where a sidewalk is provided, it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to stand or stride along and upon an adjacent roadway unless there is no motor vehicle traveling within 1,000 feet of such pedestrian on such roadway or the available sidewalk presents an imminent threat of bodily injury to such pedestrian.
>
> (c) Where a sidewalk is not provided but a shoulder is available, any pedestrian standing or striding along and upon a highway shall stand or stride only on the shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (d) Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, any pedestrian standing or striding along and upon a highway shall stand or stride as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway, and, if on a two-lane roadway, shall stand or stride only on the left side of the roadway.
>
> (e) Except as otherwise provided in this chapter, any pedestrian upon a roadway shall yield the right of way to all vehicles upon the roadway.

**What it means:** This section expressly covers *standing*, not just walking. If there's a sidewalk, stand on it. If there's only a shoulder, stand on the shoulder, as far from the lanes as you can. Pick a spot with a wide shoulder or pull-off where a driver can stop safely.

### O.C.G.A. § 40-6-1: Penalty

**Source:** [FindLaw, Georgia Code § 40-6-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-40-motor-vehicles-and-traffic/ga-code-sect-40-6-1.html) (unofficial mirror, marked "Current as of March 28, 2024")

> (a) It is unlawful and, unless otherwise declared in this chapter with respect to particular offenses, it is a misdemeanor for any person to do any act forbidden or fail to perform any act required in this chapter.

**What it means:** Standing in the roadway to solicit a ride is technically a misdemeanor. In practice it is usually handled as a citation.

## Unverified / open questions

- **Freeway pedestrians:** No Georgia statute or GDOT rule that expressly bars pedestrians from interstates or controlled-access highways was found. O.C.G.A. § 40-6-50 bars *driving* onto a controlled-access roadway except at established entrances and exits, but it doesn't address pedestrians. Personal-injury firm articles say pedestrians are "generally prohibited" on interstates but cite only § 40-6-96, which does not say that. Many ramps carry "pedestrians prohibited" signs, which may be enforceable under traffic-control-device statutes; this was not confirmed.
- **Text source:** The quoted text comes from FindLaw (current as of March 28, 2024). Justia and the official LexisNexis site could not be reached. It was retrieved through a fetch tool that returned the text in quoted segments, then joined here. Subsection (a) of § 40-6-97 is confirmed by a second source (Georgia GOHS). Check the official code for any 2024 to 2026 amendments. A search found no 2025 or 2026 bill amending § 40-6-97.
- The exact maximum misdemeanor fine (O.C.G.A. § 17-10-3) was not retrieved.
- Local ordinances (for example, Atlanta) were not checked.
