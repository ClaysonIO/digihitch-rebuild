# Hawaii (US-HI)

**Status:** Illegal statewide, unless a county ordinance allows it. HRS § 291C-77(a) bans standing in, walking along, or otherwise occupying any part of a highway to solicit a ride. Because "highway" covers the whole public right-of-way, that includes the shoulder. Counties can carve out exceptions.

## Summary

Hawaii has one of the broader hitchhiking bans in the US. The state statute doesn't stop at the travel lanes. It covers "a portion of a highway", which is the entire width between the boundary lines of the public way. The statute opens with "Except as otherwise provided by county ordinance", so a county can allow hitchhiking in some places. Honolulu (Oʻahu) is reported to do so, but I have not verified its ordinance text (see open questions).

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Statewide default: no soliciting rides from any part of a highway**, shoulders included.
- **County ordinances can override the state ban.** Check the rules for the island you are on.
- **Freeways:** the state and counties can ban classes of traffic from controlled-access highways by posting signs, and you must obey them (HRS § 291C-53 and § 291C-71).
- **Walk on the sidewalk** where one exists. Otherwise walk on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder, facing traffic.

A violation is a traffic violation (not a crime), with a fine of up to $250 for a first offence.

## Laws

### HRS § 291C-77: Pedestrians soliciting rides, business, or attention of motorist

**Source:** [Hawaii State Legislature, HRS § 291C-77](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol05_Ch0261-0319/HRS0291C/HRS_0291C-0077.htm) (official current HRS, history note through L 2015, c 35)

> (a) Except as otherwise provided by county ordinance, no person shall stand in, walk along, or otherwise occupy a portion of a highway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (b) No person shall stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway. [L 1971, c 150, pt of §1; am L 1973, c 216, §2; am L 2015, c 35, §5]

**What it means:** Unless a county ordinance says otherwise, you can't stand anywhere on a public road, shoulder included, to thumb a ride. The statute's annotations note that a 1975 Attorney General opinion (Att. Gen. Op. 75-17) found subsection (a) "is not unconstitutionally vague." To stay within the state law, solicit rides from private property next to the road, such as a parking lot or gas station, or from a spot a county ordinance expressly allows.

### HRS § 291C-1: Definitions of "highway", "roadway" and "controlled-access highway"

**Source:** [Hawaii State Legislature, HRS § 291C-1](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol05_Ch0261-0319/HRS0291C/HRS_0291C-0001.htm) (official current HRS)

> "Controlled-access highway" means every highway, street, or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over such highway, street, or roadway.
>
> [...]
>
> "Highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained and those private streets, as defined in section 46-16, over which the application of this chapter has been extended by ordinance, when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel.
>
> [...]
>
> "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways the term "roadway" as used herein refers to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the "roadway", but it is part of the "highway". § 291C-77 uses "highway", so the shoulder gives you no safe harbour in Hawaii.

### HRS § 291C-76: Pedestrians on roadways

**Source:** [Hawaii State Legislature, HRS § 291C-76](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol05_Ch0261-0319/HRS0291C/HRS_0291C-0076.htm) (official current HRS)

> (a) Where sidewalks are provided it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway, bicycle lane, or bicycle path.
>
> (b) Where sidewalks are not provided any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic which may approach from the opposite direction.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one. Otherwise walk on the left side, facing oncoming traffic.

### HRS § 291C-53 and § 291C-71: Controlled-access highways and obeying signs

**Source:** [Hawaii State Legislature, HRS § 291C-53](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol05_Ch0261-0319/HRS0291C/HRS_0291C-0053.htm) and [HRS § 291C-71](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol05_Ch0261-0319/HRS0291C/HRS_0291C-0071.htm) (official current HRS)

> **§ 291C-53** (a) The director of transportation by rules adopted in accordance with chapter 91, and the counties by ordinance, may regulate or prohibit the use of any controlled-access roadway or highway within their respective jurisdictions by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic. [...]
>
> (b) The director of transportation or a county adopting any such prohibition shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the controlled-access roadway or highway on which such prohibitions are applicable and when in place no person shall disobey the restrictions stated on such devices.

> **§ 291C-71** (a) A pedestrian shall obey the instructions of any official traffic-control device specifically applicable to the pedestrian, unless otherwise directed by a police officer.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians from freeways by name. Instead, the state or a county can ban them, and the ban takes effect through posted signs. Where a sign bans pedestrians, you may not walk there. Wait on the surface street before the on-ramp.

### HRS § 291C-161: Penalties

**Source:** [Hawaii State Legislature, HRS § 291C-161](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol05_Ch0261-0319/HRS0291C/HRS_0291C-0161.htm) (official current HRS)

> (a) It shall be a violation for any person to violate any of the provisions of this chapter, except as otherwise specified in subsections (c) and (d) and unless the violation is by other law of this State declared to be a felony, misdemeanor, or petty misdemeanor.
>
> (b) Except as provided in subsections (c) and (d), every person who is determined to have violated any provision of this chapter for which another penalty is not provided shall be fined not more than:
> (1) $250 for a first violation thereof;
> (2) $300 for a second violation committed within one year after the date of the first violation; and
> (3) $500 for a third or subsequent violation committed within one year after the date of the first violation.

**What it means:** Hitchhiking against § 291C-77 is a "violation", the lowest level of offence, not a crime. The fine is up to $250 the first time and climbs for repeat violations within a year.
