# Iowa (US-IA)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride from a private driver. Iowa's statute expressly protects soliciting a ride from the part of the highway "not ordinarily used for vehicular traffic." Being a pedestrian anywhere on a fully controlled-access highway is illegal.

## Summary

Iowa has one of the clearest hitchhiking statutes in the country. Iowa Code § 321.331(1) bans standing in the roadway to solicit a ride from the driver of a private vehicle. Subsection 2 then states that nothing in the traffic code prevents a pedestrian from standing on the part of the highway "not ordinarily used for vehicular traffic" to solicit a ride. In plain terms, hitchhiking from the shoulder is expressly lawful.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand on the shoulder, not in a lane.** The statute protects you there.
- **Stay off interstates and other fully controlled-access highways completely.** Section 321.366(1)(g) makes it unlawful to "be a pedestrian, anywhere" on such a facility. Drivers also may not stop on the shoulders or right-of-way except at rest areas or in an emergency. Wait on the surface road before the on-ramp, or at a truck stop.
- **Walk on the left side of the highway** (§ 321.326).

The scheduled fine for soliciting a ride from the roadway is $35. Being a pedestrian on a controlled-access highway carries a $135 scheduled fine.

## Laws

### Iowa Code § 321.331: Pedestrians soliciting rides

**Source:** [Iowa Legislature, Iowa Code 2026, § 321.331](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/321.331.pdf)

> 1. No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle.
> 2. Nothing in this section or this chapter shall be construed so as to prevent any pedestrian from standing on that portion of the highway or roadway, not ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle.

**What it means:** Don't stand in a traffic lane to hitch. Iowa law expressly says you may stand on the part of the road not ordinarily used by traffic, such as the shoulder, to ask for a ride. This is a statutory green light for shoulder hitchhiking, but it does not override the controlled-access ban below.

### Iowa Code § 321.1: Definitions

**Source:** [Iowa Legislature, Iowa Code 2026, § 321.1](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/321.1.pdf)

> 65. "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel.
>
> [...]
>
> 78. "Street" or "highway" means the entire width between property lines of every way or place of whatever nature when any part thereof is open to the use of the public, as a matter of right, for purposes of vehicular traffic.

**What it means:** The roadway is the travel lanes. The highway is the whole public right-of-way. Section 321.331(2) settles the question for hitchhikers by protecting the part "not ordinarily used for vehicular traffic."

### Iowa Code § 321.366: Acts prohibited on fully controlled-access facilities

**Source:** [Iowa Legislature, Iowa Code 2026, § 321.366](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/321.366.pdf) (last amended 2024 Acts, ch 1137, §6)

> 1. It is unlawful for a person, except a person operating highway maintenance equipment or an authorized emergency vehicle, to do any of the following on a fully controlled-access facility:
> [...]
> f. Stop, park, or leave standing a vehicle, whether attended or unattended, upon the shoulders, or the right-of-way except at designated rest areas or in case of an emergency or other dire necessity.
> g. Ride a bicycle or use a pedestrian conveyance, or be a pedestrian, anywhere on a fully controlled-access facility.
> 2. For the purpose of this section, "fully controlled-access facility" is a highway which gives preference to through traffic by providing access connections at interchanges with selected public roads only and by prohibiting crossings at grade or direct access at driveway connections.
> 3. Violations of this section are punishable as a scheduled violation under section 805.8A, subsection 6.

**What it means:** Don't set foot on an interstate or similar freeway, ramps and shoulders included ("anywhere"). A driver who stops on a freeway shoulder to pick you up is also breaking the law. Rest areas are the exception for stopping, but as a pedestrian you are still "on" the facility there, so it's safer to hitch from the surface road before the ramp.

### Iowa Code § 321.326: Pedestrians on left

**Source:** [Iowa Legislature, Iowa Code 2026, § 321.326](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/321.326.pdf)

> Pedestrians shall at all times when walking on or along a highway, walk on the left side of such highway.

**What it means:** When walking along a road, stay on the left, facing oncoming traffic.

### Iowa Code § 805.8A: Motor vehicle and transportation scheduled violations

**Source:** [Iowa Legislature, Iowa Code 2026, § 805.8A](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/805.8A.pdf)

> 6. Operating violations. For operating violations under the following sections, the scheduled fine is as follows:
> [...]
> y. Section 321.366 ........................................ $135.
>
> 9. Bicycle or pedestrian violations. For bicycle or pedestrian violations under the following sections, the scheduled fine for a pedestrian or bicyclist is as follows:
> [...]
> h. Section 321.331........................................ $ 35.

**What it means:** Hitching from a traffic lane carries a $35 scheduled fine. Walking on an interstate carries a $135 scheduled fine. Court costs and surcharges are added to both.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may stand on the shoulder, or any part of the road not ordinarily used by traffic, to ask for a ride; Iowa law says so expressly. Not in travel lanes, and never anywhere on an interstate or freeway.

> **Iowa Code § 321.331**
> 1. No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle.
> 2. Nothing in this section or this chapter shall be construed so as to prevent any pedestrian from standing on that portion of the highway or roadway, not ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle.

> **Iowa Code § 321.366(1)(g)**
> 1. It is unlawful for a person, [...] to do any of the following on a fully controlled-access facility:
> g. Ride a bicycle or use a pedestrian conveyance, or be a pedestrian, anywhere on a fully controlled-access facility.
