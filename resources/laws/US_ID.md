# Idaho (US-ID)

**Status:** Illegal. Idaho Code § 49-709 says "No person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting a ride". Idaho's definition of "highway" expressly includes sidewalks, shoulders, berms and rights-of-way. Violation is an infraction.

## Summary

Idaho's hitchhiking rule uses the word *highway*, not *roadway*. Idaho's definition of highway goes further than most states. It spells out that the highway extends to the adjacent property line, "including sidewalks, shoulders, berms and rights-of-way". Standing on the shoulder, or even the sidewalk, to solicit a ride is therefore covered.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Don't stand anywhere on the highway right-of-way to solicit a ride**, shoulder and sidewalk included. Practical options are private property such as gas stations, truck stops and parking lots, with the owner's permission, or asking drivers directly.
- **Walking** along a highway is allowed. Use the sidewalk if there is one, otherwise the shoulder. With neither, walk on the left side of a two-way road.
- **Interstates and other controlled-access highways:** the Idaho Transportation Department must regulate or prohibit traffic that is incompatible with normal, safe traffic flow, and must sign any prohibitions. Obey "pedestrians prohibited" signs.
- **Penalty:** an infraction. The maximum is $300, with no jail.

## Laws

### Idaho Code § 49-709: Pedestrians soliciting rides or business

**Source:** [Idaho Legislature, Idaho Code § 49-709](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH7/SECT49-709/) (history: added 1988, ch. 265, sec. 200; am. 2001, ch. 392, sec. 1)

> (1) No person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
>
> (2) No person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle, provided however, that a person may stand on a highway other than a state or federal highway to solicit contributions if authorized to do so in writing by the local authority having jurisdiction over the highway, and provided further, that any such authorization shall not be valid for more than one (1) year from the date of issuance.

**What it means:** The ban covers the whole highway, not just the travel lanes. Read literally, a sign held up from the shoulder is a violation. Hitching in Idaho is safest, legally, from private property off the highway, such as fuel stations, where you aren't "on a highway". Highway rest areas may be within the highway right-of-way, so they are not a safe bet.

### Idaho Code § 49-109(4): "Highway" defined

**Source:** [Idaho Legislature, Idaho Code § 49-109](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH1/SECT49-109/) (history: added 1988; last am. 2019, ch. 213, sec. 6)

> (4) "Highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained when any part is open to the use of the public for vehicular travel, with jurisdiction extending to the adjacent property line, including sidewalks, shoulders, berms and rights-of-way not intended for motorized traffic. The term "street" is interchangeable with highway.

**What it means:** Shoulders and sidewalks are part of the "highway" in Idaho. That is why the hitchhiking ban reaches the shoulder.

### Idaho Code § 49-119(19): "Roadway" defined

**Source:** [Idaho Legislature, Idaho Code § 49-119](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH1/SECT49-119/) (history: added 1988; last am. 2024, ch. 234, sec. 1)

> (19) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of sidewalks, shoulders, berms and rights-of-way.

**What it means:** It's included for contrast. Had the legislature written "roadway" in § 49-709, the shoulder would be fine. It wrote "highway".

### Idaho Code § 49-708: Pedestrians on highways

**Source:** [Idaho Legislature, Idaho Code § 49-708](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH7/SECT49-708/) (added 1988, ch. 265, sec. 199)

> (1) Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (2) Where a sidewalk is not available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk only on a shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (3) Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway, and, if on a two-way highway, shall walk only on the left side of the highway.

**What it means:** Walking along a highway is lawful if you keep to the sidewalk or shoulder. Soliciting a ride while you're there is what § 49-709 prohibits.

### Idaho Code §§ 49-202(23)-(24) and 49-208(1)(k): Controlled-access highways

**Source:** [Idaho Legislature, Idaho Code § 49-202](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH2/SECT49-202/) and [§ 49-208](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH2/SECT49-208/)

§ 49-202:

> (23) The department shall regulate or prohibit the use of any controlled-access highway by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic.
>
> (24) The department shall erect and maintain traffic-control devices on controlled-access highways on which any prohibitions are applicable.

§ 49-208:

> (1) The provisions of this title shall not be deemed to prevent local authorities with respect to highways under their jurisdiction and within the reasonable exercise of the police power from: [...]
> (k) Prohibiting or regulating the use of controlled-access highways by any class or kind of traffic;

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians on Idaho interstates outright. Restrictions come from the Transportation Department or local authorities and are shown by signs. Hitching on any part of an interstate, ramps included, is in any case already covered by § 49-709, because ramps are part of the highway.

### Idaho Code §§ 49-236 and 18-113A: Penalty

**Source:** [Idaho Legislature, Idaho Code § 49-236](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH2/SECT49-236/) and [§ 18-113A](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title18/T18CH1/SECT18-113A/)

§ 49-236:

> (2) It is an infraction for any person to violate any of the provisions of chapters 3, 4 and 6 through 9 of this title unless otherwise specifically provided.

§ 18-113A:

> Every offense declared to be an infraction is punishable only by a penalty not exceeding three hundred dollars ($300) as provided in this section and no imprisonment. The penalty for an infraction shall be:
> (1) The amount set by statute;
> (2) Subject to subsection (1) of this section, the amount set as a fixed penalty for that infraction as of January 1, 2014, by the Idaho supreme court infraction rule 9 [...];
> (3) The amount set by city or county ordinance [...]; or
> (4) Fifteen dollars and fifty cents ($15.50) for an infraction without a specific penalty set under subsection (1), (2) or (3) of this section.

**What it means:** Section 49-709 is in chapter 7, so a violation is an infraction: a ticket, no jail, and at most $300. The exact amount comes from the Idaho Supreme Court's infraction rule schedule, which I didn't retrieve.

## Unverified / open questions

- The fixed penalty for § 49-709 under Idaho Infraction Rule 9 was not retrieved.
- I did not find an Idaho Transportation Department administrative rule (IDAPA) listing controlled-access highways closed to pedestrians under § 49-202(23). Closures are shown by signs.
