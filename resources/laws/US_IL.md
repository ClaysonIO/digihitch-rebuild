# Illinois (US-IL)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride (625 ILCS 5/11-1006(a)). Illinois defines the roadway as "exclusive of the berm or shoulder." On the Illinois Tollway, soliciting rides is banned outright. Pedestrians are kept off controlled-access highways where the state has posted signs.

## Summary

Illinois uses the Uniform Vehicle Code wording: "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle." The Illinois Vehicle Code defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicle travel, "exclusive of the berm or shoulder." So thumbing from the shoulder, curb or sidewalk of an ordinary road is not covered by § 11-1006(a). The penalty is unusually harsh, though: any violation of § 11-1006 is a **Class A misdemeanor**, which is a criminal offense.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Stand on the shoulder or sidewalk, never in the lane.
- **Don't solicit money or work** from drivers anywhere on the highway (§ 11-1006(b) and (c)). A sign asking for gas money can turn a lawful ride request into a solicitation offense.
- **Don't walk onto a controlled-access highway** (interstate or expressway) where it is signed as closed to pedestrians. The Illinois Department of Transportation (IDOT) may ban pedestrians and must post signs (§ 11-711(b)).
- **Don't hitchhike on the Illinois Tollway** (I-88, I-90, I-94, I-294, I-355 and the other tolled routes, including the oases). Tollway rules ban soliciting rides from drivers "using the Tollway," ban loitering, and bar drivers from stopping on the right-of-way.

## Laws

### 625 ILCS 5/11-1006: Pedestrians soliciting rides or business

**Source:** [Onecle, 625 ILCS 5/11-1006](https://law.onecle.com/illinois/625ilcs5/11-1006.html) (unofficial mirror, page last modified February 18, 2015; source line "P.A. 88-589, eff. 8-14-94"). Subsections (a) and (e) cross-checked against [FindLaw's mirror](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-625-vehicles/il-st-sect-625-5-11-1006/) ("current as of January 01, 2025"), which shows the same wording. The official site, ilga.gov, refused automated access.

> (a) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle.
>
> (b) No person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment or business from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (c) No person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting contributions from the occupant of any vehicle except within a municipality when expressly permitted by municipal ordinance. [...]
>
> (d) No person shall stand on or in the proximity of a highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a highway.
>
> (e) Every person who is convicted of a violation of this Section shall be guilty of a Class A misdemeanor.

**What it means:** Asking for a ride is illegal only while you stand *in the roadway*, meaning the travel lanes. Standing on the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered (see § 1-179 below). Subsections (b) and (c) are broader. They apply to the whole "highway" right-of-way and cover asking for work, business or money, so keep your sign to a destination. Every violation of this section is a Class A misdemeanor, a criminal charge rather than a traffic ticket.

### 625 ILCS 5/1-179: Roadway

**Source:** [Onecle, 625 ILCS 5/1-179](https://law.onecle.com/illinois/625ilcs5/1-179.html) (unofficial mirror, page last modified February 18, 2015; source line "P.A. 76-1586")

> That portion of a highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways the term "roadway" as used herein shall refer to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder is expressly *not* part of the roadway. That is why thumbing from the shoulder doesn't violate § 11-1006(a).

### 625 ILCS 5/1-126: Highway

**Source:** [Onecle, 625 ILCS 5/1-126](https://law.onecle.com/illinois/625ilcs5/1-126.html) (unofficial mirror, page last modified February 18, 2015; source line "P.A. 92-780, eff. 8-6-02")

> The entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel or located on public school property.

**What it means:** "Highway" is the whole right-of-way, including shoulders, so the bans in § 11-1006(b) to (d) reach further than the ride rule in (a).

### 625 ILCS 5/11-711: Restrictions on use of controlled access highway

**Source:** [Onecle, 625 ILCS 5/11-711](https://law.onecle.com/illinois/625ilcs5/11-711.html) (unofficial mirror, page last modified February 18, 2015; source line "P.A. 76-1586")

> (b) The Department with respect to any controlled access highway under its jurisdiction may prohibit the use of any such highways by pedestrians (except in authorized areas or facilities), bicycles, farm tractors, implements of husbandry, funeral processions, and any vehicle unable to maintain the minimum speed for which the highway is posted, or other non-motorized traffic or by any person operating a motor driven cycle. [...] The Department shall erect and maintain official signs on the controlled access highway on which such prohibitions are applicable and when so erected no person may disobey the restrictions stated on such sign.

**What it means:** Illinois statute doesn't ban pedestrians from every freeway automatically. IDOT may close a controlled-access highway to pedestrians, and when it posts signs, you must obey them. Interstate on-ramps are normally signed "no pedestrians," so wait at the start of the ramp on the surface street.

### 625 ILCS 5/11-1007: Pedestrians walking on highways

**Source:** [Onecle, 625 ILCS 5/11-1007](https://law.onecle.com/illinois/625ilcs5/11-1007.html) (unofficial mirror, page last modified February 18, 2015; source line "P.A. 97-1023, eff. 1-1-13")

> (a) Except as provided in subsection (e), where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (b) Except as provided in subsection (e), where a sidewalk is not available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk only on a shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (c) Except as provided in subsection (e), where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk as near as practicable to an outside edge of a roadway, and, if on a two-way roadway, shall walk only on the left side of the roadway.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one. Otherwise use the shoulder, as far from traffic as you can. With neither, walk on the left facing traffic.

### 92 Ill. Adm. Code 2520.254: Solicitation of Rides (Illinois Tollway)

**Source:** [Cornell LII, Ill. Admin. Code tit. 92, § 2520.254](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-92-SS-2520.254) (unofficial mirror of the Illinois State Toll Highway Authority rules)

> No person shall solicit or attempt to solicit rides from the driver of any vehicle using the Tollway.

**What it means:** On the Illinois Tollway, any attempt to get a ride from a driver is banned. The rule isn't limited to the roadway, so it plausibly covers the shoulders, ramps and oases. Get dropped off before the toll road, or ask at a truck stop off the Tollway.

### 92 Ill. Adm. Code 2520.257, 2520.230 and 2520.320: Loitering, stopping and use of the Tollway

**Source:** Cornell LII mirrors of [§ 2520.257](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-92-SS-2520.257), [§ 2520.230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-92-SS-2520.230) and [§ 2520.320](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-92-SS-2520.320)

> **2520.257:** No person shall loiter on Tollway property or in any manner interfere with traffic or the control of traffic.

> **2520.230:** Parking, standing or stopping on the right-of-way is prohibited. In emergencies, stopped vehicles shall be immediately driven or moved off the traveled portion of the traffic lane or ramp. [...]

> **2520.320:** a) Any person may use the Tollway only as a transportation facility in accordance with the rules adopted by the Authority pursuant to its powers under Section 10 of the Toll Highway Act and shall not use the Tollway or the right-of-way for any other purposes.
> b) Any person using any part of the Tollway or right-of-way except as provided in this Part is guilty of a trespass.
> c) Any person who fails to immediately depart from Authority property after being directed to leave by the State Police or the Authority is subject to arrest and/or immediate removal from Authority property.

**What it means:** Drivers may not stop on the Tollway right-of-way to pick you up. Hanging around Tollway property can be treated as loitering or trespass. If State Police tell you to leave, leave.

### 92 Ill. Adm. Code 2520.281: Penalty for Violation (Tollway)

**Source:** [Cornell LII, Ill. Admin. Code tit. 92, § 2520.281](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-92-SS-2520.281) (unofficial mirror)

> Except when a different penalty is expressly provided, any person who violates any provision of this Subpart B shall be deemed guilty of a petty offense and, upon conviction, shall be subject to punishment as provided by law for petty offenses.

**What it means:** Soliciting rides on the Tollway is a petty offense, which is fine-only.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may ask for a ride from the shoulder or sidewalk; Illinois's "roadway" excludes the shoulder. Don't stand in travel lanes, ask for money or work, walk onto signed no-pedestrian expressways, or hitchhike on the Tollway.

> **625 ILCS 5/11-1006(a)**
> (a) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle.

> **625 ILCS 5/1-179**
> That portion of a highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder.

> **92 Ill. Adm. Code 2520.254**
> No person shall solicit or attempt to solicit rides from the driver of any vehicle using the Tollway.
