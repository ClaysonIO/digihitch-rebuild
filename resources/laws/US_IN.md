# Indiana (US-IN)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride (Ind. Code § 9-21-17-16). A rule from the Indiana Department of Transportation (INDOT) bans pedestrians from every Interstate highway in the state.

## Summary

Indiana follows the standard Uniform Vehicle Code pattern: "A person may not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride." The rule has an emergency exception. The "roadway" is the part of the highway "improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel," so the rule is aimed at people standing in the travel lanes. Unlike many states, the general Indiana definition does not say in so many words that the shoulder is excluded. That express exclusion appears only in a version of the definition used for one bicycle section. Still, a shoulder is not "ordinarily used for vehicular travel."

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Thumb from the shoulder, curb or sidewalk.
- **Never walk on an Interstate** (I-64, I-65, I-69, I-70, I-74, I-80, I-90, I-94, and the I-x64/x65/x69 spurs and loops). This is banned by INDOT rule 105 IAC 9-1-2, and it includes the Indiana Toll Road (I-80/I-90). Wait at the on-ramp entrance on the surface road, or at a truck stop.
- **Don't ask drivers on an Interstate ramp to stop for you.** Stopping or standing a vehicle is banned on Interstates "including ramp connections," except in rest areas (105 IAC 9-1-1).
- **Don't solicit money or work.** Standing on a highway to solicit employment or business is a separate offense (§ 9-21-17-17).

A violation of the pedestrian chapter is a Class C infraction, which is a civil fine and not a crime.

## Laws

### Ind. Code § 9-21-17-16: Solicitation of rides; restrictions; emergency

**Source:** [Indiana Code 2021, Title 9, as published by the Indiana Prosecuting Attorneys Council on in.gov (PDF)](https://www.in.gov/ipac/files/Title-9-2021.pdf) (2021 edition, IC 9-21-17-16; history note "As added by P.L.2-1991, SEC.9." with no later amendments). Cross-checked against [FindLaw's mirror of IC 9-21-17-16](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-9-motor-vehicles/in-code-sect-9-21-17-16/) (unofficial, "current as of January 01, 2026"), which shows the same operative language.

> Sec. 16. A person may not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from a person who drives a vehicle unless the person soliciting a ride is faced with an emergency on the roadway, in which case the person may secure a ride to obtain assistance.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the lanes cars drive in while asking for a ride. Standing on the shoulder or sidewalk isn't covered by this section. If you're stranded by an emergency, such as a breakdown, you may flag down a ride to get help.

### Ind. Code § 9-13-2-157: "Roadway"

**Source:** [Indiana Code 2021, Title 9 (in.gov PDF)](https://www.in.gov/ipac/files/Title-9-2021.pdf) (history note "As added by P.L.2-1991, SEC.1. Amended by P.L.42-2011, SEC.22.")

> Sec. 157. (a) Except as provided in subsection (b), "roadway" means that part of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel.
>
> (b) As used in IC 9-21-12-13, "roadway" means the part of a highway that is improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel. The term does not include the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder, even if the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder is used by persons riding bicycles or other human powered vehicles.

**What it means:** Subsection (a) is the definition that applies to the hitchhiking rule. It covers the part of the road "ordinarily used for vehicular travel," which in plain terms means the travel lanes and not the shoulder. The explicit "not the shoulder" sentence in (b) applies only to a bicycle-related section, so Indiana's rule is a little less clear-cut than in states that exclude the shoulder everywhere. Stand well off the pavement to be safe.

### Ind. Code § 9-13-2-73: "Highway or street"

**Source:** [Indiana Code 2021, Title 9 (in.gov PDF)](https://www.in.gov/ipac/files/Title-9-2021.pdf) (amended by P.L.198-2016, SEC.116)

> Sec. 73. "Highway" or "street" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every publicly maintained way when any part of the way is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel in Indiana. The term includes an alley in a city or town.

**What it means:** A "highway" is the whole public right-of-way, shoulders included. Compare the hitchhiking rule, which uses "roadway," with the solicitation rule below, which uses "highway."

### Ind. Code § 9-21-17-17: Solicitation of employment or business on highway

**Source:** [Indiana Code 2021, Title 9 (in.gov PDF)](https://www.in.gov/ipac/files/Title-9-2021.pdf) ("As added by P.L.2-1991, SEC.9.")

> Sec. 17. A person may not stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment or business from the occupant of a vehicle.

**What it means:** Asking passing drivers for work or business is banned anywhere in the right-of-way. A plain ride request is covered by § 16 instead, but don't add "will work for gas" to your sign.

### Ind. Code § 9-21-17-24: Violations; Class C infraction

**Source:** [Indiana Code 2021, Title 9 (in.gov PDF)](https://www.in.gov/ipac/files/Title-9-2021.pdf) ("As added by P.L.2-1991, SEC.9.")

> Sec. 24. A person who violates this chapter commits a Class C infraction.

**What it means:** Soliciting a ride from the roadway is an infraction, a civil violation that is not a crime. According to IC 34-28-5-4(c), as shown on [FindLaw's mirror](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-34-civil-law-and-procedure/in-code-sect-34-28-5-4/) ("current as of January 01, 2026"), a Class C infraction carries a judgment of up to $500.

### 105 Ind. Admin. Code 9-1-2: Pedestrians and certain vehicles prohibited on interstate highways

**Source:** [Cornell LII, 105 IAC 9-1-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/indiana/105-IAC-9-1-2) (unofficial mirror; INDOT rule, last readopted filed 10/4/2019, 20191030-IR-105190422RFA)

> Sec. 2. Pedestrians, motorized bicycles, bicycles, and other nonmotorized traffic shall be prohibited from enumerated highways: (1) I-64; (2) I-65; (3) I-69; (4) I-70; (5) I-74; (6) I-80; (7) I-90; (8) I-94; (9) I-164; (10) I-265; (11) I-275; (12) I-465; (13) I-469; and (14) I-865; otherwise known as the "Interstate Highway System".

**What it means:** Walking on any Indiana Interstate is banned, and that includes I-80/I-90, the Indiana Toll Road. The ban comes from an INDOT rule rather than a statute, but it covers every Interstate. Non-Interstate freeways and expressways aren't on this list.

### 105 Ind. Admin. Code 9-1-1: Stopping, standing, or parking prohibited on interstate highways

**Source:** [Cornell LII, 105 IAC 9-1-1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/indiana/105-IAC-9-1-1) (unofficial mirror; last readopted filed 10/4/2019)

> Sec. 1. Stopping, standing, or parking shall be prohibited on enumerated highways: (1) I-64; (2) I-65; (3) I-69; (4) I-70; (5) I-74; (6) I-80; (7) I-90; (8) I-94; (9) I-164; (10) I-265; (11) I-275; (12) I-465; (13) I-469; and (14) I-865; otherwise known as the "Interstate Highway System", including ramp connections, except in designated rest areas.

**What it means:** Drivers may not stop on an Interstate or its ramps except in rest areas. A driver who pulls over on a ramp to pick you up is breaking this rule, so wait where they can stop legally, before the ramp starts.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may not stand in the roadway (travel lanes) to solicit a ride, except in an emergency. Thumb from the shoulder, curb or sidewalk. Pedestrians are banned from all Indiana Interstates, including the Toll Road.
