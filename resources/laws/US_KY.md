# Kentucky (US-KY)

**Status:** Illegal on state-maintained roads (new in 2026). A law effective **July 15, 2026** bans anyone from standing or remaining stationary anywhere in a state-maintained right-of-way that isn't designated for pedestrians. That includes shoulders, ramps, medians and the grass beside the road. The older rule against standing in the roadway to solicit a ride still applies on every road. Pedestrians are banned from fully controlled access highways.

## Summary

For decades Kentucky followed the Uniform Vehicle Code pattern: "A person shall not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride" (KRS 189.570(19)). Because "roadway" excludes the shoulder, shoulder hitchhiking was lawful.

That changed in 2026. House Bill 189 (2026 Ky. Acts ch. 32) created **KRS 189.572**. It says a person "shall not sit, kneel, recline, stand, or remain physically stationary in any portion of a state-maintained right-of-way that is not designated for pedestrian use." The right-of-way expressly includes "paved and unpaved shoulders, medians, entrance and exit ramps, and adjacent grassy or drainage areas." The law does not mention hitchhiking. It was aimed at people standing beside busy roads. Standing on the shoulder of a state road to thumb a ride is squarely covered.

What this means for hitchhikers:

- **State-maintained roads** (interstates, parkways, U.S. and Kentucky numbered routes maintained by the Transportation Cabinet): no standing anywhere in the right-of-way, unless you are on a sidewalk, in a marked crosswalk or at a transit stop.
- **City and county streets** not maintained by the state: KRS 189.572 doesn't apply. The old roadway rule still does, so stay out of the travel lanes.
- **Sidewalks are exempt.** A sidewalk along a state route is a legal place to stand.
- **Interstates and parkways:** pedestrians are prohibited "within the right-of-way of a fully controlled access highway" by regulation.
- **Local exceptions:** cities and counties may create a permit process for limited exceptions.

Penalty: violations of KRS 189.570 through 189.590 carry a fine of $20 to $100 per offense (KRS 189.990(1)).

## Laws

### KRS 189.572: Prohibitions for pedestrians in state-maintained rights-of-way

**Source:** [Kentucky Legislature, KRS 189.572](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=57272) (official; "Effective: July 15, 2026. History: Created 2026 Ky. Acts ch. 32, sec. 1")

> (1) As used in this section:
> (a) "Local government" means any city, county, consolidated local government, urban-county government, charter county government, or unified local government; and
> (b) "State-maintained right-of-way" means any land, property, or infrastructure under the jurisdiction or maintenance of the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet that is used by the public for transportation purposes, including but not limited to travel lanes, paved and unpaved shoulders, medians, entrance and exit ramps, and adjacent grassy or drainage areas not designated for pedestrian use.
>
> (2) Except as provided in subsection (3) or (4) of this section, a person shall not sit, kneel, recline, stand, or remain physically stationary in any portion of a state-maintained right-of-way that is not designated for pedestrian use.
>
> (3) Subsection (2) of this section shall not apply to:
> (a) Individuals experiencing a medical emergency;
> (b) Individuals involved in a vehicle-related emergency, provided they remain in the immediate area of the vehicle;
> (c) Emergency personnel or public utility workers performing their official duties; or
> (d) Persons located within a pedestrian-designated area such as a sidewalk, marked crosswalk, or transit stop.
>
> (4) A local government may by ordinance authorize limited exceptions to subsection (2) of this section if: [...]

**What it means:** On any state-maintained road, you can't stand still on the shoulder, the ramp, the median or the grass beside the road. Walking along a road that isn't a controlled access highway is not covered; standing in one place to thumb is. Sidewalks, marked crosswalks and transit stops are exempt. Find a spot on private property with permission (a gas station, truck stop or rest-area parking lot), or a sidewalk on the way out of town.

### KRS 189.570: Pedestrians

**Source:** [Kentucky Legislature, KRS 189.570](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=57273) (official; "Effective: July 15, 2026. History: Amended 2026 Ky. Acts ch. 32, sec. 2")

> (12) Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (13) Where a sidewalk is not available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk only on a shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (14) Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, any pedestrian walking on or along a highway shall walk as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway, and, if on a two-way roadway shall walk only on the left side of the roadway.
>
> [...]
>
> (19) A person shall not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
>
> (20) A person shall not stand on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting employment or business from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> [...]
>
> (23) A person shall not occupy any portion of a state-maintained right-of-way in violation of KRS 189.572.

**What it means:** On every road, state or local, you may not stand in the travel lanes to solicit a ride. Subsection (23) was added in 2026 to tie this section to the new state right-of-way ban.

### KRS 189.990: Penalties

**Source:** [Kentucky Legislature, KRS 189.990](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=57417) (official; effective July 15, 2026)

> (1) (a) Any person who violates any of the provisions of KRS [...] 189.570 to 189.590, except 189.580(1)(b) or (6)(b), [...] shall be fined not less than twenty dollars ($20) nor more than one hundred dollars ($100) for each offense.

**What it means:** Standing in the roadway to hitch (KRS 189.570) and, by its section number, standing in a state right-of-way (KRS 189.572) each carry a fine of $20 to $100. Court costs are extra.

## History

### 2026 H.B. 189: state right-of-way standing ban

**Source:** [Kentucky General Assembly, 2026 Regular Session, House Bill 189](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/26rs/HB189.html); [2026 Ky. Acts ch. 32](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/acts/26RS/documents/0032.pdf)

H.B. 189 ("AN ACT relating to pedestrians") became law without the Governor's signature on April 5, 2026, as Acts Chapter 32. Its preamble cites "individuals remaining physically stationary in narrow medians, highway shoulders, and exit ramps" as a safety risk. It created KRS 189.572 and added subsection (23) to KRS 189.570. Both take effect July 15, 2026. Before that date, Kentucky was effectively a "restricted" state: hitchhiking from the shoulder was lawful, and only standing in the roadway was banned. Guides written before mid-2026 describe the old law.

- **Legislative history:** News coverage mentioned a proposed exemption for picketing that may have been withdrawn. The codified text quoted above has no such exemption.
