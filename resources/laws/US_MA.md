# Massachusetts (US-MA)

**Status:** Restricted. Massachusetts has no General Laws section on hitchhiking. MassDOT regulations ban it in two places: anywhere on the Mass Pike (Turnpike) and the Boston Metropolitan Highway System, and in the roadway of state highways. Hitchhiking from the shoulder of an ordinary state highway is not covered by these rules.

## Summary

Massachusetts regulates hitchhiking through MassDOT regulations, not a statute:

- **Turnpike and Metropolitan Highway System (700 CMR 7.00).** "No individual may engage in the solicitation of a ride, commonly known as 'hitchhiking', on a way." For these rules, "way" means the Turnpike, the Metropolitan Highway System (the Central Artery and the Boston tunnels), and their service and rest areas. Pedestrians are banned outright. Someone who displays a sign, signals a vehicle, or stands on Department property in view of a ramp or roadway to get a ride "may be arrested without a warrant." The fine is $50.
- **State highways (700 CMR 9.00).** "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride [...]". Here "roadway" excludes the shoulders.
- **Limited-access and express state highways (700 CMR 9.08).** Pedestrians are banned "where official signs have been erected at the approaches."

M.G.L. c. 85, § 17A, sometimes cited as a hitchhiking law, targets signaling vehicles to solicit alms or to sell merchandise or tickets. It does not mention rides.

In practice: stay off the Mass Pike entirely, ramps and service plazas included. Don't walk past "No Pedestrians" signs at interstate ramps. On other roads, stand on the shoulder or sidewalk.

In March 2024, MassDOT re-issued its state-highway rules as 700 CMR 9.00. Older guides cite the same text as 720 CMR 9.00 (see History).

## Laws

### 700 CMR 7.06(5) and (7): Turnpike and Metropolitan Highway System, limitations on use of ways

**Source:** [700 CMR 7.06, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/700-CMR-7-06) (unofficial mirror; amended eff. 10/21/2016, 2/8/2019 and 4/15/2022)

> (5) Pedestrians and Animals. No pedestrian may enter or use a way, and no person may allow a horse or other animal not properly contained in an appropriate vehicle to enter or use a way.
>
> (7) Hitchhiking and Loitering. No individual may engage in the solicitation of a ride, commonly known as "hitchhiking", on a way. An individual who, for the purpose of soliciting a ride on a way, displays a sign, signals a moving vehicle, causes the stopping of a vehicle, or stands on property of the Department in view of a ramp or roadway of a way, may be arrested without a warrant. No person may loiter in or about a way for the purpose of "hitchhiking" or for any other purpose.

**What it means:** On the Mass Pike (I-90) and the Boston Metropolitan Highway System, you may not walk or hitchhike at all. Standing on MassDOT land in view of a ramp with a sign can get you arrested, so stand on a local street or private property instead.

### 700 CMR 7.02: Definition of "Way" (for 700 CMR 7.00)

**Source:** [700 CMR 7.02, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/700-CMR-7-02) (unofficial mirror)

> Way is defined as the Turnpike, the Metropolitan Highway System, tunnels, a service or rest area, that is located on property owned or controlled and under the jurisdiction of the Department, and includes the areas appurtenant to said way that are necessary for the operation, construction and maintenance of the way.

> Metropolitan Highway System (MHS) refers to the highway system that consists of the Boston Extension, the Callahan Tunnel, the Central Artery, the Central Artery North Area, the Sumner Tunnel, and the Ted Williams Tunnel, as defined in M.G.L. c. 6C, § 1.

**What it means:** The Turnpike rules cover the Mass Pike, the Boston tunnels and Central Artery, and their service and rest areas. They do not cover every interstate in the state.

### 700 CMR 7.13: Penalties (Table 12)

**Source:** [700 CMR 7.13, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/700-CMR-7-13) (unofficial mirror; amended eff. 4/15/2022)

> (3) Fines. (a) Fines by Reference to 700 CMR 7.00. Whoever violates any provision of 700 CMR 7.00 is subject to a fine not exceeding the amount indicated in 700 CMR 7.13(3): Table 12, in addition to other penalties as the laws of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts or federal law may provide. [...]
>
> [Table 12, "Prohibited Entry to or Use of a Way: Non-vehicle":] 700 CMR 7.06(5) Pedestrians and animals $50 [...] 700 CMR 7.06(7) Hitchhiking or loitering $50

**What it means:** Walking or hitchhiking on the Pike carries a fine of up to $50. The no-warrant arrest power in 7.06(7) is a separate issue from the fine.

### 700 CMR 9.09(6): Pedestrians soliciting rides or business (state highways)

**Source:** [700 CMR 9.09, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/700-CMR-9-09) (unofficial mirror; adopted by Mass Register Issue 1516, eff. 3/1/2024)

> (6) Pedestrians Soliciting Rides or Business. No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment or business from the operator or occupant of any vehicle without the written permission of the board or officer having control of such roadway or highway.

**What it means:** On state highways, don't stand in the travel lanes to hitchhike. The shoulder is not "roadway" (see the definition below).

### 700 CMR 9.01: Definitions ("Roadway", "Highway", "Limited Access Highway")

**Source:** [700 CMR 9.01, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/700-CMR-9-01) (unofficial mirror; eff. 3/1/2024)

> Highway. The entire width between property lines of any state highway or lawful through way designated by the Department.
>
> Limited Access Highway. An express state highway with full control of access.
>
> Roadway. That portion of a highway between regularly established curb lines or that part, exclusive of shoulders, improved and intended to be used for vehicular traffic.

**What it means:** 700 CMR 9.00 governs state highways, and its "roadway" excludes shoulders.

### 700 CMR 9.08(1) and (2)(c): Limited access and express state highways

**Source:** [700 CMR 9.08, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/700-CMR-9-08) (unofficial mirror; eff. 3/1/2024)

> (1) Effect of 700 CMR 9.00. 700 CMR 9.08(2) shall be effective on all limited access State Highways and express state highways where official signs have been posted prohibiting bicycles, pedestrians and/or horsedrawn vehicles.
>
> (2) Limited Access and Express State Highway Regulations. [...] (c) Pedestrians. No person shall use any highway for pedestrian or foot traffic purposes except in emergency, where official signs have been erected at the approaches of said highway prohibiting such traffic.

**What it means:** Where a "No Pedestrians" sign is posted at the start of a limited-access highway or ramp, you may not walk past it except in an emergency.

### M.G.L. c. 85, § 17A: Soliciting from vehicles on public ways

**Source:** [Massachusetts Legislature, M.G.L. c. 85, § 17A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIV/Chapter85/Section17A)

> Section 17A. Whoever, for the purpose of soliciting any alms, contribution or subscription or of selling any merchandise, except newspapers, or ticket of admission to any game, show, exhibition, fair, ball, entertainment or public gathering, signals a moving vehicle on any public way or causes the stopping of a vehicle thereon, or accosts any occupant of a vehicle stopped thereon at the direction of a police officer or signal man, or of a signal or device for regulating traffic, shall be punished by a fine of not more than fifty dollars. [...]

**What it means:** This statute covers panhandling and street sales, not ride solicitation. It is included here because it is often misquoted as a hitchhiking ban.

## History

### 2024: 720 CMR 9.00 re-issued as 700 CMR 9.00

**Source:** [700 CMR 9.09, via Cornell LII (notes: "Adopted by Mass Register Issue 1516, eff. 3/1/2024")](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/700-CMR-9-09), compared with [720 CMR 9.09, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/720-CMR-9-09)

The ride-solicitation text of 700 CMR 9.09(6) is identical to that of the older 720 CMR 9.09(6), and so is the pedestrian rule in 9.08(2)(c). Older guides and Hitchwiki cite 720 CMR. The operative citation is now 700 CMR 9.00.

## Unverified / open questions

- **Penalty for 700 CMR 9.00 violations** (ride solicitation in a state-highway roadway, pedestrians past posted signs) was not confirmed. M.G.L. c. 85, § 2 authorizes penalties of up to $20 for rules on vehicle movements, and M.G.L. c. 90, § 18A sets $1 to $2 fines for pedestrian rules adopted under it. Which applies to 700 CMR 9.09 was not determined.
- **Whether every interstate outside the Turnpike is signed** "No Pedestrians" under 700 CMR 9.08 was not checked.
- **Municipal rules:** Cities and towns may adopt pedestrian rules (M.G.L. c. 90, § 18A). These were not researched.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** State rules do not ban hitchhiking from the shoulder or sidewalk. Don't stand in a state-highway roadway, and don't hitchhike or walk anywhere on the Mass Pike or Boston Metropolitan Highway System, or past posted "No Pedestrians" signs.

> **700 CMR 9.09(6)**
> (6) Pedestrians Soliciting Rides or Business. No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment or business from the operator or occupant of any vehicle without the written permission of the board or officer having control of such roadway or highway.
