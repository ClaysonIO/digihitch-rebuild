# Maryland (US-MD)

**Status:** Restricted. Maryland bans standing *in a roadway* to solicit a ride. "Roadway" is defined to exclude the shoulder, so the shoulder and sidewalk are outside this rule. Pedestrians are banned from controlled access highways, their ramps and their access roads.

## Summary

Maryland Transportation Article § 21-507(a) says a person "may not stand in a roadway to solicit a ride." Section 11-151 defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicle travel, "other than the shoulder." So the statewide hitchhiking rule covers the travel lanes, not the shoulder.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Stand on the shoulder or sidewalk, with room for a driver to pull fully off the road.
- **A stranded motorist may flag down help.** The ban expressly does not apply to "the occupant of a disabled vehicle who seeks the aid of another vehicle."
- **Interstates and other controlled access highways are off limits on foot, and so are their ramps and access roads** (§ 21-509). This is broader than many states: you can't stand on the on-ramp. Ask for rides on the local road before the ramp begins.
- **Walk on the left, facing traffic** where there is no sidewalk (§ 21-506).
- **Penalty:** A Maryland Vehicle Law violation is technically a misdemeanor with a fine of up to $500 (§ 27-101).

## Laws

### Md. Code, Transp. § 21-507: Solicitation of rides, business, or contributions

**Source:** [Maryland General Assembly, Transportation § 21-507](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=21-507&enactments=false)

> (a) Except for the occupant of a disabled vehicle who seeks the aid of another vehicle, a person may not stand in a roadway to solicit a ride, employment, or business from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (b) A person may not stand on or near a highway to solicit any other person to watch or guard any vehicle while it is parked or about to be parked on a highway.

**What it means:** You may not stand in the traffic lanes to hitchhike. The rule uses "roadway," which leaves out the shoulder (see § 11-151 below), so thumbing from the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered. If your own car has broken down, you may flag down help.

### Md. Code, Transp. §§ 11-127 and 11-151: Definitions of "Highway" and "Roadway"

**Source:** [Maryland General Assembly, Transportation § 11-127](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=11-127&enactments=false); [Transportation § 11-151](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=11-151&enactments=false)

> §11-127. "Highway" means: (1) The entire width between the boundary lines of any way or thoroughfare of which any part is used by the public for vehicular travel, whether or not the way or thoroughfare has been dedicated to the public and accepted by any proper authority; [...]

> §11-151. (a) "Roadway" means that part of a highway that is improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, other than the shoulder.

**What it means:** The "highway" is the whole public strip; the "roadway" is just the lanes cars drive in. The shoulder is expressly not part of the roadway.

### Md. Code, Transp. § 21-509: Pedestrians on controlled access highways

**Source:** [Maryland General Assembly, Transportation § 21-509](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=21-509&enactments=false)

> (a) Except as provided in subsection (j) of this section, a pedestrian may not walk along a controlled access highway.
>
> (b) Except as provided in subsection (j) of this section, a pedestrian may not walk on a controlled access highway.
>
> (c) Except as provided in subsection (j) of this section, a pedestrian may not walk along a ramp leading to or from a controlled access highway.
>
> (d) Except as provided in subsection (j) of this section, a pedestrian may not walk on a ramp leading to or from a controlled access highway.
>
> (e) Except as provided in subsection (j) of this section, a pedestrian may not walk along an access road leading to or from a controlled access highway.
>
> (f) Except as provided in subsection (j) of this section, a pedestrian may not walk on an access road leading to or from a controlled access highway.
>
> [...]
>
> (j) This section does not apply to a person if an emergency prevents the movement of a vehicle in which he is riding and the person goes only to the nearest telephone or other source of assistance, nor to a person boarding or leaving a bus within a bus stop approved by the State Highway Administration.

**What it means:** No walking on or along interstates and other controlled access highways, their ramps, or the access roads leading to them. The only exceptions are walking to get help when your vehicle is stuck, and getting on or off a bus at an approved stop. Get dropped off and picked up on surface streets, before the ramp.

### Md. Code, Transp. § 21-506: Pedestrians on roadways

**Source:** [Maryland General Assembly, Transportation § 21-506](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=21-506&enactments=false)

> (a) Where a sidewalk is provided, a pedestrian may not walk along and on an adjacent roadway.
>
> (b) Where a sidewalk is not provided, a pedestrian who walks along and on a highway may walk only on the left shoulder, if practicable, or on the left side of the roadway, as near as practicable to the edge of the roadway, facing any traffic that might approach from the opposite direction.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk. Without one, walk on the left shoulder facing oncoming traffic.

### Md. Code, Transp. § 27-101: Penalties

**Source:** [Maryland General Assembly, Transportation § 27-101](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gtr&section=27-101&enactments=false)

> (a) A person who violates a provision of the Maryland Vehicle Law is guilty of a misdemeanor unless the violation: (1) Is a felony under the Maryland Vehicle Law; or (2) Is punishable by a civil penalty under the applicable provision of the Maryland Vehicle Law.
>
> (b) Except as otherwise provided in the Maryland Vehicle Law, a person convicted of a misdemeanor for a violation of a provision of the Maryland Vehicle Law is subject to a fine not exceeding $500.

**What it means:** Violating § 21-507 or § 21-509 is legally a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $500. The statute quoted here sets only a fine, not jail, for these sections.

## Notable local rules

§ 21-507 itself contains county-specific subsections (c) through (j) for Calvert, Carroll, Charles, Harford, Washington, Prince George's, Anne Arundel, Allegany, Cecil, Montgomery, Baltimore, Howard and Frederick Counties. These deal with **soliciting money or donations** (and, in Anne Arundel, advertising), not rides. Two of them define "highway" very broadly (rights-of-way, shoulders, medians and more), for example Prince George's County:

> (d) (1) This subsection applies to Prince George's County. (2) (i) A person may not stand in a highway to solicit money or donations of any kind from the occupant of a vehicle. [...]

**What it means:** Holding a sign asking for money is restricted much more tightly in these counties than holding a sign asking for a ride. Don't combine the two (for example, asking for gas money from the roadside).
