# Maine (US-ME)

**Status:** Restricted. Hitchhiking is a traffic infraction on the traveled portion of a public way, anywhere on a limited-access highway (including the Maine Turnpike), and anywhere on a public way at night. In daytime, standing off the traveled portion of an ordinary road is lawful unless signs prohibit it.

## Summary

Maine has a dedicated statute, 29-A M.R.S. § 2110, "Hitchhiking forbidden". It defines "hitchhike" broadly ("by words, gestures or otherwise") and bans it in three situations:

1. **On the traveled portion of a public way.** The shoulder is not the traveled portion.
2. **On a limited-access highway, including the Maine Turnpike.** This applies to the whole highway, not only the travel lanes.
3. **On any portion of a public way during the nighttime.** Nighttime is any time outside "daytime", which runs from half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset.

In practice: hitchhike in daylight, from the shoulder of an ordinary road or from the local road before an interstate or turnpike entrance. Cities and towns may regulate or ban hitchhiking by ordinance, and MaineDOT may ban it at unsafe spots on state highways. Restricted areas must be signed.

The fine is a forfeiture of up to $50.

Pedestrians are not permitted anywhere on the Maine Turnpike (I-95 from Kittery to Augusta), its interchanges or its toll plazas under the Turnpike Authority's rules. Elsewhere, MaineDOT and municipalities may close a way to pedestrians by posting signs (§ 2052(5) and (5-A)).

## Laws

### 29-A M.R.S. § 2110: Hitchhiking forbidden

**Source:** [Maine Legislature, Office of the Revisor of Statutes, 29-A M.R.S. § 2110](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/29-A/title29-Asec2110.html) (data extracted 10/05/2026)

> 1. Definition. As used in this section, "hitchhike" means to endeavor by words, gestures or otherwise to beg, invite or secure transportation in a motor vehicle not engaged in carrying passengers for hire, unless the hitchhiker is known to the driver or a passenger.
>
> 2. Violation. A person commits a traffic infraction if that person hitchhikes on:
> A. The traveled portion of a public way;
> B. A limited access highway, including but not limited to the Maine Turnpike; or
> C. Any portion of a public way during the nighttime.

**What it means:** In daytime, stand on the shoulder, not in the lane. Never hitchhike on the Turnpike or on interstates, ramps included. After dark, you may not hitchhike from any part of a public way, so move to private property such as a gas station lot (with permission) or stop for the night.

> 3. Exception. This section does not prohibit solicitation of aid in the event of an accident or by persons who are sick or seeking assistance for the sick, if the sickness is bona fide and an emergency exists.
>
> 4. Regulation. A municipality may regulate or prohibit hitchhiking on a public way by ordinance. The Department of Transportation may regulate or prohibit hitchhiking on a state or state aid highway in the interest of safety at those locations where accidents may be a problem, limited visibility exists or severe traffic conflicts or other safety factors may occur.
>
> 5. Posting. An area in which hitchhiking has been regulated or prohibited must be clearly identified by posted signs.
>
> 6. Forfeitures. For a violation of subsection 2, a forfeiture not to exceed $50 may be adjudged.

**What it means:** Watch for "No Hitchhiking" signs, which mark local or MaineDOT bans. The penalty is a civil fine of up to $50.

### 29-A M.R.S. § 101: Definitions ("Daytime", "Nighttime", "Public way", "Way")

**Source:** [Maine Legislature, 29-A M.R.S. § 101](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/29-A/title29-Asec101.html)

> Daytime. "Daytime" means any time from 1/2 hour before sunrise to 1/2 hour after sunset.
>
> Nighttime. "Nighttime" means a time other than daytime.
>
> Public way. "Public way" means a way, owned and maintained by the State, a county or a municipality, over which the general public has a right to pass.
>
> Way. "Way" means the entire width between boundary lines of a road, highway, parkway, street or bridge used for vehicular traffic, whether public or private.

**What it means:** The night ban covers the "entire width" of the way, shoulders included, from half an hour after sunset to half an hour before sunrise.

### 29-A M.R.S. § 2052(5) and (5-A): Limiting use of a way to pedestrians

**Source:** [Maine Legislature, 29-A M.R.S. § 2052](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/29-A/title29-Asec2052.html)

> 5. Limiting use. The Department of Transportation or a municipality, with respect to a way under that authority's jurisdiction, may prohibit the use of a way by:
> A. Pedestrians; or
> B. Bicycles, roller skis or other nonmotorized traffic, scooters, motorized bicycles or tricycles or motorized scooters.
>
> 5-A. Limiting use sign restrictions. On limiting the use of a way, the authority shall erect and maintain official signs stating the prohibition. A person may not disobey the restrictions stated on those signs.

**What it means:** Where you see a "No Pedestrians" sign, for example at an interstate ramp, walking past it is an offense.

### 29-A M.R.S. § 2056(1)-(2): Pedestrians

**Source:** [Maine Legislature, 29-A M.R.S. § 2056](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/29-A/title29-Asec2056.html)

> 1. Pedestrian traffic. When use of a sidewalk next to a public way is practicable, a pedestrian may not walk on that public way.
>
> 2. Pedestrian on way. Where sidewalks are not provided, a pedestrian shall walk facing approaching traffic on the left side of the public way or the way's shoulder when practicable. [...]

**What it means:** Use sidewalks where they exist. Otherwise walk on the left, facing traffic.

### Maine Turnpike Authority Rules: Section 6 (Pedestrians) and Section 14 (Penalties)

**Source:** [Maine Turnpike Authority, Policies & Rules](https://www.maineturnpike.com/business-with-mta/policies-rules)

> Section 6 Pedestrians
> Pedestrians are not permitted on the Turnpike, on any Interchange or at any Toll Plaza. Solicitation of any kind, including but not limited to solicitation of rides, signatures or donations, passing of hand bills, displaying of signs, and attempts to sell merchandise are prohibited on Authority property. [...] Loitering on any Authority property is prohibited.

> Section 14 Penalties
> In accordance with 23 M.R.S.A. §1980, any violation of these rules shall constitute a traffic infraction, except that operation of a motor vehicle which exceeds, by 30 miles an hour or more, the speed fixed by the Authority, and failure to pay tolls, fares or charges for use of the Turnpike shall constitute Class E crimes.

**What it means:** The Turnpike (I-95 from Kittery to Augusta) is off-limits on foot, and soliciting rides is banned anywhere on Authority property, including service plazas. A violation is a traffic infraction.
