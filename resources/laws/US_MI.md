# Michigan (US-MI)

**Status:** Legal. The Michigan Vehicle Code has no statewide ban on soliciting rides. However, the state's model Uniform Traffic Code, which most cities, townships and villages adopt as a local ordinance, bans standing *in the roadway* to solicit a ride. Pedestrians are banned from limited-access highways.

## Summary

No section of the Michigan Vehicle Code (Act 300 of 1949) prohibits hitchhiking as such. The ride-soliciting rule lives in the **Uniform Traffic Code for Cities, Townships, and Villages** (Mich. Admin. Code R. 28.1001 et seq.), a model code written by the State Police. It applies only where a city, township or village has adopted it by reference under MCL 257.951. Rule 713 of that code says a person "shall not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride." "Roadway" takes its Vehicle Code meaning: the part of the highway "used for vehicular travel," which does not include the shoulder.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Never walk onto a freeway** (limited-access highway). MCL 257.679a bans pedestrians there.
- **In most towns, stay out of the travel lanes** when you thumb. Standing on the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered by Rule 713.
- **Don't block or impede traffic** with your body (MCL 257.676b).
- **Walk on the sidewalk where there is one.** Otherwise walk on the left, facing traffic (MCL 257.655).

All of these are civil infractions, not crimes. The fine for a Vehicle Code civil infraction is generally up to $100 plus costs (MCL 257.907).

## Laws

### Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.679a: Limited access highway; pedestrians and certain vehicles prohibited

**Source:** [Michigan Legislature, MCL 257.679a](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-679A.pdf) (Michigan Compiled Laws complete through PA 114 of 2026; last amended 1978 PA 510)

> (1) A person shall not operate a motorcycle with less than a 125 cubic centimeter engine, moped, farm tractor, or other self-propelled farm implement, nor shall a pedestrian, bicycle, except as provided in this section, or other nonmotorized traffic be permitted on a limited access highway in this state. Bicycles shall be permitted on paths constructed separately from the roadway and designated for the exclusive use of bicycles.
>
> (2) A person who violates this section is responsible for a civil infraction.

**What it means:** You may not walk on an interstate or other freeway in Michigan, shoulders included. Hitchhike from the surface road before the on-ramp, or from a gas station or truck stop near the interchange.

### Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.26: "Limited access highway" defined

**Source:** [Michigan Legislature, MCL 257.26](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-26.pdf) (unchanged since 1949)

> "Limited access highway" means every highway, street, or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same except at such points only, and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over such highway, street or roadway.

**What it means:** This covers freeways and expressways where you can only get on and off at interchanges.

### Mich. Admin. Code R. 28.1713: Pedestrians; soliciting ride, employment, or business in roadway prohibited (Uniform Traffic Code, Rule 713)

**Source:** [Cornell LII, Mich. Admin. Code R. 28.1713](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/michigan/Mich-Admin-Code-R-28-1713) (unofficial mirror; history note "1979 AC; 1981 AACS; 2002 AACS")

> (1) A person shall not stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, or business from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (2) A person who violates this rule is responsible for a civil infraction.

**What it means:** Where a city, township or village has adopted the Uniform Traffic Code, don't stand in the travel lanes asking for a ride. The shoulder, curb and sidewalk are not the "roadway" (see MCL 257.55 below), so thumbing from them is not covered by this rule.

### Mich. Admin. Code R. 28.1001: Definitions (Uniform Traffic Code)

**Source:** [Cornell LII, Mich. Admin. Code R. 28.1001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/michigan/Mich-Admin-Code-R-28-1001) (unofficial mirror; history note "1979 AC; 1981 AACS; 2002 AACS")

> (2) If any word or phrase used in this code is not defined in this code, but is defined in the Act, PA 300, MCL 257.1 to 257.923, then the definition in the act shall apply to the words and phrases used in this code.

**What it means:** The Uniform Traffic Code borrows the Vehicle Code's definition of "roadway."

### Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.55: "Roadway" defined

**Source:** [Michigan Legislature, MCL 257.55](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-55.pdf) (unchanged since 1949)

> "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel. In the event a highway includes 2 or more separate roadways, the term "roadway", as used herein, shall refer to any such roadway separately, but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The roadway is the part cars drive on. The definition doesn't name the shoulder, but a shoulder isn't "ordinarily used for vehicular travel," so standing on it is generally treated as outside the roadway.

### Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.951: Uniform traffic code; adoption by reference

**Source:** [Michigan Legislature, MCL 257.951](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-951.pdf) (Michigan Compiled Laws complete through PA 114 of 2026)

> (1) A city, township, or village may adopt by reference a code or ordinance for the regulation of traffic within cities, townships, and villages that has been promulgated by the director of the department of state police. [...]

**What it means:** Rule 713 is not automatically statewide. It applies inside a city, township or village that has adopted the Uniform Traffic Code, and most have. Assume it applies in town.

### Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.676b: Interference with normal flow of traffic prohibited

**Source:** [Michigan Legislature, MCL 257.676b](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-676B.pdf) (last amended 2021 PA 43)

> (1) Subject to subsection (2), a person, without authority, shall not block, obstruct, impede, or otherwise interfere with the normal flow of vehicular, streetcar, or pedestrian traffic upon a public street or highway in this state, by means of a barricade, object, or device, or with his or her person. [...]
>
> (2) Subsection (1) and any provision of the Michigan Administrative Code that prohibits a person from standing in a roadway other than a limited access highway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, or business from the occupant of any vehicle do not apply to a person who is soliciting contributions on behalf of a charitable or civic organization during daylight hours, if all of the following are satisfied: [...]
>
> (4) A person who violates this section is responsible for a civil infraction.

**What it means:** Don't step out to slow or stop cars. Subsection (2) confirms that the Legislature knows about the Administrative Code ban on standing in a roadway to solicit a ride, and it carves out only charity collectors.

### Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.655: Pedestrians on highways

**Source:** [Michigan Legislature, MCL 257.655](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-655.pdf) (last amended 1978 PA 510)

> (1) Where sidewalks are provided, a pedestrian shall not walk upon the main traveled portion of the highway. Where sidewalks are not provided, pedestrians shall, when practicable, walk on the left side of the highway facing traffic which passes nearest.
>
> (2) A person who violates this section is responsible for a civil infraction.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk. With no sidewalk, walk on the left side facing oncoming traffic.

### Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.907: Civil infraction fines

**Source:** [Michigan Legislature, MCL 257.907](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-257-907.pdf) (Michigan Compiled Laws complete through PA 114 of 2026)

> (a) Except as otherwise provided, for a civil infraction under this act or a local ordinance that substantially corresponds to a provision of this act, the person must be ordered to pay a civil fine of not more than $100.00.

**What it means:** Walking on a freeway or blocking traffic gets you a ticket of up to $100 plus court costs, not a criminal record.

## Unverified / open questions

- Rule 713 was quoted from Cornell LII, an unofficial mirror. The official Michigan Administrative Code text (Office of Administrative Hearings and Rules) was not retrieved in this session.
- Which municipalities have adopted the Uniform Traffic Code, and whether any have changed Rule 713 locally, was not checked. Detroit, for example, has its own traffic ordinance.
- Whether MCL 257.679a's ban covers on- and off-ramps isn't stated in the text. Ramps are normally part of the limited-access facility, so treat them as off-limits.
