# Minnesota (US-MN)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride from the driver of a private vehicle (Minn. Stat. § 169.22). Minnesota defines the roadway as "exclusive of the sidewalk or shoulder." Pedestrians may be banned from controlled-access highways by order of the transportation commissioner or by local ordinance, and the ban is enforced through posted signs.

## Summary

Minnesota's statute is literally titled "Hitchhiking; solicitation of business." It bans standing *in the roadway* to ask a private driver for a ride. Because "roadway" excludes the sidewalk and shoulder, hitchhiking from the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered. The one exception: where the state runs "dynamic shoulder lanes" (shoulders opened to traffic at peak times), the shoulder counts as roadway while it is open to traffic.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand on the shoulder or sidewalk, not in a lane.** Avoid shoulders that are signed as part-time travel lanes, mostly on Twin Cities freeways.
- **Obey "no pedestrians" signs** on freeways and other controlled-access highways (§ 169.305). Wait at the ramp entrance on the surface street.
- **Don't solicit money, work or business** from the roadway (§ 169.22, subd. 2).

A violation is normally a petty misdemeanor, punishable by a fine of up to $300 with no jail. It can become a misdemeanor if you endanger people or property, or after two or more petty misdemeanor convictions in the past 12 months.

## Laws

### Minn. Stat. § 169.22: Hitchhiking; solicitation of business

**Source:** [Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minn. Stat. § 169.22](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/169.22) (2026 Minnesota Statutes; history: 1937 c 464 s 56; 1974 c 379 s 3)

> Subdivision 1. Soliciting ride.
> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle.
>
> Subd. 2. Soliciting employment, business, or contributions.
> No person shall stand on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes asking for a ride. Thumbing from the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered by this section. Subdivision 2 also uses "roadway," so asking for money or work is likewise limited only in the lanes, though it's still smart to keep money requests off your sign.

### Minn. Stat. § 169.011: Definitions

**Source:** [Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minn. Stat. § 169.011](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/169.011) (2026 Minnesota Statutes)

> Subd. 68. Roadway.
> "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the sidewalk or shoulder. During periods when the commissioner allows the use of dynamic shoulder lanes as defined in subdivision 25, roadway includes that shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" as used herein shall refer to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.
>
> Subd. 74. Shoulder.
> "Shoulder" means that part of a highway which is contiguous to the regularly traveled portion of the highway and is on the same level as the highway. The shoulder may be pavement, gravel, or earth.
>
> Subd. 19. Controlled-access highway.
> "Controlled-access highway" means, in this chapter, every highway, street, or roadway in respect to which the right of access of the owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons has been acquired and to which the owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over such highway, street or roadway.

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway, so standing there to hitchhike doesn't violate § 169.22. The exception is a shoulder that is open as a "dynamic shoulder lane." While it carries traffic, it is roadway.

### Minn. Stat. § 169.305: Controlled-access rules and penalties

**Source:** [Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minn. Stat. § 169.305](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/169.305) (2026 Minnesota Statutes; last amended 2022 c 55 art 1 s 63)

> (c) The commissioner of transportation may by order, and any public authority may by ordinance, with respect to any controlled-access highway under their jurisdictions prohibit or regulate the use of any such highway by pedestrians, bicycles, or other nonmotorized traffic, or by motorized bicycles, or by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe flow of traffic.
>
> (d) The commissioner of transportation or the public authority adopting any such prohibitory rules shall erect and maintain official signs on the controlled-access highway on which such rules are applicable and when so erected no person shall disobey the restrictions stated on such signs.
>
> [...]
>
> Subd. 3. Petty misdemeanor.
> Any person who violates the provisions of this section or any order or ordinance promulgated or enacted by the commissioner of transportation or a public authority pursuant thereto is guilty of a petty misdemeanor.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians from every freeway automatically. The ban applies where the state or a local authority has prohibited pedestrians and posted signs. Interstate ramps are normally signed, so don't walk past the "no pedestrians" sign. Breaking a posted ban is a petty misdemeanor.

### Minn. Stat. § 169.89: Penalties

**Source:** [Minnesota Office of the Revisor of Statutes, Minn. Stat. § 169.89](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/169.89) (2026 Minnesota Statutes)

> Subdivision 1. Violation; when petty misdemeanor enhanced to misdemeanor.
> Unless otherwise declared in this chapter with respect to particular offenses, it is a petty misdemeanor for any person to do any act forbidden or fail to perform any act required by this chapter; except that:
> (1) a violation which is committed in a manner or under circumstances so as to endanger or be likely to endanger any person or property; or
> (2) exclusive of violations relating to the standing or parking of an unattended vehicle, a violation of any of the provisions of this chapter, classified therein as a petty misdemeanor, when preceded by two or more petty misdemeanor convictions within the immediate preceding 12-month period;
> is a misdemeanor to which the provisions of subdivision 2 shall not apply.
>
> Subd. 2. Petty misdemeanor penalty; no jury trial.
> A person charged with a petty misdemeanor is not entitled to a jury trial but shall be tried by a judge without a jury. If convicted, the person is not subject to imprisonment but shall be punished by a fine of not more than $300.

**What it means:** Hitchhiking from the roadway is a petty misdemeanor with a fine of up to $300 and no jail. If you do it in a way that endangers people, such as stepping into fast traffic, or if you have two or more recent petty misdemeanor convictions, it becomes a misdemeanor.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may ask for a ride from the shoulder or sidewalk; Minnesota's "roadway" excludes them. Don't stand in travel lanes or on shoulders opened to traffic, and obey "no pedestrians" signs on freeways.

> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle.
