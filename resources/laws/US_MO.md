# Missouri (US-MO)

**Status:** Legal. No Missouri statute that we could find prohibits soliciting a ride. General pedestrian rules apply, and cities may add their own ordinances. We found no statute expressly banning pedestrians from interstates.

## Summary

Missouri's traffic laws (Chapters 300 and 304 of the Revised Statutes of Missouri) contain no "soliciting a ride" section of the Uniform Vehicle Code type. We searched the section titles of both chapters, the Model Traffic Ordinance, and the revisor's site for "solicit" and "hitchhik" and found nothing. As far as state law goes, standing by the road with your thumb out is not specifically prohibited.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Walk on the sidewalk if there is one.** Otherwise walk on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder, facing traffic (§ 300.405). This rule is part of the Model Traffic Ordinance, which applies in cities that adopt it.
- **Stay off the travel lanes.** Missouri defines "roadway" as excluding the berm and shoulder, so the shoulder is the place to stand.
- **Interstates:** No statute or Missouri Highways and Transportation Commission rule banning pedestrians from interstates was found. That doesn't make interstates a good idea. Expect troopers to move you along, and city ordinances may ban it. Hitchhike from on-ramps on the surface road or from truck stops.
- **Check city ordinances.** Cities such as St. Louis and Kansas City have their own traffic codes. These were not checked here.

## Laws

### Mo. Rev. Stat. § 300.405: Pedestrians walking along roadways (Model Traffic Ordinance)

**Source:** [Missouri Revisor of Statutes, RSMo § 300.405](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=300.405) (effective 28 Aug 1965; L. 1965 p. 445 § 88)

> 1. Where sidewalks are provided it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
> 2. Where sidewalks are not provided any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall when practicable walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic which may approach from the opposite direction.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one. Otherwise walk on the left side, on the shoulder if possible, facing oncoming traffic. Nothing here forbids standing on the shoulder asking for a ride.

### Mo. Rev. Stat. § 300.010: Definitions (Model Traffic Ordinance)

**Source:** [Missouri Revisor of Statutes, RSMo § 300.010](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=300.010) (effective 28 Aug 2021)

> (7) "Controlled access highway", every highway, street or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over the highway, street or roadway;
>
> [...]
>
> (31) "Roadway", that portion of a highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways the term "roadway" as used herein shall refer to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively;

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway. In cities that use the Model Traffic Ordinance, the roadway rules apply to the travel lanes only.

### Mo. Rev. Stat. § 300.600: Cities may adopt ordinance by reference

**Source:** [Missouri Revisor of Statutes, RSMo § 300.600](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=300.600) (effective 28 Aug 1965)

> This ordinance or any designated part thereof shall become effective in any city of the state upon the adoption thereof by the legislative body of the city by an ordinance appropriately describing this ordinance or the part thereof adopted. [...]

**What it means:** Chapter 300 is a model code. Its rules, including § 300.405, apply only in cities that have adopted it, and a city may have its own different rules.

### Mo. Rev. Stat. § 304.001: Definitions for chapter 304 and chapter 307

**Source:** [Missouri Revisor of Statutes, RSMo § 304.001](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=304.001) (effective 28 Aug 2021; A.L. 2021 S.B. 176)

> (6) "Freeway", a divided state highway with four or more lanes, with no access to the throughways except the established interchanges and with no at-grade crossings;
> (7) "Interstate highway", a state highway included in the national system of interstate highways located within the boundaries of Missouri, [...]
> [...]
> (11) "Right-of-way", the entire width of land between the boundary lines of a state highway, including any roadway;
> (12) "Roadway", that portion of a state highway ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder;

**What it means:** On state highways, too, the "roadway" excludes the shoulder. Stand on the shoulder, not in a lane.

## Unverified / open questions

- **No statewide hitchhiking statute was found.** This conclusion rests on searching the Chapter 300 and 304 section lists on revisor.mo.gov and on web searches of the revisor's site for "soliciting a ride," which found nothing. A provision in another chapter could have been missed.
- **Interstates:** No Missouri statute or Code of State Regulations rule (Title 7, MoDOT) that bans pedestrians from interstates or freeways was found. RSMo § 304.011.2 sets a 40 mph minimum speed on interstates, but it applies to vehicles, not pedestrians. Whether MoDOT posts "no pedestrians" signs under some other authority, and what enforces them, is unverified.
- **Local ordinances** (St. Louis, Kansas City, Springfield, Columbia) were not checked. Many cities copy the Uniform Vehicle Code ban on standing in the roadway to solicit a ride.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** Hitchhiking is legal. No Missouri statute was found that bans soliciting a ride. Stand on the shoulder, off the travel lanes, and check local ordinances. No statute banning pedestrians from interstates was found, but avoid them.
