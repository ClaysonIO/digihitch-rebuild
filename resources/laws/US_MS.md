# Mississippi (US-MS)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in a roadway to solicit a ride from the driver of a private vehicle. Mississippi Department of Transportation (MDOT) rules ban pedestrians and "hitchhikers" by name from fully controlled access highways.

## Summary

Mississippi Code § 63-3-1109 reads: "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle."

One wrinkle: Mississippi's definition of "roadway" (§ 63-3-125(c)) does **not** expressly exclude the shoulder. Most states' definitions add "exclusive of the berm or shoulder"; Mississippi's does not. The definition still limits "roadway" to the part "improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel," and a shoulder is not ordinarily used for travel. Standing well off the travel lanes is the safest reading.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **No interstates or other fully controlled access highways.** An MDOT rule says "The use of any fully controlled access facility by pedestrians, bicycles, hitchhikers [...] is prohibited." Hitchhike from the surface road or a gas station before the on-ramp.
- **Stand clear of the travel lanes** on ordinary highways.

A violation of § 63-3-1109 is a misdemeanor. A first conviction carries a fine of up to $100 or up to 10 days in jail (§ 63-9-11).

## Laws

### Miss. Code Ann. § 63-3-1109: Soliciting rides

**Source:** [FindLaw, Mississippi Code § 63-3-1109](https://codes.findlaw.com/ms/title-63-motor-vehicles-and-traffic-regulations/ms-code-sect-63-3-1109.html) (unofficial mirror; FindLaw's Title 63 pages are marked "Current as of January 01, 2025"). Mississippi's official code is published by LexisNexis and was not retrieved.

> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any private vehicle.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes to hitch. The rule says nothing about the shoulder, the sidewalk or the area beyond the road edge.

### Miss. Code Ann. § 63-3-125: Definitions ("street or highway," "roadway")

**Source:** [FindLaw, Mississippi Code § 63-3-125](https://codes.findlaw.com/ms/title-63-motor-vehicles-and-traffic-regulations/ms-code-sect-63-3-125.html) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of January 01, 2025")

> (a) "Street or highway" means the entire width between property lines of every way or place of whatever nature when any part thereof is open to the use of the public, as a matter of right, for purposes of vehicular traffic.
>
> [...]
>
> (c) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel.
>
> (d) "Sidewalk" means that portion of a street between curb lines, or the lateral lines of a roadway, and the adjacent property lines intended for the use of pedestrians.

**What it means:** Unlike most states, Mississippi doesn't say in so many words that the shoulder is excluded from the "roadway." A paved shoulder is still not "ordinarily used for vehicular travel," so it is arguably outside the roadway. To be safe, stand on the grass or a pull-off beyond the shoulder edge.

### Mississippi Administrative Code, Title 37, Part 1, Rule 7501-09001-311: Fully controlled access facilities

**Source:** [Cornell LII, 37 Miss. Code. R. § 1-7501-09001-311](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/mississippi/37-Miss-Code-R-SS-1-7501-09001-311) (unofficial mirror of MDOT's "Rules, Regulations and Ordinances Governing Use of State Highways of Mississippi," Subpart 7501 Maintenance, Chapter 09001)

> The use of any fully controlled access facility by pedestrians, bicycles, hitchhikers, ridden or herded animals and animal drawn vehicles is prohibited. The use of any such fully controlled access facility by motor bicycles, non-motorized vehicles or any other vehicle unable to comply with the posted minimum speed limit is also prohibited. It shall be unlawful for any person to fish from any bridge, culvert or other structure on a state highway.

**What it means:** Interstates and other fully controlled access highways are off limits to hitchhikers, by name. This is one of the few rules in the country that uses the word "hitchhikers." Wait at the bottom of the ramp, on the local road.

### Miss. Code Ann. § 63-9-11: Penalties

**Source:** [FindLaw, Mississippi Code § 63-9-11](https://codes.findlaw.com/ms/title-63-motor-vehicles-and-traffic-regulations/ms-code-sect-63-9-11.html) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of January 01, 2025")

> (1) It is a misdemeanor for any person to violate any of the provisions of Chapter 3, 5 or 7 of this title, unless such violation is by such chapters or other law of this state declared to be a felony.
>
> (2) Every person convicted of a misdemeanor for a violation of any of the provisions of such chapters for which another penalty is not provided shall for first conviction thereof be punished by a fine of not more than One Hundred Dollars ($100.00) or by imprisonment for not more than ten (10) days; for a second such conviction within one (1) year thereafter such person shall be punished by a fine of not more than Two Hundred Dollars ($200.00) or by imprisonment for not more than twenty (20) days or by both such fine and imprisonment; upon a third or subsequent conviction within one (1) year after the first conviction such person shall be punished by a fine of not more than Five Hundred Dollars ($500.00) or by imprisonment for not more than six (6) months or by both such fine and imprisonment.

**What it means:** A first offense of soliciting a ride from the roadway (Title 63, Chapter 3) carries up to $100 or 10 days in jail. Repeat offenses within a year cost more.
