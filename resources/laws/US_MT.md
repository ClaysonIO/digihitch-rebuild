# Montana (US-MT)

**Status:** Restricted. "A person may not stand on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride." Montana defines the roadway as excluding the berm or shoulder, so hitchhiking from the shoulder is not prohibited by state law. Restrictions on pedestrians on interstates and other controlled-access highways come from the Montana Department of Transportation and are shown by signs.

## Summary

Montana follows the standard Uniform Vehicle Code pattern. You can't stand in the travel lanes to solicit a ride, but the shoulder isn't part of the roadway.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand on the shoulder, not in the lane.**
- **Walking:** use the sidewalk where one is provided. Otherwise walk on the shoulder, as far from the roadway edge as practicable.
- **Interstates and other controlled-access highways:** the Montana Department of Transportation (by rule) and local authorities (by ordinance) may prohibit any class of traffic that is incompatible with normal, safe traffic flow, and must post signs. Obey any "pedestrians prohibited" signs.
- **Penalty:** a misdemeanor punished only by a fine. It is $10 to $100 for a first offense, rising for repeat offenses within a year.

## Laws

### Mont. Code Ann. § 61-8-507: Pedestrian soliciting rides, business, or contributions

**Source:** [Montana Legislature, MCA 61-8-507](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0610/chapter_0080/part_0050/section_0070/0610-0080-0050-0070.html) (Montana Code Annotated 2025; history: En. Sec. 79, Ch. 263, L. 1955; amd. Sec. 6, Ch. 374, L. 2003)

> (1) A person may not stand on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
>
> (2) A person may not stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of a vehicle unless the solicitation is authorized by the proper jurisdictional authority.

**What it means:** Subsection (1), about rides, uses "roadway", the travel lanes. Subsection (2), about business and contributions, uses the broader "highway". Because (1) says "roadway", a hitchhiker on the shoulder is not covered by it.

### Mont. Code Ann. § 61-1-101: "Roadway" and "Highway" defined

**Source:** [Montana Legislature, MCA 61-1-101](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0610/chapter_0010/part_0010/section_0010/0610-0010-0010-0010.html) (Montana Code Annotated 2025)

> (26) "Highway" or "public highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every publicly maintained way when any part of the publicly maintained way is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel.

> (66) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event that a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term refers to any roadway separately but not to all roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder is part of the highway but not the roadway. You may hitch from the shoulder. Stay out of the lanes.

### Mont. Code Ann. § 61-8-506: Pedestrians on roadways and highways

**Source:** [Montana Legislature, MCA 61-8-506](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0610/chapter_0080/part_0050/section_0060/0610-0080-0050-0060.html) (Montana Code Annotated 2025; history: En. Sec. 78, Ch. 263, L. 1955; amd. 2003, 2005)

> (1) Where sidewalks are provided and their use is practicable, a pedestrian may not walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (2) Where sidewalks are not provided, a pedestrian, other than an intoxicated pedestrian referred to in 61-8-508, who is walking along and upon a highway may walk only on the shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one. Otherwise stay on the shoulder, as far from the traffic lane as you reasonably can.

### Mont. Code Ann. § 61-8-332: Restrictions on use of controlled-access roadway

**Source:** [Montana Legislature, MCA 61-8-332](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0610/chapter_0080/part_0030/section_0320/0610-0080-0030-0320.html) (Montana Code Annotated 2025; history: En. Sec. 60, Ch. 263, L. 1955; amd. 1974, 1991; amd. Sec. 16, Ch. 352, L. 2003)

> (1) The department of transportation may by rule and local authorities may by ordinance regulate or prohibit the use of a controlled-access highway under their respective jurisdictions by any class or kind of traffic that is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic or by any vehicle.
>
> (2) The department or the local authority that adopts the prohibitory regulation shall erect and maintain official traffic control devices on the controlled-access highway on which these regulations are applicable. A person may not violate the restrictions stated on the official traffic control devices.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians from all interstates. Any ban comes from a Transportation Department rule or local ordinance and is posted on signs. If the on-ramp says pedestrians are prohibited, wait before the sign.

### Mont. Code Ann. § 61-8-711: Penalty

**Source:** [Montana Legislature, MCA 61-8-711](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0610/chapter_0080/part_0070/section_0110/0610-0080-0070-0110.html) (Montana Code Annotated 2025)

> (1) It is a misdemeanor for a person to violate any of the provisions of this chapter unless the violation is declared to be a felony.
>
> (2) Each person convicted of a misdemeanor for a violation of any of the provisions of this chapter for which another penalty is not provided shall for a first conviction be punished by a fine of not less than $10 or more than $100. For a second conviction within 1 year after the first conviction, the person shall be punished by a fine of not less than $25 or more than $200. Upon a third or subsequent conviction within 1 year after the first conviction, the person shall be punished by a fine of not less than $50 or more than $500.

**What it means:** Standing in the lane to hitch is technically a misdemeanor, but the penalty is a fine only: $10 to $100 the first time.

## History

### 2003: hitchhiking split from business solicitation; freeway pedestrian language generalized

**Source:** [Montana Code Annotated 2001, § 61-8-507](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/2001/mca/61/8/61-8-507.htm) and [MCA 1997, § 61-8-332](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/1997/mca/61/8/61-8-332.htm) (official Montana Legislature archive)

Before the 2003 amendment (Ch. 374, L. 2003), § 61-8-507 read: "(1) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, or business from the occupant of any vehicle." The 2003 rewrite kept the roadway-only rule for rides. Business and contributions moved into a separate subsection that applies to the whole highway.

Before 2003, § 61-8-332(1) said the department "may by rule and local authorities may by ordinance prohibit the use of a controlled-access highway under their respective jurisdictions by pedestrians, bicycles, or other nonmotorized traffic or by a person operating a motor-driven cycle." The current text refers to "any class or kind of traffic" instead.

**What it means:** The rule for hitchhikers has been essentially the same since 1955: stay out of the roadway.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** Hitchhiking from the shoulder is not prohibited by state law. Montana bans standing on the roadway (travel lanes) to solicit a ride, and the roadway excludes the shoulder. Obey any signs banning pedestrians on interstates.
