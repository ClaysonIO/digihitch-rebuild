# North Carolina (US-NC)

**Status:** Restricted. On ordinary State highways, North Carolina law expressly lets you stand on the **shoulder** to solicit a ride; only the rest of the highway is off limits. On interstates and other controlled-access highways, hitchhiking is banned outright by a Department of Transportation ordinance, and so is a driver stopping to pick you up. Breaking that ordinance is a Class 1 misdemeanor.

## Summary

North Carolina has two separate rules:

1. **G.S. § 20-175(a), all State highways:** "No person shall stand in any portion of the State highways, except upon the shoulders thereof, for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any motor vehicle." This is unusually clear. The shoulder is the legal place to hitchhike, and the travel lanes and median are not.
2. **19A NCAC 02E .0410, interstates and controlled-access highways:** it is unlawful "to hitchhike or to solicit rides, or for the driver of any vehicle to stop for the purpose of picking up one who is hitchhiking or soliciting a ride, on any interstate or controlled access highway." The only exception is for occupants of a broken-down vehicle and the people who stop to help them. Under G.S. § 136-18(5), violating a DOT ordinance is a Class 1 misdemeanor, which is far more serious than an ordinary pedestrian ticket.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **On regular State roads, stand on the shoulder.** That is expressly allowed by § 20-175(a).
- **Never hitchhike on an interstate or controlled-access highway, including its shoulder.** Wait on the surface road before the on-ramp. A driver who stops for you there is also breaking the rule.
- **Don't impede traffic.** Willfully standing on the highway or street "in such a manner as to impede the regular flow of traffic" is a separate Class 2 misdemeanor (§ 20-174.1).
- **Walk on the sidewalk** if there is one; otherwise on the extreme left of the roadway or its shoulder, facing traffic (§ 20-174(d)).
- **Penalty for § 20-175:** it is in Part 11 (pedestrians) of the motor vehicle article, so a violation is an infraction (a non-criminal offense) with a penalty of up to $100 (§ 20-176).

## Laws

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-175: Pedestrians soliciting rides, employment, business or funds upon highways or streets

**Source:** [North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. § 20-175](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-175.html)

> (a) No person shall stand in any portion of the State highways, except upon the shoulders thereof, for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any motor vehicle.
>
> (b) No person shall stand or loiter in the main traveled portion, including the shoulders and median, of any State highway or street, excluding sidewalks, or stop any motor vehicle for the purpose of soliciting employment, business or contributions from the driver or occupant of any motor vehicle that impedes the normal movement of traffic on the public highways or streets: [...]
>
> (c) Repealed by Session Laws 1973, c. 1330, s. 39.
>
> (d) Local governments may enact ordinances restricting or prohibiting a person from standing on any street, highway, or right-of-way excluding sidewalks while soliciting, or attempting to solicit, any employment, business, or contributions from the driver or occupants of any vehicle. [...]

**What it means:** On a State highway you may hitchhike from the shoulder, but not from a travel lane, the median or anywhere else in the right-of-way. Note that the local-ordinance power in (d) covers soliciting employment, business or contributions, not rides. Don't ask for gas money from the roadside, because that falls under the stricter rules in (b) and (d).

### 19A NCAC 02E .0410: Hitchhiking on interstate or controlled access highways

**Source:** [North Carolina Office of Administrative Hearings, 19A NCAC 02E .0410 (PDF)](http://reports.oah.state.nc.us/ncac/title%2019a%20-%20transportation/chapter%2002%20-%20division%20of%20highways/subchapter%20e/19a%20ncac%2002e%20.0410.pdf) (readopted effective July 1, 2019)

> (a) It shall be unlawful for any person to hitchhike or to solicit rides, or for the driver of any vehicle to stop for the purpose of picking up one who is hitchhiking or soliciting a ride, on any interstate or controlled access highway.
>
> (b) This Rule shall not shall prohibit an operator or passengers in a vehicle stopped on a controlled access facility by reason of any emergency, mechanical failure, or other failure of the vehicle to operate, from requesting aid or soliciting a ride, nor does it prohibit the operator of any other vehicle from stopping to render aid or assistance and giving rides in such situations.

**What it means:** Interstates and other controlled-access highways are completely off limits for hitchhiking, shoulder and ramps included. Drivers can be charged for stopping to pick you up. The exception is if your own vehicle broke down there: then you may ask for help and others may give you a ride. ("not shall prohibit" is the rule's own wording.)

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 136-18(5): DOT ordinances; violation is a Class 1 misdemeanor

**Source:** [North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. § 136-18](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_136/GS_136-18.html)

> (5) To make rules, regulations, and ordinances for the use of, and to police traffic on, the State highways, and to prevent their abuse by individuals, corporations, and public corporations, [...] or by any other means whatsoever, and to provide ample means for the enforcement of the rules, regulations, and ordinances. The violation of any of the rules, regulations, or ordinances so prescribed by the Department of Transportation constitutes a Class 1 misdemeanor. [...]

**What it means:** Hitchhiking on an interstate is a crime, not just a ticket. See the sentencing section below for what a Class 1 misdemeanor can mean.

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-1340.23: Misdemeanor punishment limits

**Source:** [North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. § 15A-1340.23](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_15A/GS_15A-1340.23.html)

> (b) Fines. - [...] Unless otherwise provided for a specific offense, the maximum fine that may be imposed is two hundred dollars ($200.00) for a Class 3 misdemeanor and one thousand dollars ($1,000) for a Class 2 misdemeanor. The amount of the fine for a Class 1 misdemeanor and a Class A1 misdemeanor is in the discretion of the court.

From the punishment chart (offense class, then Level I "No Prior Convictions", Level II "One to Four Prior Convictions", Level III "Five or More Prior Convictions"):

> 1 1-45 days C 1-45 days C/I/A 1-120 days C/I/A
>
> 2 1-30 days C 1-45 days C/I 1-60 days C/I/A

**What it means:** For a first-time Class 1 misdemeanor (interstate hitchhiking), the sentence range is 1 to 45 days of community punishment ("C"), and the fine is up to the judge. With prior convictions, active jail time ("A") becomes possible. Class 2 (impeding traffic under § 20-174.1) carries a fine of up to $1,000.

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-174.1: Standing, sitting or lying upon highways or streets prohibited

**Source:** [North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. § 20-174.1](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-174.1.html)

> (a) A person who willfully stands, sits, or lies upon the highway or street in such a manner as to impede the regular flow of traffic is guilty of a Class 2 misdemeanor.
>
> (b) Repealed by Session Laws 2024-16, s. 4(a), effective December 1, 2024, [...]

**What it means:** Standing where cars have to slow or swerve around you is a crime on any road. Stay well back on the shoulder.

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-4.01: Definitions ("Highway", "Roadway")

**Source:** [North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. § 20-4.01](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-4.01.html)

> Highway. - The entire width between property or right-of-way lines of every way or place of whatever nature, when any part thereof is open to the use of the public as a matter of right for the purposes of vehicular traffic. The terms "highway" and "street" and their cognates are synonymous.
>
> [...]
>
> Roadway. - That portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the shoulder. [...]

**What it means:** The highway is the whole right-of-way, from line to line. § 20-175(a) bans hitchhiking anywhere on that highway except the shoulder. So the grassy area beyond the shoulder, but still inside the right-of-way, is technically off limits too.

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-174(d): Walking along highway

**Source:** [North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. § 20-174](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-174.html)

> (d) Where sidewalks are provided, it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway. Where sidewalks are not provided, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the extreme left of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic which may approach from the opposite direction. Such pedestrian shall yield the right-of-way to approaching traffic.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk. Without one, walk on the left edge or left shoulder facing traffic.

### N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-176: Penalty for misdemeanor or infraction

**Source:** [North Carolina General Assembly, G.S. § 20-176](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-176.html)

> (a) Violation of a provision of Part 9, 10, 10A, or 11 of this Article is an infraction unless the violation is specifically declared by law to be a misdemeanor or felony. [...]
>
> (b) Unless a specific penalty is otherwise provided by law, a person found responsible for an infraction contained in this Article may be ordered to pay a penalty of not more than one hundred dollars ($100.00).

**What it means:** § 20-175 sits in Part 11 ("Pedestrians' Rights and Duties"), so hitchhiking from the wrong part of a State highway is an infraction with a penalty of up to $100. The interstate ordinance is the exception, as a Class 1 misdemeanor under § 136-18(5).

## History

### 2024: § 20-174.1 amended

**Source:** [G.S. § 20-174.1](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-174.1.html)

The official history line for § 20-174.1 lists Session Law 2024-16, s. 4(a), which among other things repealed subsection (b) effective December 1, 2024. The current text, including the Class 2 misdemeanor in subsection (a) and the enhanced penalties for demonstrations and for obstructing emergency vehicles in subsections (c) and (d), is what applies now.

## Unverified / open questions

- **Pedestrians on freeways:** I did not find a North Carolina statute or DOT ordinance that bans pedestrians from interstates in general terms. 19A NCAC 02E .0409 bans riding animals, bicycles, horse-drawn wagons, "any nonmotorized vehicle" and mopeds on interstate or controlled-access highways, but does not mention walking. G.S. § 136-89.58 and § 20-140.3 list prohibited conduct on interstates without a pedestrian item; § 136-89.58 has a subdivision (7) repealed in 1999 whose former content I did not retrieve. Hence `freeway_pedestrians: unclear`. In practice, the hitchhiking ban in .0410 already rules out thumbing there.
- § 20-175(a) applies to "State highways." I did not confirm how it applies to city-maintained streets that are not part of the State highway system; local ordinances may govern there.
- I did not check local ordinances (e.g. Charlotte, Raleigh, Asheville).
- I did not retrieve S.L. 2024-16 itself, so I cannot say exactly which parts of § 20-174.1 it added or changed beyond the repeal of subsection (b).
