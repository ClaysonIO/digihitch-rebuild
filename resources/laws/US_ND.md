# North Dakota (US-ND)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride. The roadway excludes the berm and shoulder, so thumbing from the shoulder or curb is not covered by this rule. No statute bans pedestrians from interstates. The state can ban them on a controlled-access roadway by order plus posted signs, but no such order was found.

## Summary

North Dakota has the Uniform Vehicle Code rule: "No person may stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride" (N.D.C.C. § 39-10-34(1)). "Roadway" is defined as "exclusive of the berm or shoulder". Hitchhiking from the shoulder or curb is not prohibited by this section.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** A violation is a noncriminal traffic violation. The statutory fee is $20 for most moving violations.
- **Interstates (I-94, I-29):** the director of the Department of Transportation (by order) or local authorities (by ordinance) may ban classes of traffic from controlled-access roadways. Any ban is enforceable where official traffic-control devices (signs) are posted. No statute bans pedestrians on interstates outright. A December 2025 news report quoted the Highway Patrol: "In North Dakota we do not have a law saying you can't walk or bike along the interstate system." Even so, stay far from the lanes, and obey any posted sign.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** if there is one, otherwise on the shoulder. With neither, walk on the left edge facing traffic.

## Laws

### N.D.C.C. § 39-10-34: Pedestrian soliciting ride or business

**Source:** [North Dakota Legislative Branch, N.D.C.C. ch. 39-10 (PDF)](https://www.ndlegis.gov/cencode/t39c10.pdf) (official; PDF generated July 1, 2025)

> 1. No person may stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
> 2. No person may stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.
> 3. No person may stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.

**What it means:** Don't stand in a travel lane to ask for a ride. The shoulder is not part of the roadway, so standing on it with your thumb out is not covered by this section.

### N.D.C.C. § 39-01-01: Definitions

**Source:** [North Dakota Legislative Branch, N.D.C.C. ch. 39-01 (PDF)](https://www.ndlegis.gov/cencode/t39c01.pdf)

> 12. "Controlled-access highway" means every highway, street, or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over such highway, street, or roadway.
> [...]
> 76. "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" as used herein refers to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The ride-solicitation ban stops at the edge of the travel lanes.

### N.D.C.C. § 39-10-33: Pedestrian on roadway

**Source:** [North Dakota Legislative Branch, N.D.C.C. ch. 39-10 (PDF)](https://www.ndlegis.gov/cencode/t39c10.pdf)

> 1. Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, it is unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
> 2. Where a sidewalk is not available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk only on a shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
> 3. Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway, and, if on a two-way roadway, shall walk only on the left side of the roadway.
> 4. Except as otherwise provided for in this chapter, any pedestrian upon a roadway shall yield the right of way to all vehicles upon the roadway.

**What it means:** Sidewalk first, then the shoulder, then the left edge facing traffic.

### N.D.C.C. § 39-10-21: Restrictions on use of controlled-access roadway

**Source:** [North Dakota Legislative Branch, N.D.C.C. ch. 39-10 (PDF)](https://www.ndlegis.gov/cencode/t39c10.pdf)

> The director may by order, and local authorities may by ordinance, with respect to any controlled-access roadway under their respective jurisdictions, prohibit the use of any such roadway by any class or kind of traffic which is found incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic.
>
> The director or the local authority adopting any such prohibition shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the controlled-access roadway on which such prohibitions are applicable and when in place no person may disobey the restrictions stated on such devices.

**What it means:** Pedestrian bans on interstates depend on an order plus posted signs. If a ramp is signed against pedestrians, stay before the sign.

### N.D.C.C. §§ 39-06.1-02, 39-06.1-06, 39-06.1-09: Noncriminal disposition and fees

**Source:** [North Dakota Legislative Branch, N.D.C.C. ch. 39-06.1 (PDF)](https://www.ndlegis.gov/cencode/t39c06-1.pdf)

> 39-06.1-02. 1. An individual cited, in accordance with sections 39-07-07 and 39-07-08, for a traffic violation under state law or municipal ordinance, other than an offense listed in section 39-06.1-05, is deemed to be charged with a noncriminal offense. [...]
>
> 39-06.1-06. [...] 2. For a moving violation as defined in section 39-06.1-09, a fee of twenty dollars, except for: [...]
>
> 39-06.1-09. For the purposes of sections 39-06.1-06 and 39-06.1-13, a "moving violation" means [...] a violation of the provisions of chapter 39-10, 39-10.2, 39-21, or 39-27, or equivalent ordinances, except [...]

**What it means:** Section 39-10-34 is in chapter 39-10 and is not listed among the exceptions. A citation for soliciting a ride from the roadway is therefore a noncriminal moving violation with a $20 statutory fee. Moving violations can also put points on a driver's license record under § 39-06.1-10.
