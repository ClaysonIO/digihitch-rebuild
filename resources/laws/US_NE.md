# Nebraska (US-NE)

**Status:** Restricted. On ordinary roads you may not stand *in the roadway* to solicit a ride. The roadway excludes the berm and shoulder. On freeways, including their approach and exit ramps, "hitchhikers or walkers" are banned by name.

## Summary

Nebraska has two rules that matter to hitchhikers:

1. **All highways (§ 60-6,157):** "no person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride". Nebraska's "roadway" excludes the berm and shoulder, so thumbing from the shoulder or curb of an ordinary road is not banned by this section.
2. **Freeways (§ 60-6,144):** use of and entry onto a freeway is prohibited at all times for "pedestrians except in areas specifically designated for that purpose" and for "hitchhikers or walkers". The only exception is a permit from the Department of Transportation or the local authority. "Freeway" is defined to include "all interchanges and approach and exit roads thereto". That means the ramps.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Don't hitchhike on I-80 or any other freeway, including its on-ramps.** Stand on the surface road before the ramp begins.
- **On other roads, stay out of the travel lanes.** Shoulders and curbs are outside the "roadway" rule.
- **Penalty:** a traffic infraction. Fines are up to $100 for a first offense, $200 for a second within a year, and $300 for a third.

## Laws

### Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,157: Pedestrians soliciting rides or business

**Source:** [Nebraska Legislature, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,157](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-6,157) (official; source: Laws 1973, LB 45, § 47; Laws 1993, LB 370, § 253; Laws 2009, LB278, § 1)

> (1) Except as otherwise provided in subsection (3) of this section, no person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, contributions, or business from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (2) No person shall stand on or in proximity to a highway for the purposes of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a highway.
>
> (3)(a) Any municipality may, by ordinance, allow pedestrians over the age of eighteen to enter one or more roadways, except roadways that are part of the state highway system, at specified times and locations and approach vehicles when stopped by traffic control devices or traffic control signals for the purpose of soliciting contributions which are to be devoted to charitable or community betterment purposes. [...]

**What it means:** Don't stand in a travel lane to ask for a ride. The shoulder and curb are not the "roadway" (see § 60-656 below). The subsection (3) exception covers charity collections only, not rides.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,144: Restrictions on use of controlled-access highway

**Source:** [Nebraska Legislature, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,144](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-6,144) (official; last amended Laws 2017, LB339, § 201)

> Use of a freeway and entry thereon by the following shall be prohibited at all times except by permit from the Department of Transportation or from the local authority in the case of freeways not under the jurisdiction of the department:
> (1) Pedestrians except in areas specifically designated for that purpose;
> (2) Hitchhikers or walkers;
> [...]
> (14) Any person standing on or near a roadway for the purpose of soliciting or selling to an occupant of any vehicle; [...]

**What it means:** This is a flat statutory ban. No sign is needed. You may not walk on, enter, or hitchhike on a freeway. Item (14) also covers standing "on or near" a freeway roadway to solicit, so the shoulder is no defense on a freeway.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,145: Official signs on controlled-access highway

**Source:** [Nebraska Legislature, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,145](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-6,145)

> The Department of Transportation and local authorities shall erect and maintain at appropriate locations official signs on freeways under their respective jurisdictions apprising motorists of the restrictions placed upon the use of such highways by the Nebraska Rules of the Road. [...] When such signs are erected, no person shall violate the restrictions stated on such signs.

**What it means:** Signs at freeway entrances restate the ban. The ban in § 60-6,144 applies whether or not a sign is posted.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 60-621, 60-656, 60-661, 60-624: Definitions

**Source:** [§ 60-621](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-621), [§ 60-656](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-656), [§ 60-661](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-661), [§ 60-624](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-624)

> 60-621. Freeway shall mean a divided arterial highway designed primarily for through traffic with full control of access and with grade separations at all intersecting road crossings, including all interchanges and approach and exit roads thereto.
>
> 60-656. Roadway shall mean that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. If a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term roadway shall refer to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.
>
> 60-661. Shoulder shall mean that part of the highway contiguous to the roadway and designed for the accommodation of stopped vehicles, for emergency use, and for lateral support of the base and surface courses of the roadway.
>
> 60-624. Highway shall mean the entire width between the boundary limits of any street, road, avenue, boulevard, or way which is publicly maintained when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel.

**What it means:** Freeway ramps ("approach and exit roads") are part of the freeway, so the hitchhiker ban starts where the ramp starts. On non-freeway roads, the shoulder is outside the "roadway" and outside § 60-6,157.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,156: Pedestrians on highways and roadways

**Source:** [Nebraska Legislature, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 60-6,156](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-6,156)

> (1) Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway or shoulder.
>
> (2) Where a sidewalk is not available and a shoulder is available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk only on the shoulder as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (3) Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, any pedestrian who walks along and upon a highway shall walk as near as practicable to the edge of the roadway and, if on a two-way roadway, shall walk only on the left side of such roadway.

**What it means:** Where there's a sidewalk, you may not walk on the shoulder either. Use the sidewalk.

### Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 60-682 and 60-689: Penalty

**Source:** [§ 60-682](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-682) and [§ 60-689](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=60-689)

> 60-682. Unless otherwise declared in the Nebraska Rules of the Road with respect to particular offenses, a violation of any provision of the rules shall constitute a traffic infraction.
>
> 60-689. Any person who is found guilty of a traffic infraction in violation of the Nebraska Rules of the Road for which a penalty has not been specifically provided shall be fined: (1) Not more than one hundred dollars for the first offense; (2) Not more than two hundred dollars for a second offense within a one-year period; and (3) Not more than three hundred dollars for a third and subsequent offense within a one-year period.

**What it means:** Hitchhiking on a freeway or soliciting from the roadway is a traffic infraction. It is fine-only: up to $100, then $200, then $300 for repeats within a year.
