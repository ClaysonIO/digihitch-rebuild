# New Hampshire (US-NH)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand on the travelled portion of a roadway to solicit a ride. The statute expressly says hitchhiking is lawful on any road, including limited-access highways, as long as you are not standing on the paved portion.

## Summary

New Hampshire's RSA 265:40 has the usual rule ("No person shall stand on the travelled portion of a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride [...]"). Paragraph III goes further than most states and affirmatively permits hitchhiking:

> "It shall be lawful for any person to hitchhike or solicit a ride from the occupant of any vehicle upon any road or way, or limited access road and highway provided that the individual is not, when so doing, standing on the paved portion of the road or way."

So in New Hampshire you may hitchhike even along an interstate or turnpike, provided you stand off the pavement, on the grass or gravel beyond any paved shoulder. Note the difference between the two paragraphs. The paragraph I ban covers only the "travelled portion", but the paragraph III safe harbor requires you to be off the "paved portion". A paved shoulder is safest treated as off-limits.

Breaking a Chapter 265 rule is classed as a "violation" (RSA 265:2), not a misdemeanor.

No New Hampshire statute or NHDOT rule banning pedestrians from limited-access highways was found for this page, so `freeway_pedestrians` is marked unclear. Obey any posted signs.

## Laws

### RSA 265:40: Pedestrians Soliciting Rides or Business

**Source:** [New Hampshire General Court, RSA 265:40](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXI/265/265-40.htm). The official page refused direct downloads in this session. The text below was retrieved from the official page through a fetch tool in short consecutive quoted pieces and reassembled. See open questions.

> I. No person shall stand on the travelled portion of a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, business or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.
> [...]
> III. It shall be lawful for any person to hitchhike or solicit a ride from the occupant of any vehicle upon any road or way, or limited access road and highway provided that the individual is not, when so doing, standing on the paved portion of the road or way.
>
> Source. RSA 262-A:38. 1963, 330:1. 1971, 532:1. 1974, 31:1. 1981, 146:1; 479:8, 38. 1999, 243:1, eff. July 9, 1999.

**What it means:** Stay off the pavement and you may hitchhike on any road in the state, interstates and turnpikes included. Standing in a travel lane is a violation. Paragraph II (soliciting to watch parked cars) and paragraph IV (selling event tickets to moving vehicles) do not affect hitchhikers.

### RSA 259:92: Roadway

**Source:** [New Hampshire General Court, RSA 259:92](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXI/259/259-92.htm) (retrieved the same way as RSA 265:40)

> "Roadway" shall mean that portion of a way improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the sidewalk, berm or shoulder even though such sidewalk, berm or shoulder is used by persons riding bicycles or other human powered vehicles. In the event a way includes 2 or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" as used herein shall refer to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.
>
> Source. 1981, 146:1, eff. Jan. 1, 1982.

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway, so standing on it does not break paragraph I. To fit the paragraph III safe harbor, though, you must also be off the paved portion.

### RSA 265:39, I: Pedestrians on Roadway

**Source:** [New Hampshire General Court, RSA 265:39](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXI/265/265-39.htm) (retrieved the same way as RSA 265:40)

> I. Where sidewalks are provided it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk where there is one. Paragraph II of the same section tells pedestrians without a sidewalk to use the shoulder, or else the left edge of the road.

### RSA 265:2: Required Obedience to Traffic Laws; Penalty

**Source:** [New Hampshire General Court, RSA 265:2](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXI/265/265-2.htm) (retrieved the same way as RSA 265:40)

> 265:2 Required Obedience to Traffic Laws; Penalty. – Unless otherwise declared in this chapter with respect to particular offenses, any person who commits any act forbidden or fails to perform any act required in this chapter shall be guilty of a violation.
>
> Source. RSA 262-A:2. 1963, 330:1. 1973, 529:47. 1981, 146:1, eff. Jan. 1, 1982.

**What it means:** Breaking RSA 265:40 is classed as a "violation", not a misdemeanor.
