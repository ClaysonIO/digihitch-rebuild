# New Jersey (US-NJ)

**Status:** Illegal. New Jersey bars standing "in a highway" to solicit a ride, and "highway" means the full width of the public way, so the shoulder is included. Pedestrians and ride solicitation are also banned on the New Jersey Turnpike and the Garden State Parkway.

## Summary

New Jersey's rule, N.J.S.A. 39:4-59 ("Begging rides prohibited"), is stricter than the usual Uniform Vehicle Code wording. Most states say "roadway", which excludes the shoulder. New Jersey says "highway", which § 39:1-1 defines as "the entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained." So thumbing from the shoulder of a public road is covered. Only a ride from a bus or streetcar is exempt.

What this means in practice:

- **Don't stand anywhere in a public highway to solicit a ride,** shoulder included. Private property next to the road, such as a gas station, truck stop or parking lot (with the owner's permission), is outside the "highway".
- **New Jersey Turnpike and Garden State Parkway:** pedestrians are prohibited and "soliciting of rides on the Roadway is prohibited" (N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.9 and 19:9-1.13). Here "Roadway" is defined to mean the whole Turnpike and Parkway, including service areas and interchanges.
- **Other limited-access highways:** the Commissioner of Transportation and counties may prohibit pedestrians by regulation (N.J.S.A. 27:7A-6). The text of the state rule was not retrieved for this page.

Penalty: § 39:4-59 does not set its own penalty, so the general penalty in § 39:4-203 applies. That is a fine of up to $50 or up to 15 days in jail, or both. A higher range applies to some articles of the chapter. On Turnpike Authority property, a violation is punishable by a fine of up to $500, up to 30 days, or both (N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.33).

## Laws

### N.J.S.A. 39:4-59: Begging rides prohibited

**Source:** [New Jersey Legislature, official statutes text download (STATUTES-TEXT.zip, file dated 2026-11-01)](https://pub.njleg.gov/statutes/STATUTES-TEXT.zip)

> 39:4-59. Begging rides prohibited
> No person shall stand in a highway for the purpose of or while soliciting a ride from the operator of any vehicle other than an omnibus or a street car.
>
> Amended by L.1951, c. 23, p. 78, s. 34.

**What it means:** Asking for a ride while standing anywhere in a public highway is an offense, shoulder included (see the definition below). The only exception is asking a bus or streetcar driver. Solicit rides from private property off the highway instead, with permission, for example at gas stations, truck stops or diners where drivers are already stopped. Turnpike and Parkway service areas do not count (see below).

### N.J.S.A. 39:1-1: Definitions ("Highway", "Roadway", "Limited-access highway")

**Source:** [New Jersey Legislature, official statutes text download](https://pub.njleg.gov/statutes/STATUTES-TEXT.zip)

> "Highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel.
>
> "Limited-access highway" means every highway, street, or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from, except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over such highway, street, or roadway; and includes any highway designated as a "freeway" or "parkway" by authority of law.
>
> "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. [...]

**What it means:** "Highway" covers the whole public right-of-way, including shoulders and sidewalks. "Roadway" excludes the shoulder, but § 39:4-59 uses "highway", not "roadway". That is why New Jersey is rated illegal here, not merely restricted.

### N.J.S.A. 39:4-203: General penalty for violations of chapter

**Source:** [New Jersey Legislature, official statutes text download](https://pub.njleg.gov/statutes/STATUTES-TEXT.zip)

> For a violation of a provision of this chapter or any supplement thereto for which no specific penalty is provided, the offender shall be liable to a penalty of not more than $50.00 or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 15 days, or both; except that for a violation of a section of article 11, 13, 14 or 17 of this chapter or any supplement thereto for which no specific penalty is provided, the offender shall be liable to a penalty of not less than $50.00 or more than $200.00 or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 15 days, or both.

**What it means:** A violation of § 39:4-59 carries a fine of up to $50 (or up to $200 if the section falls in one of the listed articles), and theoretically up to 15 days in jail.

### N.J.S.A. 27:7A-6: Restricted use of limited access highways

**Source:** [New Jersey Legislature, official statutes text download](https://pub.njleg.gov/statutes/STATUTES-TEXT.zip)

> The commissioner, with respect to limited access highways under his jurisdiction, and the governing body of a county, with respect to limited access highways under its jurisdiction, shall have the authority to restrict the use of roadways in limited access highways to passenger motor vehicles, to prohibit the use of any roadway in limited access highways by certain classes of vehicles or by pedestrians, bicycles or other nonmotorized traffic [...]

**What it means:** NJDOT and counties can ban pedestrians from limited-access highways by regulation. Expect "no pedestrians" signs at interstate ramps. Even without them, § 39:4-59 already bans hitchhiking anywhere in a highway.

### N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.9 and 19:9-1.13: New Jersey Turnpike Authority rules (Turnpike and Garden State Parkway)

**Source:** [N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.9, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-19-9-1-9) and [N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.13, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-19-9-1-13) (unofficial mirror; both last amended by 57 N.J.R. 55(c), effective 1/6/2025)

> 19:9-1.9 (a) Use of the Roadway and entry thereon by the following, unless otherwise authorized by the Authority, is prohibited: 1. Pedestrians; [...]

> 19:9-1.13 (a) Soliciting of alms or contributions on the Roadway is prohibited. (b) Soliciting of rides on the Roadway is prohibited. [...]

**What it means:** On the Turnpike and the Parkway you may not be on foot or solicit rides at all. The definition below shows that this includes service areas, toll plazas and interchanges.

### N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.1: Definitions ("Roadway", "Turnpike", "Parkway")

**Source:** [N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.1, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-19-9-1-1) (unofficial mirror)

> "Roadway" means, collectively, the Turnpike and the Parkway.
>
> "Turnpike" means the express highway, superhighway, or motorway known as the New Jersey Turnpike, owned and operated by the Authority pursuant to the provisions at N.J.S.A. 27:23-1 et seq., and shall include, but not be limited to, the Turnpike right-of-way, all bridges, tunnels, underpasses, interchanges, entrance plazas, approaches, toll houses, service areas, service stations, service facilities, [...]

**What it means:** Unlike the ordinary statutory meaning, "Roadway" in these rules covers the entire Turnpike or Parkway property. Service areas count, so asking for rides at a Turnpike service plaza is prohibited.

### N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.33: Violations and penalties

**Source:** [N.J.A.C. 19:9-1.33, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-19-9-1-33) (unofficial mirror)

> (a) If the violation of this chapter would have been a violation of law or ordinance if committed on any public road, street, or highway in the municipality in which such violation occurred, it shall be tried and punished in the same manner as if it had been committed in such municipality. (b) Except as set forth at (a) above and N.J.A.C. 19:9-9.3(a), any violation of this chapter shall be punishable by a fine not exceeding $ 500.00 or by imprisonment not exceeding 30 days or by both such fine and imprisonment. [...]

**What it means:** Hitchhiking on the Turnpike or Parkway is punished like the equivalent offense on a public road. Other violations of the Authority's rules carry up to $500 and/or 30 days.
