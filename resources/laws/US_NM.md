# New Mexico (US-NM)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride. The roadway excludes the berm and shoulder, so thumbing from the shoulder or curb is not covered by this rule. Pedestrians can be banned from controlled-access highways where signs are posted.

## Summary

New Mexico uses the Uniform Vehicle Code wording: "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment or business from the occupant of any vehicle" (NMSA § 66-7-340). New Mexico defines "roadway" as excluding "the berm or shoulder". Hitchhiking from the shoulder, curb or sidewalk is therefore not banned by state statute.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Soliciting from the roadway is a "penalty assessment misdemeanor". The scheduled penalty is $25 (see § 66-8-116 below).
- **Freeways:** the State Transportation Commission or a local authority may restrict or ban classes of traffic, such as pedestrians, from a controlled-access roadway. The ban applies once signs or other official traffic-control devices are in place.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** where there is one. Otherwise walk on the left side of the road or its shoulder, facing traffic.
- **Albuquerque and Santa Fe** have median-safety ordinances that limit standing on narrow medians, according to news reports. The texts were not reviewed here. Check them if you are hitchhiking in those cities.

## Laws

### NMSA 1978, § 66-7-340: Pedestrians soliciting rides or business

**Source:** [FindLaw, N.M. Stat. § 66-7-340](https://codes.findlaw.com/nm/chapter-66-motor-vehicles/nm-st-sect-66-7-340/) (unofficial mirror, "current as of January 01, 2024"). The official compilation at nmonesource.com refused automated access during this session.

> A. No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment or business from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> B. No person shall stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.

**What it means:** Don't step into the lanes to ask for a ride. The rule covers only the roadway, which by definition excludes the shoulder. Standing on the shoulder or behind the curb with your thumb out is not prohibited by this section.

### NMSA 1978, § 66-1-4.15: Definitions ("roadway")

**Source:** [New Mexico Legislature, 2024 S.B. 88 (as passed), § 4 amending § 66-1-4.15](https://www.nmlegis.gov/Sessions/24%20Regular/final/SB0088.PDF) (signed by the governor, Laws 2024, ch. 13). The bill restates the full section.

> Q. "roadway" means that portion of a street or highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder; when a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term "roadway" refers to each roadway separately but not to all of the roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway. The ride-solicitation ban in § 66-7-340 applies to the travel lanes, not the shoulder.

### NMSA 1978, § 66-7-339: Pedestrians on roadways

**Source:** [New Mexico Legislature, 2024 (1st Special Session) S.B. 11 (introduced), § 5](https://www.nmlegis.gov/Sessions/24%20Special/bills/senate/SB0011.HTML). The bill reprints the existing section with proposed additions underlined. The quote below is the existing text only. The bill died, so this text remains in force. It matches the FindLaw mirror (current as of January 01, 2024).

> A. Where sidewalks are provided, it is unlawful for a pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> B. Where sidewalks are not provided, a pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic that may approach from the opposite direction.
>
> C. A person who violates the provisions of this section is guilty of a penalty assessment misdemeanor.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk where there is one. Otherwise walk facing traffic on the left side of the road or shoulder.

### NMSA 1978, § 66-7-321: Restrictions on use of controlled-access roadway

**Source:** [FindLaw, N.M. Stat. § 66-7-321](https://codes.findlaw.com/nm/chapter-66-motor-vehicles/nm-st-sect-66-7-321/) (unofficial mirror, "current as of January 01, 2024")

> A. The state transportation commission, by resolution or order entered in its minutes, and local authorities, by ordinance, may regulate or prohibit the use of any controlled-access roadway within their respective jurisdictions by any class or kind of traffic that is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic.
>
> B. The state transportation commission or the local authority adopting any such prohibition shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the controlled-access roadway on which the prohibitions are applicable, and, when in place, no person shall disobey the restrictions stated on the devices.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians from every New Mexico freeway. Where a sign bans pedestrians, you must stay off. Stand at the start of the on-ramp, before any such sign, or at a truck stop.

### NMSA 1978, § 66-8-116: Penalty assessment misdemeanors (schedule)

**Source:** [New Mexico Legislature, 2025 S.B. 265 (introduced), § 4](https://www.nmlegis.gov/Sessions/25%20Regular/bills/senate/SB0265.html). The bill reprints the existing schedule, with proposed increases shown as bracketed deletions and underlined insertions. The quote below is the existing (bracketed) amounts. S.B. 265 died. Later bills that would also have raised these amounts (2025 S.B. 225, 2026 S.B. 214) also died.

> A. As used in the Motor Vehicle Code and the Boat Act, "penalty assessment misdemeanor" means violation of any of the following listed sections of the NMSA 1978 for which, except as provided in Subsections D through F of this section, the listed penalty assessment is established:
> [...]
> COMMON NAME OF OFFENSE / SECTION VIOLATED / PENALTY ASSESSMENT
> [...]
> Controlled access violation / 66-7-321 / 25.00
> [...]
> Pedestrian violation / 66-7-333 through 66-7-340 / 25.00

**What it means:** Soliciting a ride from the roadway (§ 66-7-340), walking on the wrong side (§ 66-7-339) and ignoring a freeway pedestrian ban (§ 66-7-321) each carry a $25 penalty assessment, plus court fees.

## History

### 2024 special session: "Unsafe Use of Public Highways and Medians Act" (died)

**Source:** [2024 (1st Special Session) S.B. 11](https://www.nmlegis.gov/Sessions/24%20Special/bills/senate/SB0011.HTML) and its [bill status page](https://www.nmlegis.gov/Legislation/Legislation?chamber=S&legType=B&legNo=11&year=24s) ("Died")

The bill would have made it unlawful for a pedestrian to "occupy a travel lane of the exit or entrance ramp of a highway". It would also have banned walking on any highway with a posted speed limit of 30 mph or more. It died. Neither rule is law.
