# Nevada (US-NV)

**Status:** Illegal. Nevada law says a person "shall not stand in a highway to solicit a ride". Nevada defines "highway" as the entire width between the boundary lines of the way, which includes the shoulder. Violation is a misdemeanor, though a prosecutor may choose to treat it as a civil infraction.

## Summary

Most states that follow the Uniform Vehicle Code ban standing in the *roadway* (the travel lanes) to hitch. Nevada's NRS 484B.297(3) uses the word *highway* instead. Nevada defines a highway as "the entire width between the boundary lines" of a public way. On its face, that covers the shoulder and everything else inside the right-of-way, so there is no safe-harbor shoulder in Nevada.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Don't stand anywhere on a highway, shoulder included, to solicit a ride.** Hitch from places outside the highway right-of-way, such as gas stations, truck stops and parking lots. Ask drivers directly where the property owner allows it.
- **Walking:** use the sidewalk where one exists. Otherwise walk on the left side facing traffic.
- **Controlled-access highways (freeways):** the Nevada Department of Transportation (NDOT) or a local authority may require a permit for pedestrians, or prohibit them where pedestrian use wouldn't be safe. Violating such a restriction is a civil infraction.
- **Penalty:** hitchhiking under NRS 484B.297 is a misdemeanor. Since 2021, a prosecutor may elect to treat a traffic misdemeanor as a civil infraction instead.

## Laws

### NRS 484B.297: Walking along highways; solicitation of ride

**Source:** [Nevada Legislature, NRS 484B.297](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-484B.html#NRS484BSec297) (history: Added 1969; amended 1991, 2001, 2015, 2019)

> 1. Except as otherwise provided in subsection 6, where sidewalks are provided, it is unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent highway.
>
> 2. Except as otherwise provided in subsection 6, pedestrians walking along highways where sidewalks are not provided shall walk on the left side of those highways facing the approaching traffic.
>
> 3. A person shall not stand in a highway to solicit a ride or any business from the driver or any occupant of a vehicle. A person shall not, without a permit issued pursuant to NRS 244.3555 or 268.423, solicit any contribution from the driver or any occupant of a vehicle.
>
> 4. It is unlawful for any pedestrian who is under the influence of intoxicating liquors or any narcotic or stupefying drug to be within the traveled portion of any highway.
> [...]
>
> 7. A person who violates the provisions of this section is guilty of a misdemeanor.

**What it means:** The ban is on standing "in a highway", not just in the travel lanes. Read with the definition below, that includes the shoulder. Find a spot off the highway itself, like a fuel station forecourt or a pull-out on private property where you have permission.

### NRS 484A.095 and 484A.220: "Highway" and "Roadway" defined

**Source:** [Nevada Legislature, NRS chapter 484A](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-484A.html#NRS484ASec095) (484A.095: Added 1969, amended 1981; 484A.220: Added 1973)

> NRS 484A.095 "Highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every way dedicated to a public authority when any part of the way is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular traffic, whether or not the public authority is maintaining the way.

> NRS 484A.220 "Roadway" means that portion of a highway which is improved and ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, exclusive of the shoulder.

**What it means:** Nevada's statutes use both terms, and the hitchhiking rule uses the broader one. The roadway excludes the shoulder. The highway includes it.

### NRS 484B.593: Restrictions on use of controlled-access highway

**Source:** [Nevada Legislature, NRS 484B.593](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-484B.html#NRS484BSec593) (history: Added 1969; amended 1979, 1987, 1991, 2009, 2021)

> 1. The Department of Transportation or a local authority, after considering the advice of the Nevada Bicycle and Pedestrian Advisory Board, may with respect to any controlled-access highway under its jurisdiction:
> (a) Require a permit for the use of the highway by pedestrians, bicycles or other nonmotorized traffic or by any person operating a power cycle; or
> (b) If it determines that the use of the highway for such a purpose would not be safe, prohibit the use of the highway by pedestrians, bicycles or other nonmotorized traffic.
>
> 2. Any person who violates any prohibition or restriction enacted pursuant to subsection 1 is guilty of a civil infraction punishable pursuant to NRS 484A.703 to 484A.705, inclusive.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians from every freeway, but NDOT or a local authority may close specific freeways to them. Even where walking is allowed, standing on any part of the freeway to hitch still violates NRS 484B.297(3).

### NRS 484A.060: "Controlled-access highway" defined

**Source:** [Nevada Legislature, NRS 484A.060](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-484A.html#NRS484ASec060) (Added 1969)

> "Controlled-access highway" means every highway to or from which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by a public authority.

**What it means:** Interstates and other freeways.

### NRS 484A.7049: Misdemeanor may be treated as civil infraction

**Source:** [Nevada Legislature, NRS 484A.7049](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-484A.html#NRS484ASec7049) (Added 2021; amended 2025)

> A prosecuting attorney may elect to treat a violation of a provision of chapters 483 to 484E, inclusive, 486 or 490 of NRS that is punishable as a misdemeanor, other than a violation of NRS 484C.110 or 484C.120, as a civil infraction pursuant to NRS 484A.703 to 484A.705, inclusive.

**What it means:** Since 2021, the default penalty for a traffic offense with no stated penalty has been a civil infraction (NRS 484A.900). The hitchhiking section still says "misdemeanor", but a prosecutor can choose to downgrade it to a civil infraction, which is not a criminal offense.
