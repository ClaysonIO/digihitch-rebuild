# New York (US-NY)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in the roadway (the travel lanes) to solicit a ride. The shoulder is not part of the "roadway". Pedestrians are banned from state expressways and interstates, including their ramps, and hitchhiking is banned everywhere on the New York State Thruway.

## Summary

New York follows the Uniform Vehicle Code pattern. Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1157(a) says "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride." Section 140 defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicle travel, "exclusive of the shoulder and slope." So hitchhiking from the shoulder or curb of an ordinary road is not covered by § 1157(a).

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Stand on the shoulder, curb or sidewalk where a car can pull fully off the road.
- **No walking on state expressways or interstates, or their entrance and exit ramps** (§ 1229-a), except at rest areas, in emergencies, or on a provided sidewalk or path. This rules out standing on an interstate on-ramp. Stand on the local road before the ramp begins.
- **No hitchhiking anywhere on the Thruway system, including toll plazas** (21 NYCRR § 102.4). Drivers may not stop to pick up or drop off hitchhikers there either.
- **Outside cities and villages,** § 1157(c) bars occupying any part of a state highway "for the purpose of selling or soliciting". See the open question below on whether this reaches ride solicitation.

A violation of the Vehicle and Traffic Law is a traffic infraction. Under § 1800(b), a first conviction carries a fine of up to $150, up to 15 days in jail, or both.

## Laws

### N.Y. Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1157: Pedestrians soliciting rides, or business

**Source:** [NY State Senate, Consolidated Laws, VAT § 1157](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/VAT/1157)

> § 1157. Pedestrians soliciting rides, or business. (a) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, or to solicit from or sell to an occupant of any vehicle.
> (b) No person shall stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.
> (c) No person shall occupy any part of a state highway, except in a city or village, in any manner for the purpose of selling or soliciting.

**What it means:** Subsection (a) is the hitchhiking rule. It applies only to the "roadway", meaning the travel lanes. Thumbing from the shoulder of a normal road is not covered. Subsection (c) is broader ("any part of a state highway"), but it is aimed at selling and soliciting outside cities and villages. It does not mention rides. Avoid lingering on state highway rights-of-way in rural areas if police object.

### N.Y. Vehicle and Traffic Law § 140: Roadway

**Source:** [NY State Senate, Consolidated Laws, VAT § 140](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/VAT/140)

> § 140. Roadway. That portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the shoulder and slope. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways the term "roadway" as used herein shall refer to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder and the slope beside it are not "roadway". This is why § 1157(a) does not reach a hitchhiker standing on the shoulder.

### N.Y. Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1229-a: Pedestrians prohibited on state expressway and interstate highways

**Source:** [NY State Senate, Consolidated Laws, VAT § 1229-A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/VAT/1229-A)

> § 1229-a. Pedestrians, animals, and non-motorized vehicles prohibited on state expressway highways or state interstate route highways including the entrances thereto and exits therefrom. No person, unless otherwise directed by a police officer shall: (a) As a pedestrian, occupy any space within the limits of a state expressway highway or state interstate route highway, including the entrances thereto and exits therefrom, except: in a rest area, parking area, or scenic overlook; in the performance of public works or official duties; as a result of an emergency caused by an accident or breakdown of a motor vehicle or to obtain assistance; where a sidewalk, footpath or pedestrian crossing of such a highway is provided; [...]

**What it means:** On interstates and state expressways you may not be on foot anywhere within the highway limits, including the ramps. Rest areas and parking areas are an exception, so asking drivers for a ride at a rest area is not barred by this section. Elsewhere, hitchhike from the surface street before the ramp.

### N.Y. Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1156: Pedestrians on roadways

**Source:** [NY State Senate, Consolidated Laws, VAT § 1156](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/VAT/1156)

> § 1156. Pedestrians on roadways. (a) Where sidewalks are provided and they may be used with safety it shall be unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
> (b) Where sidewalks are not provided any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall when practicable walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic which may approach from the opposite direction. Upon the approach of any vehicle from the opposite direction, such pedestrian shall move as far to the left as is practicable.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one. Otherwise walk on the left, facing traffic.

### N.Y. Vehicle and Traffic Law § 1800: Penalties for traffic infractions

**Source:** [NY State Senate, Consolidated Laws, VAT § 1800](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/VAT/1800)

> § 1800. Penalties for traffic infractions. (a) It is a traffic infraction for any person to violate any of the provisions of this chapter or of any local law, ordinance, order, rule or regulation adopted pursuant to this chapter, unless such violation is by this chapter or other law of this state declared to be a misdemeanor or a felony.
> (b) 1. Every person convicted of a traffic infraction for a violation of any of the provisions of this chapter [...] for which another penalty is not provided shall for a first conviction thereof be punished by a fine of not more than one hundred fifty dollars or by imprisonment for not more than fifteen days or by both such fine and imprisonment; for a conviction of a second violation, both of which were committed within a period of eighteen months, such person shall be punished by a fine of not more than three hundred dollars or by imprisonment for not more than forty-five days or by both such fine and imprisonment; upon a conviction of a third or subsequent violation, all of which were committed within a period of eighteen months, such person shall be punished by a fine of not more than four hundred fifty dollars or by imprisonment for not more than ninety days or by both such fine and imprisonment [...]

**What it means:** Violating § 1157 or § 1229-a is a traffic infraction, not a crime. The maximum is $150 and/or 15 days for a first offense, rising for repeat offenses within 18 months.

### 21 NYCRR § 102.4: Hitchhiking; loitering (New York State Thruway)

**Source:** [N.Y. Comp. Codes R. & Regs. tit. 21, § 102.4, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/21-NYCRR-102.4) (unofficial mirror of the Thruway Authority regulations, Title 21, Chapter III, Part 102)

> The solicitation of a ride, commonly known as hitchhiking, or stopping any vehicle for the purpose of picking up or discharging a hitchhiker, on any portion of the Thruway system, including toll plazas, is prohibited. Loitering in or about the toll plazas or any other portion of the Thruway system, for the purpose of hitchhiking or for any other purpose, is prohibited.

**What it means:** The Thruway (I-87/I-90 and its spurs) is completely off-limits for hitchhiking, including at toll plazas. This rule also covers drivers, who may not stop to pick up or drop off a hitchhiker on the Thruway.

### 21 NYCRR § 102.1: Prohibited uses of the Thruway

**Source:** [N.Y. Comp. Codes R. & Regs. tit. 21, § 102.1, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/21-NYCRR-102.1) (unofficial mirror)

> (a) Use of the Thruway system by the following is prohibited at all times, with the noted exceptions: (1) Pedestrians, except on the Grand Island Bridges sidewalks and on the Governor Mario M. Cuomo Bridge's shared use path when such sidewalks and/or shared use path are not closed or otherwise prescribed at the sole discretion of the Thruway Authority. [...]

**What it means:** You may not be on foot anywhere on the Thruway system, apart from two bridge paths.

## Unverified / open questions

- **§ 1157(c) and rides:** It is unclear whether "selling or soliciting" in § 1157(c) covers soliciting a ride on a rural state highway shoulder. No case law was checked.
- **Thruway penalty:** The penalty for violating 21 NYCRR Part 102 was not confirmed. It may be a traffic infraction under VTL § 1800(a), as a "rule or regulation adopted pursuant to this chapter", but the rule's enabling authority was not checked.
- **Local rules:** New York City and other local ordinances were not researched.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** The hitchhiking ban covers only the roadway, so the shoulder, curb or sidewalk is outside it. Stay off interstates, expressways and their ramps (rest areas excepted), and don't hitchhike anywhere on the Thruway.
