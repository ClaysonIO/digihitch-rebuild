# Ohio (US-OH)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not solicit a ride while on the roadway (the travel and parking lanes), but Ohio's definition of "roadway" excludes the berm and shoulder. Pedestrians are banned from freeways, and hitchhiking is banned outright on the Ohio Turnpike.

## Summary

Ohio Revised Code § 4511.51(A) says no person "while on a roadway outside a safety zone shall solicit a ride from the driver of any vehicle." Section 4511.01(EE) defines "roadway" as the part of a highway used for vehicle travel and parking lanes, "not including the berm, sidewalk, or shoulder." So thumbing from the sidewalk, curb or shoulder of an ordinary road is not covered by § 4511.51(A).

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes** when you ask for a ride.
- **Don't go onto a freeway at all.** Section 4511.051 bars pedestrians from anywhere within the right-of-way of a freeway, which includes the shoulders, except in a rest area, on a marked pedestrian path, or in an emergency such as a breakdown.
- **The Ohio Turnpike is stricter.** Turnpike Commission rules ban hitchhiking, ban drivers from stopping to pick up or drop off hitchhikers, ban loitering at toll plazas and service plazas, and bar pedestrians from the turnpike.
- **Panhandling-style solicitation is separate.** Section 4511.51(B) bars standing on a highway to solicit "employment, business, or contributions" from vehicle occupants. That division is not about rides.

A first violation of § 4511.51(A) or § 4511.051 is a minor misdemeanor (fine of up to $150). Prior traffic convictions within a year raise it to a fourth- or third-degree misdemeanor.

## Laws

### Ohio Rev. Code § 4511.51: Hitchhiking - soliciting employment, business, or contributions from occupant of vehicle

**Source:** [Ohio Laws (Legislative Service Commission), R.C. 4511.51](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4511.51) (effective January 1, 2004; latest legislation S.B. 123, 124th General Assembly)

> (A) No person while on a roadway outside a safety zone shall solicit a ride from the driver of any vehicle.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes (or parking lanes) asking for a ride. Because "roadway" excludes the shoulder and sidewalk (see § 4511.01 below), standing on the shoulder or sidewalk with your thumb out is not covered by this division. A "safety zone" is an officially marked pedestrian island within the roadway.

> (B)(1) Except as provided in division (B)(2) of this section, no person shall stand on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business, or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.

**What it means:** This bans standing anywhere on a "highway" (which includes the whole right-of-way) to ask drivers for money, work or business. It doesn't mention rides, so it is not a hitchhiking rule. Don't combine a ride request with asking for gas money or donations on a sign.

> (C) No person shall hang onto or ride on the outside of any motor vehicle, streetcar, or trackless trolley while it is moving upon a roadway, [...]
>
> (D) No operator shall knowingly permit any person to hang onto, or ride on the outside of, any motor vehicle, streetcar, or trackless trolley while it is moving upon a roadway, [...]

**What it means:** Ride inside the vehicle. Riding on the outside is illegal for you and for the driver.

> (G)(1) Except as otherwise provided in this division, whoever violates any provision of divisions (A) to (D) of this section is guilty of a minor misdemeanor. If, within one year of the offense, the offender previously has been convicted of or pleaded guilty to one predicate motor vehicle or traffic offense, whoever violates any provision of divisions (A) to (D) of this section is guilty of a misdemeanor of the fourth degree. If, within one year of the offense, the offender previously has been convicted of two or more predicate motor vehicle or traffic offenses, whoever violates any provision of divisions (A) to (D) of this section is guilty of a misdemeanor of the third degree.

**What it means:** A first offense is a minor misdemeanor, the lowest level of offense in Ohio, which carries no jail. Repeat traffic offenses within a year make it a more serious misdemeanor.

### Ohio Rev. Code § 4511.01: Definitions

**Source:** [Ohio Laws (Legislative Service Commission), R.C. 4511.01](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4511.01) (effective September 30, 2025; latest legislation H.B. 96, 136th General Assembly)

> (BB) "Street" or "highway" means a general term for denoting a public way for purposes of travel by vehicles, streetcars, trackless trolleys, and vulnerable road users, including the entire area within the right-of-way.
>
> [...]
>
> (EE) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel and parking lanes, not including the berm, sidewalk, or shoulder, even if the berm, sidewalk, or shoulder is used by a person operating a bicycle or other human-powered vehicle. If a highway includes two or more separate roadways the term "roadway" means any such roadway separately but not all such roadways collectively.
>
> [...]
>
> (MM) "Safety zone" means the area or space officially set apart within a roadway for the exclusive use of pedestrians and protected or marked or indicated by adequate signs as to be plainly visible at all times.
>
> [...]
>
> (YY) "Freeway" means a divided multi-lane highway for through traffic with all crossroads separated in grade and with full control of access.

**What it means:** The "roadway" is only the lanes cars drive and park in. The shoulder, berm and sidewalk are not part of it, so the hitchhiking ban in § 4511.51(A) does not reach them. A "highway" is the whole right-of-way, which matters for the freeway ban below.

### Ohio Rev. Code § 4511.051: Freeways - prohibited acts

**Source:** [Ohio Laws (Legislative Service Commission), R.C. 4511.051](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4511.051) (effective October 29, 2018; latest legislation H.B. 250 and H.B. 95, 132nd General Assembly)

> (A) No person, unless otherwise directed by a police officer, shall:
> (1) As a pedestrian, occupy any space within the limits of the right-of-way of a freeway, except: in a rest area; on a facility that is separated from the roadway and shoulders of the freeway and is designed and appropriately marked for pedestrian use; in the performance of public works or official duties; as a result of an emergency caused by an accident or breakdown of a motor vehicle; or to obtain assistance;
>
> [...]
>
> (B) Except as otherwise provided in this division, whoever violates this section is guilty of a minor misdemeanor. If, within one year of the offense, the offender previously has been convicted of or pleaded guilty to one predicate motor vehicle or traffic offense, whoever violates this section is guilty of a misdemeanor of the fourth degree. If, within one year of the offense, the offender previously has been convicted of two or more predicate motor vehicle or traffic offenses, whoever violates this section is guilty of a misdemeanor of the third degree. [...]

**What it means:** As a pedestrian you may not be anywhere inside a freeway's right-of-way. That covers the shoulders, and ramps are generally inside the freeway right-of-way too (the statute doesn't name ramps, so where the right-of-way begins is a local fact). Rest areas are an exception. Hitchhike from the surface street before the on-ramp, or from a gas station or truck stop near the interchange.

### Ohio Admin. Code 5537-3-02: Hitchhiking and loitering prohibited (Ohio Turnpike)

**Source:** [Ohio Laws, Ohio Administrative Code rule 5537-3-02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-5537-3-02) (effective March 3, 2001; Ohio Turnpike and Infrastructure Commission)

> The solicitation of a ride, commonly known as "hitchhiking," or stopping for the purpose of picking up or discharging a hitchhiker, is hereby prohibited on the turnpike. Loitering in or about the toll plazas, service plazas or any other portion of the turnpike is prohibited.

**What it means:** On the Ohio Turnpike (I-80/I-90 across northern Ohio), hitchhiking is banned everywhere, including service plazas and toll plazas. Drivers are also forbidden to stop to pick you up or drop you off. Asking for rides at turnpike service plazas can get you cited.

### Ohio Admin. Code 5537-3-01: Prohibited users (Ohio Turnpike)

**Source:** [Ohio Laws, Ohio Administrative Code rule 5537-3-01](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-5537-3-01) (effective September 1, 2023)

> The following shall not be permitted to travel on the turnpike: (A) Pedestrians. (B) Bicycles. [...]

**What it means:** You may not walk on the turnpike.

### Ohio Rev. Code §§ 5537.16(C) and 5537.99: Penalty for violating turnpike rules

**Source:** [R.C. 5537.16](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5537.16) (effective March 23, 2022) and [R.C. 5537.99](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5537.99) (effective July 1, 2009), Ohio Laws

> (C) No person shall violate any such bylaws or rules of the commission.

> (A) Except as provided in division (B) of this section, whoever violates division (C) of section 5537.16 of the Revised Code is guilty of a minor misdemeanor on a first offense; on each subsequent offense such person is guilty of a misdemeanor of the fourth degree.

**What it means:** Breaking the turnpike hitchhiking rule is a minor misdemeanor the first time, and a fourth-degree misdemeanor after that. This applies to drivers who stop for you, too.

### Ohio Rev. Code § 2929.28: Financial sanctions for misdemeanors

**Source:** [Ohio Laws, R.C. 2929.28](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2929.28) (effective October 3, 2023)

> (iv) For a misdemeanor of the fourth degree, not more than two hundred fifty dollars;
> (v) For a minor misdemeanor, not more than one hundred fifty dollars.

**What it means:** The maximum fine for a minor misdemeanor is $150, and $250 for a fourth-degree misdemeanor, plus court costs.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may ask for a ride from the shoulder, berm or sidewalk; Ohio's "roadway" excludes them. Don't solicit from travel or parking lanes. No pedestrians on freeways, and no hitchhiking anywhere on the Ohio Turnpike.
