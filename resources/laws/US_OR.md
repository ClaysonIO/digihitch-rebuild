# Oregon (US-OR)

**Status:** Restricted. Being on the roadway to solicit a ride is "unlawful hitchhiking". Oregon defines the roadway as excluding the shoulder, and its pedestrian law expressly lets a hitchhiker stand on the right shoulder facing traffic. Pedestrians are banned only on specific, signed freeway segments around Portland and Medford.

## Summary

Oregon is one of the few states whose statutes openly accommodate hitchhikers:

- **ORS 814.080** makes it an offense to be *on a roadway* to solicit a ride.
- **ORS 801.450** defines "roadway" as excluding the shoulder.
- **ORS 814.070** normally requires pedestrians on two-way highways to use the left shoulder. It makes an exception for **a hitchhiker on the right shoulder, as long as the hitchhiker faces the traffic in the adjacent lane**.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stand on the shoulder, not in the lane.** The right shoulder is fine if you face oncoming traffic in that lane.
- **Freeways:** an Oregon Department of Transportation rule bans pedestrians only on listed freeway segments: parts of I-84, US 26 (Sunset Highway), I-5, I-205, all of I-405 and US 30 in the Portland area, plus I-5 through Medford. The ban applies once signs are up. No statute bans pedestrians from all freeways.
- **Penalty:** unlawful hitchhiking is a Class D traffic violation. The maximum fine is $250 and the presumptive fine is $115. There is no jail.

## Laws

### ORS 814.080: Unlawful hitchhiking

**Source:** [Oregon State Legislature, ORS chapter 814](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors814.html) (2025 Edition; history: 1983 c.338 §559; 1995 c.383 §87. Not on the 2026 session's list of amended or repealed sections)

> (1) A person commits the offense of unlawful hitchhiking if the person is on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
>
> (2) The offense described in this section, unlawful hitchhiking, is a Class D traffic violation.

**What it means:** The offense is being *on the roadway*, the travel lanes. Stand on the shoulder and you aren't committing it.

### ORS 801.450 and 801.480: "Roadway" and "Shoulder" defined

**Source:** [Oregon State Legislature, ORS chapter 801](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors801.html) (2025 Edition; both 1983 c.338)

> 801.450 Roadway. "Roadway" means the portion of a highway that is improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the shoulder. In the event a highway includes two or more separate roadways the term "roadway" shall refer to any such roadway separately, but not to all such roadways collectively.

> 801.480 Shoulder. "Shoulder" means the portion of a highway, whether paved or unpaved, contiguous to the roadway that is primarily for use by pedestrians, for the accommodation of stopped vehicles, for emergency use and for lateral support of base and surface courses.

**What it means:** By law the shoulder is not roadway, and it is "primarily for use by pedestrians". The shoulder is where a hitchhiker belongs.

### ORS 814.070: Pedestrian position on a highway (hitchhiker exception)

**Source:** [Oregon State Legislature, ORS chapter 814](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors814.html) (2025 Edition; history ends 2013 c.474 §1)

> (1) A pedestrian commits the offense of pedestrian with improper position upon or improperly proceeding along a highway if the pedestrian does any of the following:
> (a) Takes a position upon or proceeds along and upon the roadway where there is an adjacent usable sidewalk or shoulder.
> (b) Does not take a position upon or proceed along and upon the shoulder, as far as practicable from the roadway edge, on a highway that has an adjacent shoulder area on one or both sides.
> (c) Except in the case of the divided highway, does not take a position upon or proceed along and upon the left shoulder and as far as practicable from the roadway edge on a two-way highway that has no sidewalk and that does have an adjacent shoulder area. This paragraph does not apply to:
> (A) A hitchhiker who takes a position upon or proceeds along and upon the right shoulder so long as the hitchhiker does so facing the vehicles using the adjacent lane of the roadway; [...]
> (d) Does not take a position upon or proceed along and upon the right highway shoulder, as far as practicable from the roadway edge, on a divided highway that has no sidewalk and does have a shoulder area. [...]
> (e) Fails to take a position upon or proceed along and upon a highway that has neither sidewalk nor shoulder available, as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway, and, if the roadway is a two-way roadway, only on the left side of it.
> [...]
> (5) The offense described in this section, pedestrian with improper position upon or improperly proceeding along a highway, is a Class D traffic violation.

**What it means:** Pedestrians normally use the left shoulder on two-way roads. Hitchhikers get an explicit exception: you may stand on the right shoulder, on the side where the cars you want are traveling, as long as you face them. Stay as far from the lane edge as practicable. Use a sidewalk if there is one.

### OAR 734-020-0045: Prohibition of bicycles and pedestrians on freeways

**Source:** [Oregon Secretary of State, Oregon Administrative Rules, Chapter 734, Division 20](https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=3305) (last amended HWD 13-2010, f. & cert. ef. 10-25-10)

> (1) Bicycles and pedestrians as defined in ORS 801.150 and 801.385, are prohibited upon the following segments of freeways within the State of Oregon:
> (a) Portland area:
> (A) The Columbia River Highway No. 2 (Banfield/I-84) from its intersection with I-5, M.P. 0.00, to 238th Drive, M.P. 15.96;
> (B) The Sunset Highway No. 47 easterly of the Jefferson Street Interchange, M.P. 73.35;
> (C) Interstate 5 (Hwy. No. 1) northerly of the Beaverton-Tigard Highway Interchange, M.P. 292.20;
> (D) Interstate 205 (Hwy. No. 64) northerly of the Overcrossing of the Oswego Highway No. 3, M.P. 8.82;
> (E) Interstate 405 (Hwy. No. 61) in its entirety; and
> (F) Lower Columbia Highway No. 2W from its intersection with I-405, M.P. 0.00, to 23rd Street, M.P. 1.99.
> (b) Medford area: Interstate 5 (Pacific Highway No. 1) from the South Medford Interchange, M.P. 27.16, to the North Medford Interchange, M.P. 30.29 (in Medford).
>
> (2) The closure of the above sections to bicycles and pedestrians shall become effective following the erection of adequate signing.
>
> (3) The prohibition of pedestrians from the above segments of freeways does not prohibit the driver and passengers of a disabled vehicle from walking to the nearest freeway exit in accordance with ORS 814.100.

**What it means:** Outside these urban segments, no Oregon rule bans pedestrians from freeways. Even so, a hitchhiker in a freeway travel lane is breaking ORS 814.080, and a freeway shoulder is a dangerous place to stand. The freeway on-ramp is the usual spot. Inside Portland and Medford, stay off the listed freeways entirely.

### ORS 810.020 and 811.445: Signed restrictions on throughways

**Source:** [Oregon State Legislature, ORS chapter 810](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors810.html) and [ORS chapter 811](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors811.html) (2025 Edition)

> 810.020 (1) Each road authority may prohibit or restrict the use of a throughway in its jurisdiction by any of the following: [...] (b) Bicycles or other nonmotorized traffic. [...] (2) Regulation under this section becomes effective when appropriate signs giving notice of the regulation are erected upon a throughway and the approaches to the throughway.

> 811.445 (1) A person commits the offense of use of a throughway when prohibited if any use restrictions or prohibitions are posted by appropriate signs giving notice thereof and the person violates any restriction or prohibition so posted. [...] (3) The offense described in this section, use of a throughway when prohibited, is a Class D traffic violation.

**What it means:** Each road authority can post signs closing a throughway to pedestrians. Ignoring such a sign is also a Class D violation.

### ORS 153.018 and 153.019: Fines for a Class D violation

**Source:** [Oregon State Legislature, ORS chapter 153](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors153.html) (2025 Edition)

> 153.018 (1) The penalty for committing a violation is a fine. The law creating a violation may impose other penalties in addition to a fine but may not impose a term of imprisonment.
> (2) Except as otherwise provided by law, the maximum fine for a violation committed by an individual is: [...] (d) $250 for a Class D violation.

> 153.019 (1) Except as provided in ORS 153.020, the presumptive fines for violations are: [...] (d) $115 for a Class D violation.

**What it means:** A hitchhiking ticket is a violation, not a crime. The maximum is $250, the usual (presumptive) fine is $115, and you can't be jailed for it.
