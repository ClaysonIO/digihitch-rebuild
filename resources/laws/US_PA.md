# Pennsylvania (US-PA)

**Status:** Restricted. Pennsylvania bans standing *on a roadway* to solicit a ride. The legal definition of "roadway" excludes the shoulder and berm, so thumbing from the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered by that rule. The Pennsylvania Turnpike is different: hitchhiking and all pedestrian entry onto the Turnpike right-of-way are banned there by regulation.

## Summary

The Vehicle Code (75 Pa.C.S. § 3545) follows the standard Uniform Vehicle Code pattern: "No person shall [...] Stand on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride." Section 102 defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicle travel, "exclusive of the sidewalk, berm or shoulder." So the statewide rule reaches people standing in the travel lanes, not people standing on the shoulder or sidewalk.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Stand on the shoulder, berm or sidewalk, and pick a spot where a driver can pull completely off the road.
- **The Pennsylvania Turnpike is off limits.** Turnpike Commission regulations prohibit hitchhiking and prohibit pedestrians from entering the Turnpike right-of-way at all, and they also prohibit drivers from stopping to let hitchhikers board. Ask for rides on a local road before the Turnpike entrance, not on the Turnpike itself.
- **Other freeways:** The Vehicle Code does not contain a blanket statutory ban on pedestrians on freeways. Instead, § 3313 lets PennDOT prohibit any "class or kind of traffic" on a limited access highway and requires it to post signs at the entrances; once signs are posted, disobeying them is a violation. In practice most interstate ramps carry signs barring pedestrians, so treat a signed ramp as closed to you.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** if there is one; otherwise on the shoulder; otherwise on the left edge facing traffic (§ 3544).

A violation of § 3545 is a summary offense with a $25 fine under § 6502. A Turnpike regulation violation is also a summary offense with a $25 fine.

## Laws

### 75 Pa.C.S. § 3545: Pedestrians soliciting rides or business

**Source:** [Pennsylvania General Assembly, 75 Pa.C.S. § 3545](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=75&div=0&chapter=35&section=45&subsctn=0)

> No person shall:
>
> (1) Stand on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride.
>
> (2) Stand on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (3) Stand on or in proximity to a highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.

**What it means:** You may not stand in the part of the road cars drive on to ask for a ride. The rule says "roadway," not "highway," so standing on the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered (see the definition below).

### 75 Pa.C.S. § 102: Definitions ("Roadway", "Highway", "Limited access highway", "Freeway")

**Source:** [Pennsylvania General Assembly, 75 Pa.C.S. § 102](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=75&div=0&chapter=1&section=2&subsctn=0)

> "Freeway." A limited access highway to which the only means of ingress and egress is by interchange ramps.
>
> [...]
>
> "Highway." The entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained when any part thereof is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel. [...]
>
> [...]
>
> "Limited access highway." A highway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access except at points and in the manner determined by the authority having jurisdiction over the highway.
>
> [...]
>
> "Roadway." That portion of a highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the sidewalk, berm or shoulder even though such sidewalk, berm or shoulder is used by pedalcycles. [...]

**What it means:** The "highway" is the whole public right-of-way, edge to edge. The "roadway" is only the travel lanes, and expressly excludes the shoulder, berm and sidewalk. That is why § 3545 does not reach a hitchhiker standing on the shoulder.

### 75 Pa.C.S. § 3313: Restrictions on use of limited access highways

**Source:** [Pennsylvania General Assembly, 75 Pa.C.S. § 3313](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=75&div=0&chapter=33&section=13&subsctn=0)

> (a) General rule.-- The department may regulate or prohibit the use of any limited access highway by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic.
>
> (b) Traffic-control devices at entrances.-- The department, when adopting any prohibition under this section, shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices at the entrances to the limited access highway on which the prohibitions are applicable and when in place no person shall disobey the restrictions stated on the devices.

**What it means:** PennDOT can close a limited access highway to pedestrians, but it does so with signs at the entrances. If the ramp has a "pedestrians prohibited" sign, walking past it is a violation. Stand before the sign.

### 75 Pa.C.S. § 3544: Pedestrians walking along or on highway

**Source:** [Pennsylvania General Assembly, 75 Pa.C.S. § 3544](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=75&div=0&chapter=35&section=44&subsctn=0)

> (a) Mandatory use of available sidewalk.-- Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable, it is unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (b) Absence of sidewalk.-- Where a sidewalk is not available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk only on a shoulder as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (c) Absence of sidewalk and shoulder.-- Where neither a sidewalk nor a shoulder is available, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall walk as near as practicable to an outside edge of the roadway and, if on a two-way roadway, shall walk only on the left side of the roadway.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk if there is one; otherwise the shoulder; otherwise walk on the left edge facing traffic.

### 67 Pa. Code §§ 601.10, 601.11: Pennsylvania Turnpike pedestrian travel and hitchhiking

**Source:** [Pennsylvania Code, 67 Pa. Code Chapter 601 (Turnpike Commission traffic regulations)](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/067/chapter601/chap601toc.html)

> § 601.10. Pedestrian travel.
>
> Pedestrian travel or entry upon the right-of-way of the Turnpike System is prohibited.
>
> § 601.11. Hitchhiking.
>
> The solicitation for a ride, commonly known as "hitchhiking," is prohibited, and stopping or allowing a vehicle to be standing while hitchhikers board is also prohibited.

From § 601.1 (Definitions):

> Turnpike System—The limited access highway maintained and constructed by the Commission, extending eastward from the Ohio line to the New Jersey line and northward from a point near Norristown, Pennsylvania to a point near Scranton, Pennsylvania and other extensions thereto, which have been or may hereafter be designated as part of the Turnpike System by the Legislature.

**What it means:** On the Turnpike System, you may not walk on the right-of-way at all and you may not hitchhike, and drivers may not stop to pick you up. This includes the shoulders and ramps inside the Turnpike's boundaries.

### 75 Pa.C.S. § 6502 and 67 Pa. Code § 601.16: Penalties

**Source:** [Pennsylvania General Assembly, 75 Pa.C.S. § 6502](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=75&div=0&chapter=65&section=2&subsctn=0); [67 Pa. Code § 601.16](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/067/chapter601/chap601toc.html)

> (a) Violations of this title.-- It is a summary offense for any person to violate any of the provisions of this title unless the violation is by this title or other statute of this Commonwealth declared to be a misdemeanor or felony. Every person convicted of a summary offense for a violation of any of the provisions of this title for which another penalty is not provided shall be sentenced to pay a fine of $25.

> § 601.16. Penalties.
>
> A person violating a provision of this part for which no penalty has otherwise been provided by statute is guilty of a summary offense and shall, upon conviction, be sentenced to pay a fine of $25.

**What it means:** Breaking § 3545, § 3544, a § 3313 sign, or the Turnpike rules is a summary offense (the lowest level, like a traffic ticket) with a $25 base fine.
