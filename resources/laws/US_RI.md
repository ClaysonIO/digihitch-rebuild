# Rhode Island (US-RI)

**Status:** Restricted. Hitchhiking is banned anywhere on a freeway. On other roads it is banned only in the roadway or traveled portion, so the shoulder is allowed. Each violation carries a $100 civil fine.

## Summary

Rhode Island has two hitchhiking statutes:

- **§ 24-10-17(a): freeways.** Trying "by words, gestures, or otherwise" to get a ride "on any freeway" is prohibited. The ban is not limited to the travel lanes.
- **§ 24-10-17(b) and § 31-18-12: all other roads.** You may not solicit a ride on the "traveled portion" of a public highway (§ 24-10-17(b)) or "stand in a roadway" to solicit one (§ 31-18-12). Section 31-1-23(h) defines "roadway" to exclude the berm and the shoulder.

So on ordinary roads, standing on the shoulder or curb with your thumb out is lawful. On a freeway, including its shoulders and, presumably, its ramps (see open questions), it is not.

Both sections carry a $100 fine on the Traffic Tribunal violations schedule (§ 31-41.1-4). In 2021 the General Assembly removed the words "shall be guilty of a misdemeanor" from § 24-10-17 (see History), so freeway hitchhiking is no longer a misdemeanor.

Pedestrians may not cross a freeway except in an emergency (§ 31-18-17). No Rhode Island statute was found that bans walking along a freeway, but soliciting a ride there is banned anyway.

## Laws

### R.I. Gen. Laws § 24-10-17: Soliciting rides in motor vehicles

**Source:** [Rhode Island General Assembly, R.I. Gen. Laws § 24-10-17](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE24/24-10/24-10-17.htm)

> (a) Any person who endeavors by words, gestures, or otherwise to beg, invite, or secure transportation in any motor vehicle on any freeway within the state, except in the case of a bona fide emergency or in the case of sickness, is subject to fines enumerated in § 31-41.1-4.
> (b) Any person who endeavors to solicit a ride in a motor vehicle in the manner described in this section on the traveled portion of any other public highway in this state shall be subject to fines enumerated in § 31-41.1-4.
>
> History of Section. P.L. 1966, ch. 69, § 1; P.L. 2002, ch. 58, § 1; P.L. 2002, ch. 292, § 102; P.L. 2008, ch. 100, art. 12, § 4; P.L. 2021, ch. 49, § 1, effective June 14, 2021; P.L. 2021, ch. 50, § 1, effective June 11, 2021.

**What it means:** On a freeway (for example the interstates), any attempt to get a ride is an offense, wherever you stand. On other public roads, only soliciting from the traveled portion is an offense. Stay on the shoulder.

### R.I. Gen. Laws § 24-10-1: "Freeway" defined

**Source:** [Rhode Island General Assembly, R.I. Gen. Laws § 24-10-1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE24/24-10/24-10-1.htm)

> A "freeway" is a way especially designed for through traffic over which abutters have no easement or right of light, air, or access by reason of the fact that their property abuts upon the way.

**What it means:** A "freeway" is a limited-access highway that adjoining properties cannot connect to directly. Interstates and expressways qualify.

### R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-18-12: Hitchhiking in road

**Source:** [Rhode Island General Assembly, R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-18-12](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE31/31-18/31-18-12.htm)

> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle. Violations of this section are subject to fines enumerated in § 31-41.1-4.
>
> History of Section. P.L. 1950, ch. 2595, art. 29, § 6; G.L. 1956, § 31-18-12; P.L. 2002, ch. 292, § 113.

**What it means:** This is the standard Uniform Vehicle Code rule. Don't stand in the travel lanes to hitchhike.

### R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-1-23(h): "Roadway"

**Source:** [Rhode Island General Assembly, R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-1-23](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE31/31-1/31-1-23.htm)

> (h) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, excluding the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder even when used by persons riding bicycles. In the event a highway includes two (2) or more separate roadways, "roadway" refers to the roadway separately and not the roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder, berm and sidewalk are not "roadway". Hitchhiking from them is outside § 31-18-12, except on freeways, where § 24-10-17(a) applies everywhere.

### R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-18-17: Pedestrians on freeways

**Source:** [Rhode Island General Assembly, R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-18-17](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE31/31-18/31-18-17.htm)

> Any pedestrian who shall cross any freeway as defined by § 24-10-1, except in an emergency or to render assistance in case of an accident or unforeseen cause, shall be deemed to be guilty of a civil violation.

**What it means:** Crossing a freeway on foot is a civil violation. The statute does not mention walking along one, but you cannot hitchhike there in any case.

### R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-41.1-4: Schedule of violations

**Source:** [Rhode Island General Assembly, R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-41.1-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE31/31-41.1/31-41.1-4.htm)

> (a) The penalties for violations of the enumerated sections, listed in numerical order, correspond to the fines described. [...]
>
> 24-10-17 Soliciting rides in motor vehicles 100.00
> [...]
> 31-18-12 Hitchhiking 100.00

**What it means:** Hitchhiking where it is prohibited is a $100 civil fine, payable through the Traffic Tribunal.

## History

### 2021: "guilty of a misdemeanor" removed from § 24-10-17

**Source:** [P.L. 2021, ch. 49 (2021-S 0681), enacted 06/14/2021](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law21/law21049.htm) and [P.L. 2021, ch. 50 (2021-H 5977), enacted 06/11/2021](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws/law21/law21050.htm)

The enacted text of chapter 49 shows these words struck through in both subsections (a) and (b) of § 24-10-17: "shall be guilty of a misdemeanor. Violations of this section are". The sentence now ends "is subject to fines enumerated in § 31-41.1-4" (a) and "shall be subject to fines enumerated in § 31-41.1-4" (b).

**What it means:** Older guides that call Rhode Island freeway hitchhiking a misdemeanor describe the pre-2021 law. It is now a civil traffic fine.

## Unverified / open questions

- **Ramps:** Whether freeway on-ramps and off-ramps count as part of the "freeway" under § 24-10-17(a) was not confirmed by statute or case law. Assume they do.
- **Pedestrians walking along freeways:** No statute or RIDOT regulation banning this was found. Posted signs may apply.
- **Local ordinances** were not researched.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** On ordinary roads you may solicit a ride from the shoulder, berm or sidewalk, but not from the roadway. Hitchhiking is banned anywhere on a freeway, shoulders included; treat ramps as part of the freeway.

> **R.I. Gen. Laws § 24-10-17(a), (b)**
> (a) Any person who endeavors by words, gestures, or otherwise to beg, invite, or secure transportation in any motor vehicle on any freeway within the state, except in the case of a bona fide emergency or in the case of sickness, is subject to fines enumerated in § 31-41.1-4.
> (b) Any person who endeavors to solicit a ride in a motor vehicle in the manner described in this section on the traveled portion of any other public highway in this state shall be subject to fines enumerated in § 31-41.1-4.

> **R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-18-12**
> No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the driver of any vehicle.

> **R.I. Gen. Laws § 31-1-23(h)**
> (h) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, excluding the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder even when used by persons riding bicycles.
