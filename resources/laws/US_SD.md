# South Dakota (US-SD)

**Status:** Legal. No South Dakota statute prohibits soliciting a ride. Pedestrians can be banned from controlled-access highways only by a Transportation Commission rule (or a local ordinance) plus posted signs.

## Summary

South Dakota is one of the few states without the Uniform Vehicle Code "no person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride" section. Chapter 32-27 (Pedestrians' Rights and Duties) has no soliciting provision. A search of Title 32 (Motor Vehicles) and the highway chapters found no hitchhiking statute.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Walk facing traffic** on the left side of the road or its shoulder where there is no sidewalk (a petty offense if you don't).
- **Interstates (I-90, I-29, I-190, I-229):** the Transportation Commission may ban pedestrians from a controlled-access roadway by formal rule, and local authorities may do so by ordinance. Once signs are erected, disobeying them is a Class 2 misdemeanor. I found no such Transportation Commission rule in the traffic-safety chapter of the Administrative Rules (ARSD 70:01:03). Obey any sign you see at a ramp.
- **Don't block traffic.** Obstructing vehicular or pedestrian traffic can be charged as disorderly conduct (SDCL 22-18-35).
- **Cities can have their own rules.** For example, Sioux Falls reportedly bans standing in a roadway to solicit a ride (not verified here).

## Laws

### SDCL § 32-27-5: Highways without sidewalks, duty to walk facing traffic

**Source:** [South Dakota Legislature, SDCL 32-27-5](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/32-27-5) (official; source: SL 1961, ch 151; SL 1989, ch 255, § 187)

> Where sidewalks are not provided any pedestrian walking along or upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic which may approach from the opposite direction. A violation of this section is a petty offense.

**What it means:** Walk against traffic. Standing still on the shoulder with your thumb out is not addressed by any statute.

### SDCL § 32-26-11: Prohibition of pedestrians on controlled-access highway

**Source:** [South Dakota Legislature, SDCL 32-26-11](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/32-26-11) (official; source: SL 1961, ch 150, § 3; SL 1987, ch 210, § 35; SL 1989, ch 255, §§ 163, 182)

> The transportation commission may, by rule adopted pursuant to chapter 1-26, and local authorities may by ordinance, with respect to any controlled-access roadway under their respective jurisdictions, prohibit the use of any such roadway by pedestrians, bicycles, or other nonmotorized traffic or by any person operating a motor-driven cycle. The Department of Transportation or the local authority adopting any such prohibitory regulation shall erect and maintain official signs on the controlled-access roadway on which such regulations are applicable. If so erected no person may disobey the restrictions stated on such signs. The violation for failure to obey a regulatory sign under this section is a Class 2 misdemeanor.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians on interstates outright. A ban requires a formal rule (or a city or county ordinance) and signs. Where a sign prohibits pedestrians, disobeying it is a Class 2 misdemeanor: up to 30 days in jail and/or a $500 fine (SDCL 22-6-2). The safest place to hitchhike is at the start of the on-ramp.

### SDCL § 32-14-1: Definitions

**Source:** [South Dakota Legislature, SDCL 32-14-1](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/32-14-1)

> (11) "Highway," the entire width between the boundary lines of every way publicly maintained when any part thereof is open to the use of the public as a matter of right for purposes of vehicular travel;
> [...]
> (27) "Roadway," that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. If a highway includes two or more separate roadways, the term, roadway, refers to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively;

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway. This matters for any local ordinance written in "roadway" terms.

### SDCL § 22-18-35: Disorderly conduct

**Source:** [South Dakota Legislature, SDCL 22-18-35](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/22-18-35)

> Any person who intentionally causes serious public inconvenience, annoyance, or alarm to any other person, or creates a risk thereof by: [...] (4) Obstructing vehicular or pedestrian traffic; is guilty of disorderly conduct. Disorderly conduct is a Class 2 misdemeanor. [...]

**What it means:** Hitchhiking itself is legal. Deliberately getting in the way of traffic, for example by stepping into the lane to make cars stop, can be charged as disorderly conduct.

### SDCL § 22-6-2: Misdemeanor classes and penalties

**Source:** [South Dakota Legislature, SDCL 22-6-2](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/22-6-2)

> (2) Class 2 misdemeanor: thirty days imprisonment in a county jail or five hundred dollars fine, or both.

**What it means:** This is the maximum penalty for ignoring a posted freeway pedestrian ban under § 32-26-11.

## Notable local rules

Search results point to a Sioux Falls ordinance making it unlawful "to stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the operator of any private vehicle". I could not retrieve the primary text (the American Legal Publishing code library refused automated access), so it is not quoted here. Check city rules before hitchhiking inside Sioux Falls or Rapid City.

## Unverified / open questions

- I found no Transportation Commission rule under § 32-26-11 in ARSD chapter 70:01:03 (Traffic safety). I did not search every chapter of ARSD Title 70. If DOT has posted "pedestrians prohibited" signs at interstate ramps without a rule, their enforceability is unclear. That is why `freeway_pedestrians` is marked `unclear`.
- SDCL 32-27-5.1 was "Repealed by SL 1989, ch 255, § 183". I could not determine what it said.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** Hitchhiking is legal: no South Dakota statute bans soliciting a ride. Stay out of the travel lanes, don't block traffic, and obey any posted sign banning pedestrians from an interstate.
