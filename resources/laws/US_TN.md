# Tennessee (US-TN)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in a roadway to solicit a ride. Tennessee defines "roadway" to exclude the berm or shoulder. Hitchhiking is banned outright inside Tennessee state parks and natural areas. A 2019 law bars loitering in or near the median of a state highway.

## Summary

Tenn. Code Ann. § 55-8-139(a)(1) reads: "No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride or employment from the occupant of any vehicle." Tennessee's definition of "roadway" excludes the berm or shoulder, so standing on the shoulder or curb is outside this rule.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of medians.** Since 2019, § 55-8-139(c) bans loitering "in, or in proximity to, the median of a state highway." A first offense gets a warning citation.
- **Walk on the left, facing traffic,** on the road or its shoulder where there's no sidewalk (§ 55-8-138). Use sidewalks where they exist.
- **No hitchhiking in state parks and natural areas.** A state parks rule says "Hitchhiking or the soliciting of transportation is prohibited."
- **Interstates:** The Tennessee Department of Transportation (TDOT) says pedestrians may use any road "that is not access controlled (like an interstate)." The statute or rule behind that was not found in this research. Treat interstates as off limits.

A violation of § 55-8-139 is a Class C misdemeanor, punishable by up to 30 days in jail, a fine of up to $50, or both.

## Laws

### Tenn. Code Ann. § 55-8-139: Soliciting rides, employment, and median loitering

**Source:** [FindLaw, Tennessee Code § 55-8-139](https://codes.findlaw.com/tn/title-55-motor-and-other-vehicles/tn-code-sect-55-8-139.html) (unofficial mirror, marked "Current as of January 02, 2024"). Tennessee's official code is published by LexisNexis and was not retrieved.

> (a)(1) No person shall stand in a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride or employment from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (2) It is an offense for an occupant of a vehicle to park, leave standing, or stop the vehicle on a street or highway and to block or impede the normal movement of traffic for the purpose of soliciting, negotiating, or consummating an employment transaction with a person standing on or in proximity to the street or highway.
>
> (b) No person shall stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.
>
> (c) No person shall loiter or conduct any commercial activity in, or in proximity to, the median of a state highway.
>
> [...]
>
> (e) A violation of this section is a Class C misdemeanor; except, that:
> (1) A person who violates subsection (c) must receive a warning citation for a first offense; and
> (2) If a vehicle parked, left standing, or stopped on a street or highway in violation of subdivision (a)(2) causes or contributes to a motor vehicle accident, then a violation of this section is a Class B misdemeanor.

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes to hitch. The shoulder is outside the "roadway." Don't stand in the median of a state highway. Subsection (a)(2) targets drivers who stop in traffic to hire day laborers, not drivers who pick up hitchhikers.

### Tenn. Code Ann. § 55-8-101: Definitions ("roadway," "highway," "controlled-access highway")

**Source:** [FindLaw, Tennessee Code § 55-8-101](https://codes.findlaw.com/tn/title-55-motor-and-other-vehicles/tn-code-sect-55-8-101.html) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of January 02, 2024")

> (15) "Controlled-access highway" means every highway, street or roadway in respect to which owners or occupants of abutting lands and other persons have no legal right of access to or from the same, except at such points only and in such manner as may be determined by the public authority having jurisdiction over the highway, street or roadway;
>
> [...]
>
> (30) "Highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every way when any part thereto is open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel;
>
> [...]
>
> (68) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two (2) or more separate roadways, "roadway" refers to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively;

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the "roadway," so the ride-solicitation ban doesn't reach it.

### Tenn. Code Ann. § 55-8-138: Pedestrians on roadways

**Source:** [FindLaw, Tennessee Code § 55-8-138](https://codes.findlaw.com/tn/title-55-motor-and-other-vehicles/tn-code-sect-55-8-138.html) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of January 02, 2024")

> (a) Except as provided in this section, where sidewalks are provided, it is unlawful for any pedestrian to walk or use a wheelchair along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (b) Where sidewalks are not provided or are obstructed, any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic that may approach from the opposite direction.
>
> [...]
>
> (d) A violation of this section is a Class C misdemeanor.

**What it means:** Use sidewalks where they exist. Otherwise walk on the left, facing oncoming traffic.

### Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0400-02-02-.04: Begging and soliciting (state parks and natural areas)

**Source:** [Cornell LII, Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0400-02-02-.04](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/tennessee/Tenn-Comp-R-Regs-0400-02-02-.04) (unofficial mirror; Division of State Parks and Division of Natural Areas, Chapter 0400-02-02 "Public Use and Recreation"; amended effective September 12, 2010)

> (1) Begging is prohibited.
> (2) Hitchhiking or the soliciting of transportation is prohibited.

**What it means:** Inside Tennessee state parks and state natural areas, you may not hitchhike at all, even from the shoulder.

## History

### 2019: median loitering ban (Pub. Ch. 184)

**Source:** [Tennessee General Assembly, HB 0832 (111th General Assembly) bill history](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default.aspx?BillNumber=HB0832&GA=111)

The bill's summary reads: "As enacted, prohibits persons from loitering or conducting any commercial activity in, or in proximity to, the median of a state highway; creates a Class C misdemeanor offense that requires issuance of a warning citation for a first offense." It became Public Chapter 184, signed May 1, 2019, effective July 1, 2019. Some secondary websites attribute the warning-citation rule to a 2025 public chapter. The bill history shows it dates from 2019.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** Stand on the shoulder or curb, not in the roadway; Tennessee's "roadway" excludes the shoulder. Don't loiter in state-highway medians. No hitchhiking in state parks or natural areas; treat interstates as off limits.
