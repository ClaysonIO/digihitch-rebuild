# Texas (US-TX)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand *in a roadway* to solicit a ride. The roadway does not include the shoulder, so thumbing from the shoulder or curb is not covered by this rule. Pedestrians can be banned from controlled-access highways where signs are posted.

## Summary

Texas follows the standard Uniform Vehicle Code rule: "A person may not stand in a roadway to solicit a ride." Texas defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for travel, "other than the berm or shoulder". Standing on the shoulder, the curb or the sidewalk to hitchhike is therefore not banned by state law. Standing in a travel lane is.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** Standing in the roadway to solicit a ride is a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of $1 to $200.
- **Freeways:** the Texas Transportation Commission (on state highways) and cities or counties (on their own roads) may ban pedestrians from limited-access or controlled-access highways. The ban applies where official signs are posted, so check the signs at your spot.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** if there is one. Otherwise walk on the left side of the road or shoulder, facing traffic.

## Laws

### Tex. Transp. Code § 552.007: Solicitation by pedestrians

**Source:** [Texas Legislative Council, Transportation Code ch. 552](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.552.htm) (official statute text, file last modified April 2026; section unchanged since Acts 1995, 74th Leg., ch. 165)

> (a) A person may not stand in a roadway to solicit a ride, contribution, employment, or business from an occupant of a vehicle, except that a person may stand in a roadway to solicit a charitable contribution if authorized to do so by the local authority having jurisdiction over the roadway.
>
> (b) A person may not stand on or near a highway to solicit the watching or guarding of a vehicle parked or to be parked on the highway.

**What it means:** Don't step into the lanes to ask for a ride. The rule covers only the "roadway", and Texas law excludes the shoulder from the roadway (see § 541.302 below). Standing on the shoulder or curb with a sign or your thumb out is not prohibited by this section. Make sure a driver can pull fully off the road to pick you up.

### Tex. Transp. Code § 552.0071(g): Roadway for charity solicitation

**Source:** [Texas Legislative Council, Transportation Code ch. 552](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.552.htm)

> (g) For purposes of a solicitation under Subsection (a), a roadway is defined to include the roadbed, shoulder, median, curbs, safety zones, sidewalks, and utility easements located adjacent to or near the roadway.

**What it means:** This broader "roadway" definition applies only to local permits for charity collections (such as firefighters' boot drives). It does not change the general definition that applies to ride solicitation.

### Tex. Transp. Code § 541.302: Traffic areas (definitions)

**Source:** [Texas Legislative Council, Transportation Code ch. 541](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.541.htm)

> (5) "Highway or street" means the width between the boundary lines of a publicly maintained way any part of which is open to the public for vehicular travel.
> [...]
> (11) "Roadway" means the portion of a highway, other than the berm or shoulder, that is improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel. If a highway includes at least two separate roadways, the term applies to each roadway separately.
> [...]
> (15) "Shoulder" means the portion of a highway that is: (A) adjacent to the roadway; (B) designed or ordinarily used for parking; (C) distinguished from the roadway by different design, construction, or marking; and (D) not intended for normal vehicular travel.

**What it means:** The shoulder is part of the "highway" but not the "roadway". The ride-solicitation ban in § 552.007(a) covers only the roadway.

### Tex. Transp. Code § 552.006: Use of sidewalk

**Source:** [Texas Legislative Council, Transportation Code ch. 552](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.552.htm) (last amended by Acts 2023, 88th Leg., R.S., Ch. 815 (H.B. 1277), eff. September 1, 2023)

> (a) A pedestrian may not walk along and on a roadway if an adjacent sidewalk is provided and is accessible to the pedestrian.
>
> (b) If a sidewalk is not provided, a pedestrian walking along and on a highway shall walk on the left side of the roadway or the shoulder of the highway facing oncoming traffic, unless the left side of the roadway or the shoulder of the highway facing oncoming traffic is obstructed or unsafe.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk where there is one. Otherwise walk facing traffic on the left side of the road or shoulder.

### Tex. Transp. Code § 545.065: State and local regulation of limited-access or controlled-access highways

**Source:** [Texas Legislative Council, Transportation Code ch. 545](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.545.htm)

> (a) The Texas Transportation Commission by resolution or order recorded in its minutes may prohibit the use of a limited-access or controlled-access highway under the jurisdiction of the commission by a parade, funeral procession, pedestrian, bicycle, electric bicycle, motorcycle equipped with a motor that has an engine piston displacement of 250 cubic centimeters or less, or nonmotorized traffic.
>
> (b) If the commission adopts a rule under Subsection (a), the commission shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the portions of the limited-access or controlled-access highway to which the rule applies.
>
> (c) A local authority by ordinance may prohibit the use of a limited-access or controlled-access roadway under the jurisdiction of the authority by a parade, funeral procession, pedestrian, bicycle, electric bicycle, motorcycle equipped with a motor that has an engine piston displacement of 250 cubic centimeters or less, or nonmotorized traffic.
>
> (d) If a local authority adopts an ordinance under Subsection (c), the authority shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the portions of the limited-access or controlled-access roadway to which the ordinance applies.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians from every Texas freeway. The state or a city can ban them on a particular freeway, and the ban is marked by signs. If you see a "pedestrians prohibited" sign at a ramp, stand before the sign, not past it. Frontage roads are not part of the freeway itself.

### Tex. Transp. Code §§ 542.301 and 542.401: Offense and penalty

**Source:** [Texas Legislative Council, Transportation Code ch. 542](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.542.htm)

> Sec. 542.301. GENERAL OFFENSE. (a) A person commits an offense if the person performs an act prohibited or fails to perform an act required by this subtitle. (b) Except as otherwise provided, an offense under this subtitle is a misdemeanor.
>
> Sec. 542.401. GENERAL PENALTY. A person convicted of an offense that is a misdemeanor under this subtitle for which another penalty is not provided shall be punished by a fine of not less than $1 or more than $200.

**What it means:** Soliciting a ride from the roadway, or walking on a posted freeway, is a fine-only misdemeanor of $1 to $200, plus court costs.

## Unverified / open questions

- I did not retrieve any specific Texas Transportation Commission minute orders that ban pedestrians on particular freeways. The signs on the ground are the operative notice.
- Toll roads run by regional authorities (for example NTTA, HCTRA, CTRMA) may have their own rules. I did not research them.
- City ordinances on soliciting from roadways and medians (Houston, Dallas, San Antonio, Austin and others) were not reviewed.
