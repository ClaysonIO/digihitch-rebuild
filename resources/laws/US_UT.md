# Utah (US-UT)

**Status:** Legal. No Utah statute currently prohibits soliciting a ride. Walking on no-access freeways is prohibited, and you may not impede or block traffic on interstates, freeways, state highways, or fast roads.

## Summary

Utah no longer has a statewide ban on hitchhiking. Before 2017, Utah Code § 41-6a-1009(4) listed "soliciting rides, contributions, or other business" as conduct that may impede or block traffic. The 2017 General Session (H.B. 161, "Pedestrian Safety Amendments") struck that wording. The section now targets handing money or property between pedestrians and occupants of moving traffic.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Don't walk on no-access freeways** (interstates and other limited-access highways) unless there is an emergency. Stand at the on-ramp, before the freeway begins.
- **Don't impede or block traffic** on interstates, freeways, state highways or routes, or highways that are paved with a speed limit of 35 mph or more, that have a median, or that share the right-of-way with a railway. This includes shoulders, on-ramps, off-ramps and medians.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** if there is one. Otherwise walk on the shoulder, or on the left edge facing traffic.
- **Cities and counties may add their own rules** for the locations listed in subsection (4).

A violation is an infraction. A third violation of subsection (4) within one year is a class C misdemeanor.

## Laws

### Utah Code § 41-6a-1009: Use of roadway by pedestrians -- Prohibited activities

**Source:** [Utah Legislature, Utah Code § 41-6a-1009](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title41/Chapter6A/41-6a-S1009.html) (version effective 5/8/2018, amended by Chapter 122, 2018 General Session)

> (1) Where there is a sidewalk provided and its use is practicable, a pedestrian may not walk along or on an adjacent roadway.
>
> (2) Where a sidewalk is not provided, a pedestrian walking along or on a highway shall walk only on the shoulder, as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (3) Where a sidewalk or a shoulder is not available, a pedestrian walking along or on a highway shall:
> (a) walk as near as practicable to the outside edge of the roadway; and
> (b) if on a two-way roadway, walk only on the left side of the roadway facing traffic.

**What it means:** If there is a sidewalk, walk on it. If there isn't one, walk on the shoulder. If there's no shoulder, walk on the left side of the road facing traffic.

> (4)(a) An individual may not impede or block traffic within any of the following:
> (i) an interstate system, as defined in Section 72-1-102;
> (ii) a freeway, as defined in Section 41-6a-102;
> (iii) a state highway, as defined in Title 72, Chapter 4, Designation of State Highways Act;
> (iv) a state route, or "SR," as defined in Section 72-1-102; or
> (v) a highway, as defined in Section 72-1-102, that:
> (A) is paved and has a speed limit of 35 miles per hour or higher;
> (B) has a median, whether elevated or flat; or
> (C) has a fixed guideway as defined in Section 59-12-102 or any other railway that shares the highway right-of-way.
>
> (b) The locations described in Subsection (4)(a) include:
> (i) shoulder areas, as defined in Section 41-6a-102;
> (ii) on-ramps;
> (iii) off-ramps; and
> (iv) an area between the roadways of a divided highway, as defined in Section 41-6a-102.
>
> (c) The locations described in Subsection (4)(a) do not include sidewalks, as defined in Section 41-6a-102.
>
> (d) Conduct that may impede or block traffic includes:
> (i) while a pedestrian, accepting, transacting, exchanging, or otherwise taking possession or control of money or property from a person within a motor vehicle while that motor vehicle is within an area described in Subsection (4)(a); [...]
>
> (f) A county or municipality may adopt a resolution, ordinance, or regulation prohibiting conduct in locations described in Subsections (4)(a) and (b) within any of the roadways under its jurisdiction.

**What it means:** Standing on a shoulder or ramp with your thumb out is not listed as prohibited conduct. Stepping into traffic or making cars stop in a travel lane is. Pick a spot where a driver can pull completely off the road. Local ordinances can be stricter (see (4)(f)), so check city rules in places like Salt Lake City.

> (7) A pedestrian may not walk along or on a no-access freeway facility except during an emergency.

**What it means:** Don't walk on interstates or other limited-access freeways. Hitchhike from the on-ramp entrance or a nearby gas station, not from the freeway shoulder.

> (9)(a) Except as provided in Subsection (9)(b), a violation of this section is an infraction.
> (b) A third or subsequent violation of Subsection (4) in a one-year period is a class C misdemeanor.

**What it means:** A first or second violation is an infraction, which carries a fine and no jail. Repeat violations of the traffic-blocking rule within a year become a class C misdemeanor.

## History

### 2017 H.B. 161: "soliciting rides" removed

**Source:** [Utah Legislature, 2017 H.B. 161 (enrolled), Pedestrian Safety Amendments](https://le.utah.gov/~2017/bills/hbillenr/HB0161.htm)

Before this bill, § 41-6a-1009(4)(d) read: "Conduct that impedes or blocks traffic may include: (i) loitering; (ii) demonstrating or picketing; (iii) distributing materials; (iv) gathering signatures; (v) holding signs; or (vi) soliciting rides, contributions, or other business." The bill struck that whole list and replaced it with the money and property exchange language quoted above.

**What it means:** Older guides that say "Utah bans soliciting rides on highways" describe the pre-2017 law.
