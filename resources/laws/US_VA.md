# Virginia (US-VA)

**Status:** Restricted. Virginia bans standing or stopping *in any roadway* to solicit rides. The Code defines "roadway" as excluding the shoulder, so hitchhiking from the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered by that rule. Pedestrians may be barred from controlled access highways where the Commonwealth Transportation Board has restricted them and posted markers.

## Summary

Va. Code § 46.2-929 is one sentence: "Pedestrians shall not stand or stop in any roadway for the purpose of soliciting rides." Section 46.2-100 defines "roadway" as the part of the highway used for vehicle travel, "exclusive of the shoulder." So the ban covers the travel lanes, not the shoulder.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay out of the travel lanes.** The shoulder or sidewalk is outside § 46.2-929. Pick a spot with room for a car to pull fully off the road.
- **Interstates and other controlled access highways:** § 46.2-808 lets the Commonwealth Transportation Board prohibit pedestrians on controlled access highways "or any part thereof," with conspicuous markers at the ends of the restricted section. Where those markers are posted, walking past them is a traffic infraction. Ask for rides before the sign.
- **Posted no-loitering zones.** § 46.2-930 bans loitering on bridges or parts of the right-of-way where state or local officials have posted signs.
- **Walking along the road:** use the sidewalk; otherwise walk on the left facing traffic, or on either shoulder if it is wide enough (§ 46.2-928).
- **Penalty:** a traffic infraction (not a crime), fine of up to $250 (§ 46.2-113 with § 18.2-11).

## Laws

### Va. Code § 46.2-929: Pedestrians soliciting rides

**Source:** [Code of Virginia, § 46.2-929 (Virginia Law Library, law.lis.virginia.gov)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter8/section46.2-929/)

> Pedestrians shall not stand or stop in any roadway for the purpose of soliciting rides.

**What it means:** You may not stand in a traffic lane to hitchhike. Because "roadway" excludes the shoulder (see below), standing on the shoulder or sidewalk is not covered by this section.

### Va. Code § 46.2-100: Definitions ("Highway", "Roadway", "Shoulder")

**Source:** [Code of Virginia, § 46.2-100](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter1/section46.2-100/)

> "Highway" means the entire width between the boundary lines of every way or place open to the use of the public for purposes of vehicular travel in the Commonwealth, including the streets and alleys, [...]
>
> "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the shoulder. A highway may include two or more roadways if divided by a physical barrier or barriers or an unpaved area.
>
> "Shoulder" means that part of a highway between the portion regularly traveled by vehicular traffic and the lateral curbline or ditch.

**What it means:** The shoulder is part of the "highway" but not part of the "roadway." The hitchhiking rule only reaches the roadway.

### Va. Code § 46.2-808: Commonwealth Transportation Board may prohibit certain uses of controlled access highways; penalty

**Source:** [Code of Virginia, § 46.2-808](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter8/section46.2-808/)

> A. The Commonwealth Transportation Board may, when necessary to promote safety, prohibit the use of controlled access highways or any part thereof by any or all of the following:
>
> 1. Pedestrians,
>
> [...]
>
> B. The termini of any section of controlled access highways, use of which is restricted under the provisions of this section, shall be clearly indicated by a conspicuous marker.
>
> [...] Any person violating a restriction or prohibition imposed pursuant to this section shall be guilty of a traffic infraction.

**What it means:** There is no blanket statute banning pedestrians from every Virginia freeway. Instead, the state bans them section by section and marks the restricted sections with signs. If you see a "pedestrians prohibited" sign at a ramp, stay behind it.

### Va. Code § 46.2-930: Loitering on bridges or highway rights-of-way

**Source:** [Code of Virginia, § 46.2-930](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter8/section46.2-930/)

> Pedestrians shall not loiter on any bridge or in any portion of the right-of-way of any highway where loitering has been determined by the Commissioner of Highways or the local governing body of any county, city, or town to present a public safety hazard and on which the Commissioner of Highways or the governing body of any county, city, or town has posted signs prohibiting such action. [...] Any person violating the provisions of this section shall be guilty of a traffic infraction.

**What it means:** Where there is a posted "no loitering" sign on a bridge or roadside, waiting there for a ride can be a violation even on the shoulder.

### Va. Code § 46.2-928: Pedestrians not to use roadway except when necessary; keeping to left

**Source:** [Code of Virginia, § 46.2-928](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter8/section46.2-928/) (amended 2026, c. 431)

> Pedestrians shall not use the roadways for travel, except when necessary to do so because of the absence of sidewalks which are reasonably suitable and passable for their use. If they walk on the hard surface, or the main travelled portion of the roadway, they shall keep to the extreme left side or edge thereof, or where the shoulders of the highway are of sufficient width to permit, they may walk on either shoulder thereof. Pedestrians walking on a roadway shall face oncoming traffic. However, when walking on a roadway that is part of a highway divided by a physical barrier or barriers or an unpaved area, and when there are no shoulders of the highway present, pedestrians may keep to the extreme right side or edge of the roadway, regardless of the direction of traffic they face.

**What it means:** Use the sidewalk. Without one, walk on the far left facing traffic, or on either shoulder if it is wide enough. On a divided road with no shoulder, you may keep to the right edge.

### Va. Code § 46.2-113 and § 18.2-11: Penalties

**Source:** [Code of Virginia, § 46.2-113](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter1/section46.2-113/); [Code of Virginia, § 18.2-11](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title18.2/chapter1/section18.2-11/)

> It is unlawful for any person to violate any of the provisions of this title, or any regulation adopted pursuant to this title, or local ordinances adopted pursuant to the authority granted in this title. Unless otherwise stated, these violations shall constitute traffic infractions punishable by a fine of not more than that provided for a Class 4 misdemeanor under § 18.2-11. [...]

> (d) For Class 4 misdemeanors, a fine of not more than $250.

**What it means:** Hitchhiking from a travel lane, or walking past a freeway pedestrian ban, is a traffic infraction with a maximum fine of $250. It is not a criminal misdemeanor.

**What's allowed:** You may hitchhike from the shoulder or sidewalk; Virginia only bans standing or stopping in the roadway (travel lanes) to solicit rides. Don't pass markers barring pedestrians from controlled access highways.
