# Vermont (US-VT)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not stand in the roadway to solicit a ride, and the shoulder is not part of the roadway. On limited-access highways (the interstates and their ramps), a Traffic Committee rule with the force of law bans both hitchhiking and pedestrians.

## Summary

Vermont's statute, 23 V.S.A. § 1056(a), follows the Uniform Vehicle Code pattern: "no person may stand within the roadway of a highway [...] for the purpose of soliciting a ride." Section 4(32) defines "roadway" as the part used for vehicular traffic, "exclusive of the shoulder." On ordinary roads, standing on the shoulder with your thumb out is lawful.

Limited-access highways are different. The Vermont Traffic Committee's "Regulations Governing Use of State Highways with Limited Access Facilities" apply to the whole right-of-way, including "all ramps and highways provided for approach thereto". These rules:

- prohibit "soliciting of rides on limited access highways, commonly known as 'hitchhiking'", and loitering there for hitchhiking; and
- prohibit pedestrians "except in areas specifically designated for that purpose."

The rule makes an exception for Agency of Transportation commuter parking areas inside a limited-access right-of-way, except on the Interstate system.

Penalties: a violation of § 1056 or of the Traffic Committee rule is a civil "traffic violation" under 23 V.S.A. § 2302, with a maximum penalty of $1,000. The usual waiver (ticket) amount was not confirmed.

## Laws

### 23 V.S.A. § 1056: Highway solicitations

**Source:** [Vermont General Assembly, 23 V.S.A. § 1056](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/23/013/01056)

> (a) Except as provided in subsection (b) of this section, no person may stand within the roadway of a highway as defined in subdivision 4(32) of this title for the purpose of soliciting a ride, contributions, employment, or business from the occupant of any vehicle.
> (b) Not-for-profit and municipal organizations may solicit contributions on the roadway of a highway, other than limited access highways, provided that approval is granted by the local legislative body [...]
>
> (Added 1971, No. 258 (Adj. Sess.), § 3, eff. March 1, 1973; amended 1973, No. 20, eff. March 7, 1973; 1989, No. 212 (Adj. Sess.).)

**What it means:** Don't stand in the travel lanes to hitchhike. The shoulder, sidewalk or a pull-off are fine on ordinary roads.

### 23 V.S.A. § 4(32): "Roadway"

**Source:** [Vermont General Assembly, 23 V.S.A. § 4](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/23/001/00004)

> (32) "Roadway" is that portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular traffic, exclusive of the shoulder.

**What it means:** The shoulder is expressly excluded, so § 1056(a) does not reach a hitchhiker standing on it.

### 23 V.S.A. § 1055(b): Pedestrians on roadways

**Source:** [Vermont General Assembly, 23 V.S.A. § 1055](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/23/013/01055)

> (a) [Repealed.]
> (b) Any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing the direction of possible oncoming traffic. (Added 1971, No. 258 (Adj. Sess.), § 3, eff. March 1, 1973; amended 2023, No. 165 (Adj. Sess.), § 45, eff. July 1, 2024.)

**What it means:** Walk on the left, facing traffic. Subsection (a) was repealed by 2023, No. 165 (Adj. Sess.), effective July 1, 2024. Its former text was not retrieved for this page.

### Code Vt. R. 14-053-004: Regulations Governing Use of State Highways with Limited Access Facilities

**Source:** [Code Vt. R. 14-053-004, via Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/vermont/14-004-Code-Vt-R-14-053-004-X) (unofficial mirror; effective November 28, 1971, last amended September 13, 1983 per the notes shown; statutory authority 23 V.S.A. § 1004)

> B. Penalties These regulations, together with alterations and amendments thereto, made under this Act shall have the force of law and violations thereof shall be penalized under Title 23, Vermont Statutes Annotated, Section 2205(c).
>
> C. Definition Limited access highways shall mean any portion of the right of way of a limited access facility as defined under Title 19, Vermont Statutes Annotated, Section 1862, and shall include but not be limited to the National System of Interstate and Defense Highways, and shall include all ramps and highways provided for approach thereto.

> Article 1. 8. Limitations on Use of Highway [...] b. Use of limited access highways and entry thereon by the following is prohibited at all times: (1) Vehicles drawn by animals. (2) Pedestrians except in areas specifically designated for that purpose. [...]

> Article 2. 9. Hitchhiking and Loitering Prohibited
> Soliciting of rides on limited access highways, commonly known as "hitchhiking," is prohibited. Loitering in or about limited access highways for the purposes of hitchhiking or participating in a "car pool," or as part of a demonstration or protest, or for any other purpose, is prohibited. Nothing contained in the above wording shall prohibit the use of commuter parking areas, constructed by the Agency of Transportation, within the right of way of a limited access highway, except for the National System of Interstate and Defense Highways.

**What it means:** On I-89, I-91, I-93 and other limited-access highways, you may not hitchhike or walk, and that includes the ramps. Stand on the local road before the ramp starts.

### 23 V.S.A. § 2302(a): Traffic violation defined

**Source:** [Vermont General Assembly, 23 V.S.A. § 2302](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/23/024/02302)

> (a) As used in this chapter, "traffic violation" means:
> (1) a violation of any provision of this title or rule adopted under this title for which no term of imprisonment is provided by law and for which a penalty of no more than $1,000.00 is provided; [...]

**What it means:** Hitchhiking violations are civil traffic tickets, not crimes.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may solicit a ride from the shoulder of an ordinary road, but not while standing in the roadway. Hitchhiking and walking are banned on limited-access highways, including interstates and their ramps.
