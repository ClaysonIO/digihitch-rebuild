# Wisconsin (US-WI)

**Status:** Restricted. You may not be *on a roadway* to solicit a ride (Wis. Stat. § 346.29(1)). Wisconsin's definition of "roadway" excludes the berm and shoulder. Pedestrians are barred from expressways and freeways where official signs prohibit them.

## Summary

Wisconsin bans soliciting a ride while you are on the roadway, meaning the travel lanes. The statute defines "roadway" as the area "between the regularly established curb lines" or the part "ordinarily used for vehicular travel, excluding the berm or shoulder." So thumbing from the shoulder or from behind the curb is not covered by § 346.29(1). The ban has a built-in exception for "public passenger vehicles," such as buses and taxis.

What applies to hitchhikers:

- **Stay off the travel lanes and behind the curb** when you ask for a ride.
- **Don't stand or loiter in the roadway** in a way that interferes with traffic (§ 346.29(2)).
- **Stay off expressways and freeways** where "no pedestrians" signs are posted (§ 346.16(2)(a)). Interstate on-ramps are typically signed, so wait at the start of the ramp.
- **Walk on the left side, facing traffic,** when there's no sidewalk (§ 346.28).

The penalties are small civil forfeitures. A first violation of § 346.29 costs $2 to $20. Walking on a signed freeway costs $30 to $300.

## Laws

### Wis. Stat. § 346.29: When standing or loitering in roadway or highway prohibited

**Source:** [LawServer, Wisconsin Statutes 346.29](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/wisconsin/wi-laws/wisconsin_laws_346-29) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024"). Subsection (1) is cross-checked against [Wisconsin Civil Jury Instruction 1250 (Wisconsin Court System, 2022, Release No. 53)](https://wilawlibrary.gov/jury/files/civil/1250.pdf), which quotes the same words and cites "Wis. Stat. § 346.29(1)." The official site, docs.legis.wisconsin.gov, could not be reached from this environment.

> (1) No person shall be on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the operator of any vehicle other than a public passenger vehicle.
>
> (2) No person shall stand or loiter on any roadway other than in a safety zone if such act interferes with the lawful movement of traffic.

**What it means:** You can't stand in the travel lanes to ask a private driver for a ride. The shoulder and the area behind the curb are not "roadway" (see § 340.01(54) below), so thumbing from there is not covered by this section. Hailing a bus or taxi from the roadway is expressly allowed.

### Wis. Stat. § 340.01(54): "Roadway" defined

**Source:** [LawServer, Wisconsin Statutes 340.01](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/wisconsin/wi-laws/wisconsin_laws_340-01) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (54) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway between the regularly established curb lines or that portion which is improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, excluding the berm or shoulder. In a divided highway the term "roadway" refers to each roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively.

**What it means:** The shoulder and berm are expressly excluded. In town, everything between the curbs counts as roadway, so stand on the sidewalk side of the curb.

### Wis. Stat. § 340.01(22): "Highway" defined

**Source:** [LawServer, Wisconsin Statutes 340.01](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/wisconsin/wi-laws/wisconsin_laws_340-01) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (22) "Highway" means all public ways and thoroughfares and bridges on the same. It includes the entire width between the boundary lines of every way open to the use of the public as a matter of right for the purposes of vehicular travel. [...]

**What it means:** "Highway" is the whole right-of-way. The freeway rule below uses "go upon any expressway or freeway," which reaches beyond just the lanes.

### Wis. Stat. § 346.16: Use of controlled-access highways, expressways and freeways

**Source:** [LawServer, Wisconsin Statutes 346.16](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/wisconsin/wi-laws/wisconsin_laws_346-16) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (2) (a) Except as provided in par. (b), no pedestrian or person riding a bicycle or other nonmotorized vehicle and no person operating a moped or motor bicycle may go upon any expressway or freeway when official signs have been erected prohibiting such person from using the expressway or freeway.
>
> [...]
>
> (b) A pedestrian or other person under par. (a) or (am) may go upon a portion of a hiking trail, cross-country ski trail, bridle trail or bicycle trail incorporated into the highway right-of-way and crossing the highway if the portion of the trail is constructed under s. 84.06 (11).

**What it means:** The pedestrian ban applies only where official signs prohibit pedestrians. Those signs are standard at interstate and freeway on-ramps, so in practice don't walk past them. Hitchhike from the surface street before the sign.

### Wis. Stat. § 346.28: Pedestrians on highways

**Source:** [LawServer, Wisconsin Statutes 346.28](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/wisconsin/wi-laws/wisconsin_laws_346-28) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (1) Any pedestrian traveling along and upon a highway other than upon a sidewalk shall travel on and along the left side of the highway and upon meeting a vehicle shall, if practicable, move to the extreme outer limit of the traveled portion of the highway.

**What it means:** When there's no sidewalk, walk on the left facing traffic, and step to the edge when cars come.

### Wis. Stat. § 346.30: Penalty for violating sections 346.23 to 346.29

**Source:** [LawServer, Wisconsin Statutes 346.30](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/wisconsin/wi-laws/wisconsin_laws_346-30) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (1) (a) Any pedestrian violating s. 346.23, 346.24 (2), 346.25, 346.28 or 346.29 and any personal delivery device operator violating s. 346.23 or 346.24 (2) may be required to forfeit not less than $2 nor more than $20 for the first offense and not less than $10 nor more than $50 for the 2nd or subsequent conviction within a year.

**What it means:** Soliciting a ride from the roadway is a civil forfeiture of $2 to $20 the first time, and $10 to $50 for a repeat within a year. It is not a crime.

### Wis. Stat. § 346.17: Penalty for violating sections 346.04 to 346.16

**Source:** [LawServer, Wisconsin Statutes 346.17](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/wisconsin/wi-laws/wisconsin_laws_346-17) (unofficial mirror, "Current as of: 2024")

> (2) Except as provided in sub. (6), any person violating ss. 346.05, 346.07 (2) or (3), 346.072, 346.08, 346.09, 346.10 (2) to (4), 346.11, 346.13 (2) or 346.14 to 346.16 may be required to forfeit not less than $30 nor more than $300.

**What it means:** Walking onto a signed freeway carries a much bigger forfeiture, $30 to $300, than hitchhiking from the roadway.

## Unverified / open questions

- The official Wisconsin Legislature site (docs.legis.wisconsin.gov) refused connections from this environment. All statute text comes from LawServer, an unofficial mirror marked current as of 2024. Section 346.29(1) was independently confirmed by the Wisconsin Court System's 2022 jury instruction. Amendments by 2025 to 2026 Wisconsin Acts were not checked against the official site.
- Wis. Stat. § 346.02(8)(a) says that chapter 346's rules on "highways, streets, alleys, roadways and sidewalks also apply to pedestrian ways," defined as "a walk designated for the use of pedestrian travel." Some online guides read this as making hitchhiking from sidewalks illegal. No primary source was found supporting that reading, and the hitchhiking ban itself is tied to the "roadway."
- Local ordinances (for example in Milwaukee or Madison) were not checked.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** You may ask for a ride from the shoulder or behind the curb; Wisconsin's "roadway" excludes the shoulder. Don't solicit from travel lanes, and don't enter expressways or freeways signed against pedestrians.

> **Wis. Stat. § 346.29(1)**
> (1) No person shall be on a roadway for the purpose of soliciting a ride from the operator of any vehicle other than a public passenger vehicle.

> **Wis. Stat. § 340.01(54)**
> (54) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway between the regularly established curb lines or that portion which is improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, excluding the berm or shoulder.

> **Wis. Stat. § 346.16(2)(a)**
> (2) (a) Except as provided in par. (b), no pedestrian or person riding a bicycle or other nonmotorized vehicle and no person operating a moped or motor bicycle may go upon any expressway or freeway when official signs have been erected prohibiting such person from using the expressway or freeway.
