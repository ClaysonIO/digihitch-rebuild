# Wyoming (US-WY)

**Status:** Legal. Wyoming repealed its ban on soliciting rides on highways in 2013. Ordinary pedestrian rules still apply. The Wyoming Department of Transportation (WYDOT) and local authorities may restrict pedestrians on controlled-access highways (interstates) where signs are posted.

## Summary

Until 2013, Wyo. Stat. § 31-5-606(a) said "No person shall be on a highway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, business or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle." That ban covered the whole highway, shoulders included. Senate File 29 (2013), titled simply "Hitchhiking", struck the words "a ride," along with the disabled-vehicle exception. The act took effect July 1, 2013. The section now covers only employment, business and contributions.

What still applies to hitchhikers:

- **Hitchhiking itself is not a statutory offense** in Wyoming.
- **Walk on the sidewalk** where one exists. Otherwise walk on the left side of the road or its shoulder, facing traffic.
- **Yield to vehicles** when you're on the roadway, and don't step into traffic to stop a car.
- **Interstates and other controlled-access highways:** WYDOT (by rule) and local authorities (by ordinance) may prohibit or regulate any class of traffic that is incompatible with safe traffic flow, and must post signs. Obey "pedestrians prohibited" signs.
- **Penalty** for breaking a pedestrian rule: a misdemeanor, with a fine of up to $200 for a first conviction.

## Laws

### Wyo. Stat. § 31-5-606: Soliciting on streets and highways

**Source:** [Wyoming Legislature, Wyoming Statutes Title 31 (PDF)](https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title31.pdf) (file generated May 8, 2026)

> (a) No person shall be on a highway for the purpose of soliciting employment, business or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle.
>
> (b) No person shall stand on or in proximity to a street or highway for the purpose of soliciting the watching or guarding of any vehicle while parked or about to be parked on a street or highway.

**What it means:** Soliciting a ride is no longer listed. Don't offer to work or ask for money from drivers, because that is still prohibited on any part of the highway. A sign asking for a ride to a destination is fine. A sign asking for cash is not.

### Wyo. Stat. § 31-5-605: Walking along roadways or highways

**Source:** [Wyoming Legislature, Wyoming Statutes Title 31 (PDF)](https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title31.pdf)

> (a) Where a sidewalk is provided and its use is practicable it is unlawful for any pedestrian to walk along and upon an adjacent roadway.
>
> (b) Where sidewalks are not provided any pedestrian walking along and upon a highway shall, when practicable, walk only on the left side of the roadway or its shoulder facing traffic which may approach from the opposite direction as far as practicable from the edge of the roadway.
>
> (c) Except as otherwise provided in this act, any pedestrian upon a roadway shall yield the right-of-way to all vehicles upon the roadway.

**What it means:** When walking between spots, use the left shoulder facing traffic. When you stop to hitch, pick a place where drivers can pull fully off the road.

### Wyo. Stat. § 31-5-102(a)(xl): "Roadway" defined

**Source:** [Wyoming Legislature, Wyoming Statutes Title 31 (PDF)](https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title31.pdf)

> (xl) "Roadway" means that portion of a highway improved, designed or ordinarily used for vehicular travel, exclusive of the sidewalk, berm or shoulder. In the event a highway includes two (2) or more separate roadways the term "roadway" as used herein shall refer to any such roadway separately but not to all such roadways collectively;

**What it means:** The shoulder is not part of the roadway. Standing on the shoulder doesn't count as being "upon a roadway" for § 31-5-605(c).

### Wyo. Stat. § 31-5-213: Restrictions on use of controlled-access highways

**Source:** [Wyoming Legislature, Wyoming Statutes Title 31 (PDF)](https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title31.pdf)

> (a) The department may by rule or regulation, and local authorities may by ordinance, with respect to any controlled-access highway under their respective jurisdictions prohibit or regulate the use of the highway by any class or kind of traffic which is found to be incompatible with the normal and safe movement of traffic.
>
> (b) The department or the local authority adopting any prohibitory regulation under subsection (a) of this section shall erect and maintain official traffic-control devices on the controlled-access highway on which the regulations are applicable and when so erected no person shall disobey the restrictions stated on the devices.

**What it means:** No statute bans pedestrians on Wyoming interstates. Any ban comes from a WYDOT rule or local ordinance and must be signed. Where an on-ramp is signed against pedestrians, hitch from before the sign.

### Wyo. Stat. § 31-5-1201: Penalty

**Source:** [Wyoming Legislature, Wyoming Statutes Title 31 (PDF)](https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title31.pdf)

> (a) It is a misdemeanor for any person to violate any of the provisions of this act or rules and regulations authorized under this act unless the violation is by this act or other law of this state declared to be a felony.
>
> (b) Every person convicted of a misdemeanor for a violation of any of the provisions of this act or rules and regulations authorized under this act for which another penalty is not provided shall:
> (i) For a first conviction be punished by a fine of not more than two hundred dollars ($200.00);
> (ii) For a second conviction of the same offense within one (1) year thereafter, be punished by a fine of not more than three hundred dollars ($300.00);
> (iii) For a third or subsequent conviction of the same offense within one (1) year after the first conviction, be punished by a fine of not more than five hundred dollars ($500.00) or by imprisonment for not more than six (6) months, or both.

**What it means:** Breaking a pedestrian rule, such as walking on a posted interstate, is a misdemeanor. The first offense carries a fine of up to $200.

## History

### 2013 SF0029 (Enrolled Act 58): hitchhiking ban repealed

**Source:** [Wyoming Legislature, 2013 SF0029 enrolled act](https://wyoleg.gov/2013/Enroll/SF0029.pdf) and [bill summary](https://www.wyoleg.gov/2013/Summaries/SF0029.htm)

The enrolled act amends § 31-5-606(a). The bill text shows the pre-2013 wording, with the deleted words struck through in the original. In plain text (strike-through formatting not reproduced) that wording reads:

> (a) No person shall be on a highway for the purpose of soliciting a ride, employment, business or contributions from the occupant of any vehicle. This subsection does not apply to persons attempting to obtain assistance for disabled vehicles.

The Legislative Service Office summary describes the bill this way: "This act repeals the state statutory prohibition on soliciting rides on highways (hitchhiking)." Effective date: July 1, 2013. Prime sponsor: Senator Christensen.

**What it means:** Older guides that list Wyoming as a no-hitchhiking state describe the law before July 1, 2013.

## Unverified / open questions

- I did not retrieve any WYDOT rule adopted under § 31-5-213 that closes interstates to pedestrians, so whether a statewide pedestrian ban exists by rule is unconfirmed. Watch for signs.

- National parks in Wyoming (Yellowstone, Grand Teton) are under federal regulations, which were not researched here.

## Pocket card

**What's allowed:** Hitchhiking is legal: no Wyoming statute bans soliciting a ride (the ban was repealed in 2013). Stay out of traffic, don't ask drivers for money or work, and obey any sign banning pedestrians from an interstate.
